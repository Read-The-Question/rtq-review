<div class='question question'>

$\pounds 50000$ in $\pounds 50$ notes weighs about $1.3 \ \text{Kg}$. How much, **in grams**, does one $\pounds 50$ note weigh?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of notes}      &= \dfrac {50000} {50} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {50 \times 1000} {50} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {\cancel{50} \times 1000} {\cancel{50}} \\\\
                            &= 1000 \\\\
\text{Weight of a note}     &= \dfrac {1.3} { 1000} \ \text{kg} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {1.3 \times 1000} { 1000} \ \text {g} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {1.3 \times 1 \cancel{0} \cancel{0} \cancel{0}} { 1 \cancel{0} \cancel{0} \cancel{0} } \\\\
                            &= 1.3 \ \text {g}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$1.3 \ \text {g}$

<div class='question question'>

David is $15$ stone $11$. Andrew is $275$ pounds. If there are $14$ pounds in $1$ stone what is the difference between their weights? Give your answer in pounds. 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: It is really important to use the right units in calculations and convert where required.


$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Difference}           &= 275 \text{ pounds} - 15 \text{ stone } 11 \text{ pounds} \\\\
                            &= 275 \text{ pounds} - 15 \times 14 \text{ pounds} - 11 \text{ pounds} \\\\
                            &= 275 \text{ pounds} - 15 \times 14 \text{ pounds} - 11 \text{ pounds} \\\\
                            &= 275 - 210 - 11 \\\\
                            &= 54 \text{ pounds}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
