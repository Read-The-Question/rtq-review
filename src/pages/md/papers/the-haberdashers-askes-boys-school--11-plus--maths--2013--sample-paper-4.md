<div class='question question'>

If a dozen eggs cost $\pounds 2.80$, how much do $18$ eggs cost?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cost of 1 egg}      &= \pounds \dfrac {2.8} {12} \\\\
\text{Cost of 18 egg}     &= 18 \times \dfrac {2.8} {12} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {18 \times 2.8} {12} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {18 \times 28} {12 \times 10} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {6 \times 3 \times 7 \times 4} {3 \times 4 \times 10} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {6 \times \cancel 3 \times 7 \times \cancel 4} {\cancel 3 \times \cancel 4 \times 10} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {6 \times 7} {10} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {42} {10} \\\\
                          &= \pounds 4.2
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Let the cost of $18$ eggs $= a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
12 \ \text{eggs} : \pounds 2.8                  &= 18 \ \text{eggs} : \pounds a \\\\
\dfrac {12} {2.8}                                &= \dfrac {18} {a} \\\\
12a                                             &= 18 \times 2.8 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {18 \times 2.8} {12} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {18 \times 28} {12 \times 10} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {6 \times 3 \times 2 \times 14} {6 \times 2 \times 10} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 6 \times 3 \times \cancel 2 \times 14} {\cancel 6 \times \cancel 2 \times 10} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {3 \times 14} {10} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {42} {10} \\\\
a                                               &= \pounds 4.2
\end{aligned}
$

Cost of $18$ eggs $= \pounds 4.2$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{eggs}                 &:& \text{cost} \\\\
= & 12                          &:& \pounds 2.80 \\\\
= & 1                           &:& \dfrac {2.8} {12} \\\\
= & 1 \times 18                 &:& \dfrac {2.8 \times 18} {12} \\\\
= & 18                          &:& \dfrac {28 \times 18} {12 \times 10} \\\\
= & 18                          &:& \dfrac {2 \times 14 \times 3 \times 6} {2 \times 6 \times 10} \\\\
= & 18                          &:& \dfrac {\cancel 2 \times 14 \times 3 \times \cancel 6} {\cancel 2 \times \cancel 6 \times 10} \\\\
= & 18                          &:& \dfrac {14 \times 3} {10} \\\\
= & 18                          &:& \dfrac {42} {10} \\\\
= & 18                          &:& \pounds 4.2
\end{array}
$

Cost of $18$ eggs $= \pounds 4.2$

</div>
</div>

An orange costs twice as much as an apple. Which one of the following does not cost the same as the other four?

- Two oranges and three apples

- Seven apples

- One orange and five apples

- Three oranges and one apple

- Four oranges

</div>

<div class='working'>

Let the cost of apple $= a$

Cost of orange $= 2a$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cost of Two oranges and three apples}     &= 2 \times 2a + 3 \times a \\\\
                                                &= 4a + 3a \\\\
                                                &= 7a \\\\
\text{Cost of seven apples}                     &= 7 \times a \\\\
                                                &= 7a \\\\
\text{Cost of One orange and five apples}       &= 2 \times a + 5 \times a \\\\
                                                &= 2a + 5a \\\\
                                                &= 7a \\\\
\text{Cost of Three oranges and one apple}      &= 3 \times 2a + a \\\\ 
                                                &= 6a + a \\\\ 
                                                &= 7a \\\\ 
\text{Cost of four oranges}                     &= 4 \times 2a \\\\
                                                &= 8a
\end{aligned}
$

The price of four oranges is not the same as the price of the other four.

</div>

<p>E</p>

Four oranges

The area of land which consists of forest but no mountain is $32 \ \text{km}^2$ and the area which is both forest and 
mountain is $6 \ \text{km}^2$. The area of water is $67 \ \text{km2}$. 

Work out the area of land which consists of mountain but no forest.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Area of rectangle} = \text{length} \times \text{width}$


As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Area}                 &= \text {Land} - \text {Water} - \text {Forest} \\\\
                             &= 160 - 67 - (32 + 6) \\\\
                             &= 160 - 67 - 38 \\\\
                             &= 55 \ \text {km} ^ 2
\end {aligned}
$

Area of land which consists of mountain but no forest $= 55 \ \text {km}^2$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$55 \ \text {km} ^ 2$

</div>

A total of $121$ people live on the island. If $81$ people live on land which is mountainous and $48$ people live on land which is forested, how many live in the region which is both mountainous and forested?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

As per the question,

ABBR: $\text{Area of rectangle} = \text{length} \times \text{width}$


As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Number of people}     &= \text {People in mountainous land} + \text {People in forested land} - \text {Total people} \\\\
                             &= 81 + 48 - 121 \\\\
                             &= 8
\end {aligned}
$

$8$ people live in the region which is both mountainous and forested.

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$8$
