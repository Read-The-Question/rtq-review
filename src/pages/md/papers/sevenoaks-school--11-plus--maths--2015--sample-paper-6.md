<div class='question question'>

Gavin spends $\pounds 4.90$ on two items in a shop. One item cost $40$p more than the other.  How much did each item cost?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: It is really important to use the right units in calculations and convert where required.


$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Let the cost of First item}  &= a \\\\
\text{The cost of second item}     &= a + 40 \text{p} \\\\ 
                                   &= a + \pounds \dfrac{40}{100} \\\\
                                   &= a + 0.4
\end{aligned}
$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
a + a + 0.40                                      &= 4.90  \\\\
2a + 0.40                                         &= 4.90 \\\\
2a                                                &= 4.90 - 0.40 \\\\
2a                                                &= 4.50 \\\\
a                                                 &= \dfrac {4.50} {2} \\\\
a                                                 &= \dfrac {2.25 \times 2} {2} \\\\
a                                                 &= \dfrac {2.25 \times \cancel{2}} {\cancel{2}} \\\\
a                                                 &= \pounds 2.25
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cost of the first item}      &= \pounds 2.25 \\\\
\text{Cost of second item }        &= a + 0.40 \\\\
                                   &= 2.25 + 0.40 \\\\
                                   &= 2.65 \\\\
                                   &= \pounds 2.65
\end{aligned}
$


</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$\pounds 2.65$ and $\pounds 2.25$

<div class='question question'>

Seesaws cost $\pounds 200$ each in January 2010. In January 2011 they had gone up by $45 \%$. Calculate how much a seesaw cost in January 2011.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

If the price has gone up by $45 \%$, then the new price will be $100 + 45 = 145 \%$ of the original price. Using that, we get

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Price of seesaw in} \ 2011         &= 145 \% \times 200 \\\\
                                          &= \dfrac {145}{100} \times 200 \\\\
                                          &= \dfrac {145 \times 200}{100}  \\\\
                                          &= \dfrac {145 \times 2 \cancel{0} \cancel{0}}{1 \cancel{0} \cancel{0}}  \\\\
                                          &= 145 \times 2  \\\\
                                          &= \pounds 290
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$\pounds 290$

</div>

<div class='question question'>

*Purple Fire* paint is made by mixing red and blue paint in the ratio $3:1$. If I want to make $1 \ \text{litre}$ of *Purple Fire* paint, how much red paint do I need?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the volume of red paint (in ml) $= a$

Volume of blue paint $= 1000 - a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
3 \ \text{ml} : 1 \ \text{ml}                   &= a \ \text{ml} : (1000 - a) \ \text{ml} \\\\
\dfrac {3} {1}                                  &= \dfrac {a} {1000 - a} \\\\
3 \times (1000 - a)                             &= a \\\\
3000 - 3a                                       &= a \\\\
3000                                            &= a + 3a \\\\
a + 3a                                          &= 3000 \\\\
4a                                              &= 3000 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {3000} {4} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {4 \times 750} {4} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times 750} {\cancel 4} \\\\
a                                               &= 750 \ \text{ml}
\end{aligned}
$

Red paint needed $= 750 \ \text{ml}$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using whole to part ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{purple}                               &:& \text{red} \\\\
= & (1 + 3) \ \text{ml}                         &:& 3 \ \text{ml} \\\\
= & 4                                           &:& 3 \\\\
= & 4 \times 250                                &:& 3 \times 250 \\\\
= & 1000 \ \text{ml}                            &:& 750 \ \text{ml} \\\\
= & 1 \ \text{litre}                            &:& 750 \ \text{ml}
\end{array}
$

Red paint needed $= 750 \ \text{ml}$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$750 \ \text{ml}$ 

</div>

How many letters of the word **MATHEMATICS** do not have any lines of symmetry?

M, A,  T,  H, and I : If you fold vertically, along the middle of  these alphabets, both halves would match exactly. 

E C : If you fold horizontally along the middle line of these alphabets, both halves would match exactly. 

There is only $1$ letter $S$ in **MATHEMATICS** that do not have any lines of symmetry.

<div class='question question'>

A solid square based pyramid has all of its corners cut off, as shown. How many edges does the resulting shape have?

![sevenoaks-school--11-plus--maths--2015--sample-paper-6/section-1-question-11-00.png](/assets/sevenoaks-school--11-plus--maths--2015--sample-paper-6/section-1-question-11-00.png "sevenoaks-school--11-plus--maths--2015--sample-paper-6/section-1-question-11-00.png")

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>


A pyramid with a square base has $8$ edges. $4$ edges to the square and $4$ where the triangles meet each other. 

A triangle has $3$ edges.

No. of corners cut off at base $= 4$ corners

Every corner that is cut off at base results into a new triangle edges.

Total edges at base 

= Old edges + ($4$ corners $\times 3$ triangle edges)

$
\begin{aligned}
&= 4 + (4 \times 3) \\
&= 4 + 12
\end{aligned}
$

Now let's look at the top. When the tip is cut it results into a square top, as in the picture.

Total edges at top 

= Old edges + ($1$ corner $\times 4$ edges of the new square)

$
\begin{aligned}
&= 4 + (1 \times 4) \\
&= 4 + 4 \\
&= 8
\end{aligned}
$


Total edges = new edges at base + new edges at tip 

$
\begin{aligned}
&= 16 + 8
&= 24
\end{aligned}
$

The resulting shape will result in $24$ edges.

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$24$

</div>
</div>
