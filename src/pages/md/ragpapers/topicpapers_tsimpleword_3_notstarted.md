<div class='question question'>

Tom uses $750 \ \text{g}$ of sugar from a $2.5 \ \text{kg}$ packet. How many kilograms of sugar are left? 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: It is really important to use the right units in calculations and convert where required.

The quantity of sugar is given in grams and the packet is given in kilograms.

$
\begin{aligned}
1 \text {kg}  &= 1000 \text {g} \\\\
750 \text {g} &= \dfrac {750} {1000} \text {kg} \\\\
&= 0.75 \text {kg}
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
& 2.5 - 0.75 \\\\
&= 1.75
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$1.75 \text{kg}$

An adult weighs $\bold{80\ \text{kg}}$.

What is the mass of this water?

<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
&= 60 \% \ \text{of total mass} \\\\
&= 60 \% \times 80 \\\\
&= \dfrac {60} {100} \times 80 \\\\
&= \dfrac {60 \times 80} {100} \\\\
&= \dfrac {6 \cancel {0} \times 8 \cancel {0}} {1 \cancel {0} \cancel {0}} \\\\
&= 6 \times 8 \\\\
&= 48 \text { kg}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>

<div class='answer'>

$48 \text { kg}$

</div>
