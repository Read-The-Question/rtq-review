                                &= \sqrt {9 + 0} \\\\
                                &= \sqrt {9} \\\\
                                &= 3 \\\\
\text{Height of the triangle}   &= 2 - 1 \\\\
                                &= 1 \\\\
\text{Area of the square}       &= \dfrac {1} {2} \times \text{(Base of the triangle)} \times \text{(Height of the triangle)} \\\\
                                &= \dfrac {1} {2} \times 3 \times 1 \\\\
                                &= \dfrac {3} {2} \\\\
                                &= 1.5
\end{aligned}

rectangles of equal size.

The middle rectangle is removed and replaced on the side of the 
original square to form an octagon as shown.

How many times bigger is the perimeter of the octagon than 
the perimeter of the original square?

![bancrofts-school--11-plus--maths--2021--sample-paper-7/section-1-question-35-subquestion-2-00.png](/assets/bancrofts-school--11-plus--maths--2021--sample-paper-7/section-1-question-35-subquestion-2-00.png "bancrofts-school--11-plus--maths--2021--sample-paper-7/section-1-question-35-subquestion-2-00.png")

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Perimeter of square}      &= 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 \\\\
                                &= 12 \ \text{cm} \\\\

                                &= 20 \ \text{cm} \\\\
\text{Difference}               &= 20 - 12 \\\\
                                &= 8 \ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>

$8 \ \text{cm}$

                                &= \sqrt {(-5)) ^ 2 + 0 ^ 2} \\\\
                                &= \sqrt {25 + 0} \\\\
                                &= \sqrt {25} \\\\
                                &= 5 \ \text{cm} \\\\
\text{Area of rectangle}        &= \text{Length of rectangle} \times \text{Breadth of rectangle} \\\\
                                &= 4 \times 5 \\\\
                                &= 20 \ {\text{cm}} ^ 2
\end{aligned}
