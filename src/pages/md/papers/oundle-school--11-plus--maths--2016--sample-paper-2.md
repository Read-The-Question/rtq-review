<div class='question question'>

A book usually costs $\pounds 18.60$ but it is reduced by $15\%$.How much does it cost now? 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

If the price is reduced by $15 \%$, then the sale price will be $100 - 15 = 85 \%$ of the original price. Using that we get,

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {New price}         &= 85 \% \text { of } 18.60 \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {85} {100} \times 18.6 \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {85 \times 18.6} {100} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {1581} {100} \\\\
                          &= \pounds 15.81
\end {aligned}
$

</div>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{New price}            &= \text {Original price} - \text {Discount} \\\\
                            &= 18.60 - 15 \% \text{ of } 18.60  \\\\
                            &= 18.60 - \dfrac {15} {100} \times 18.60 \\\\
                            &= 18.60 - \dfrac {15 \times 18.60} {100} \\\\
                            &= 18.60 - \dfrac {279} {100} \\\\
                            &= 18.60 - 2.79 \\\\
                            &= \pounds 15.81
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$\pounds 15.81$

</div>
</div>

$a$ and $b$ are two *different* positive whole numbers which make the following statement true

$3a + 2b = 30$ 

Find two possible pairs of numbers which make the statement true.

</div>

<div class='working'>

For $a =10$,

$
\begin{aligned}
3a + 2b                             &= 30 \\\\
3 \times 10 + 2b                    &= 30 \\\\
30 + 2b                             &= 30 \\\\
2b                                  &= 30 - 30 \\\\
2b                                  &= 0 \\\\
b                                   &= 0
\end{aligned}
$

For $a= 2$,

$
\begin{aligned}
3a + 2b                             &= 30 \\\\
3 \times 2 + 2b                     &= 30 \\\\
2b                                  &= 30 - 6 \\\\
2b                                  &= 24 \\\\
b                                   &= \dfrac {24} {2} \\\\
b                                   &= \dfrac {12 \times \cancel {2}} {\cancel {2}} \\\\
b                                   &= 12
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$a = 10,b = 0 \\
a = 2,b = 12$

Two workers are paid a total of $\pounds 506$ by their employer. If one is paid $20\%$ more than the other, how much do they each earn? 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>


Let the wage of first labourer $= a$

Wage of second labourer $= a + 20 \% \times a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
a + a + 20 \% \times a                                                      &= 506 \\\\
2a + \dfrac{20}{100} \times a                                               &= 506 \\\\
2a + \dfrac{20 \times a}{100}                                               &= 506 \\\\
\dfrac{2a \times 100}{1 \times 100} + \dfrac{20a \times 1}{100 \times 1}    &= 506 \\\\
\dfrac {2a \times 100 + 20a} {100}                                          &= 506 \\\\
\dfrac{200a + 20a}{100}                                                     &= 506 \\\\
\dfrac{220a}{100}                                                           &= 506 \\\\
\dfrac{220a}{100}                                                           &= 506 \\\\
220a                                                                        &= 506 \times 100 \\\\
a                                                                           &= \dfrac{506 \times 100}{220} \\\\
a                                                                           &= \dfrac{506 \times 10 \times \cancel{10}}{22 \times \cancel{10}} \\\\
a                                                                           &= \dfrac{506 \times 5 \times \cancel{2}}{11 \times \cancel{2}} \\\\
a                                                                           &= \dfrac{46 \times \cancel{11} \times 5}{\cancel{11}} \\\\
a                                                                           &= 46 \times 5 \\\\
a                                                                           &= \pounds 230 \\\\
\text{Wage of first worker}                                                 &= \pounds 230 \\\\
\text{Wage of second worker}                                                &= 506 - 230 \\\\
                                                                            &= \pounds 276
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$\pounds 230, \ \pounds 276$
