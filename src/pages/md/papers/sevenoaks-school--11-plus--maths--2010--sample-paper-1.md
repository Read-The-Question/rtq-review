<div class='question question'>

A sunflower is one hundred and fifty centimetres tall.

How tall will it be if its height increases by ten per cent?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

If the height is increased by $10\%$ then new height will be $100 + 10 = 110\%$ of the orginal height.

Using this we get,

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{New height}  &= 110 \% \text { of} \ 150 \\\\
                   &= \dfrac {110} {100} \times 150 \\\\
                   &= \dfrac {110 \times 150} {100} \\\\
                   &= \dfrac {11 \cancel {0} \times 15 \cancel {0}} {1 \cancel {0} \cancel {0}} \\\\
                   &= 11 \times 15 \\\\
                   &= 165 \ \text {cm}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$165 \ \text{cm}$

</div>
</div>

In a box of pens, one half are black, one sixth are red and the rest are blue.  What fraction of the pens is blue?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the total pens $= a$

Black pens $= \dfrac{a}{2}$

Red pens $= \dfrac{a}{6}$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Blue pens}                 &= a - \dfrac {a} {2} - \dfrac {a} {6}    \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {6a - 3a - a} {6}             \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {2a} {6}             \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {\cancel{2}a} {3 \times \cancel {2}} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {a} {3}
\end{aligned}
$

Fraction of blue pens $= \dfrac{1}{3}$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$\dfrac {1} {3}$

<div class='question question'>

Bella makes purple paint by mixing blue paint and red paint in the ratio of $4:3$. How much blue paint is needed to make $21$ litres of purple paint?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the volume of blue paint $= b$

Volume of red paint $= 21 - b$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
b \ \text{litres} : (21 - b) \ \text{litres}       &= 4 \ \text{litres} : 3 \ \text{litres} \\\\
\dfrac {b} {21 - b}                                &= \dfrac {4} {3} \\\\
3 \times b                                         &= 4 \times (21 - b) \\\\
3b                                                 &= 84 - 4b \\\\
3b + 4b                                            &= 84 \\\\
7b                                                 &= 84 \\\\
b                                                  &= \dfrac {84} {7} \\\\
b                                                  &= \dfrac {7 \times 12} {7} \\\\
b                                                  &= \dfrac {\cancel 7 \times 12} {\cancel 7} \\\\
b                                                  &= 12 \ \text{litres}
\end{aligned}
$

Blue paint needed $= 12 \ \text{litres}$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using whole to part ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{purple}                                &:& \text{blue} \\\\
= & (4 + 3) \ \text{litres}                      &:& 4 \ \text{litres} \\\\
= & 7                                            &:& 4 \\\\
= & 7 \times 3                                   &:& 4 \times 3 \\\\
= & 21 \ \text{litres}                           &:& 12 \ \text{litres}
\end{array}
$

Blue paint needed $= 12 \ \text{litres}$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$12 \ \text{litres}$

</div>

<div class='question question'>

Six cubes each have a **surface area** of **$24\ \text{cm}^2$**. They are joined together to make a cuboid.

![sevenoaks-school--11-plus--maths--2010--sample-paper-1/section-1-question-14-00.png](/assets/sevenoaks-school--11-plus--maths--2010--sample-paper-1/section-1-question-14-00.png "sevenoaks-school--11-plus--maths--2010--sample-paper-1/section-1-question-14-00.png")

What could the **surface area** of this cuboid be? 

There are two different answers. Write them both.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Surface area of cube} = 6 \times \text{edge} \times \text{edge}$


$
\begin{aligned}
6a^2   &= 24 \\\\
a^2    &= \dfrac {24} {6} \\\\
a^2    &= \dfrac {6 \times 4} {6} \\\\
a^2    &= \dfrac {\cancel 6 \times 4} {\cancel 6} \\\\
a^2    &= 4 \\\\
a      &= \sqrt {4} \\\\
a      &= 2 \ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$

Possiblity $1 =$ $2$ cubes piled upon one another 

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Surface Area} &= 2 \times (3a \times 2a) + 2 \times 3a \times a + 2 \times a \times a \\\\
                    &= 12a^2 + 6a^2 + 2a^2 \\\\
                    &= 20a^2 \\\\
                    &= 20 \times 2 \times 2 \\\\
                    &= 80 \ \text{cm}^2 
\end{aligned}
$


Possiblity $2 =$ $1$ cubes piled upon one another 

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Surface Area} &= 4 \times (6a \times a) + 2 \times a \times a \\\\
                    &= 24a^2 + 2a^2 \\\\
                    &= 26a^2 \\\\
                    &= 26 \times 2 \times 2 \\\\
                    &= 104 \ \text{cm}^2 
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$80 \ \text{cm}^2 ,104 \ \text{cm}^2 $
