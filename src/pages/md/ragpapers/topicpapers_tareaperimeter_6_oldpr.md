![streatham-and-clapham-high-school--11-plus--maths--2019--sample-paper-2/section-1-question-9-00.png](/assets/streatham-and-clapham-high-school--11-plus--maths--2019--sample-paper-2/section-1-question-9-00.png "streatham-and-clapham-high-school--11-plus--maths--2019--sample-paper-2/section-1-question-9-00.png")

Area = $49 \ \text{cm}^{2}$

- $49 \ \text{cm}$
- $98 \ \text{cm}$
- $28 \ \text{cm}$
- $20 \ \text{cm}$
- $14 \ \text{cm}$ 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Area of square} = \text{side} \times \text{side}$


Let the side of the square (in cm) $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
a \times a              &= 49 \\\\
a^2                     &= 49 \\\\
a                       &= \sqrt {49} \\\\
a                       &= 7 \ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$

Let's find the perimeter of the square.

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Perimeter}       &= 4 \times a \\\\
                        &= 4 \times 7 \\\\
                        &= 28 \ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>

A roll of wallpaper is $10 \ \text{m}$ long and $0.6 \ \text{m}$ wide. How many rolls of 
wallpaper are needed to cover a wall $3 \ \text{m}$ high and $8 \ \text{m}$ wide?

- $5$
- $4$
- $7$
- $10$
- $11$ 

</div>

<div class='working'>

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of rolls}  &= \dfrac {\text{Area of the Wall}} {\text{Area of a wallpaper roll}} \\\\
                        &= \dfrac {8 \times 3} {10 \times 0.6} \\\\
                        &= \dfrac {24} {6} \\\\
                        &= \dfrac {6 \times 4} {6} \\\\
                        &= \dfrac {\cancel {6} \times 4} {\cancel {6}} \\\\
                        &= 4
\end{aligned}
$

<div class='question question'>

A square piece of card has perimeter $28 \ \text{cm}$. 
Ann cuts the card into two rectangles. 
The perimeter of one of the rectangles is $16 \ \text{cm}$. 
What is the  perimeter of the other rectangle?

- $26$
- $16$
- $14$
- $24$
- $20$ 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Perimeter of square} = 4 \times \text{side}$


ABBR: $\text{Perimeter of rectangle} = 2 \times (\text{length} + \text{width})$


Let the side of the square $= a$

Let the width of the first rectangle $= b$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
4 \times a              &= 28 \\\\
a                       &= \dfrac {28} {4} \\\\ 
a                       &= \dfrac {4 \times 7} {4} \\\\ 
a                       &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times 7} {\cancel 4} \\\\
a                       &= 7  \ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Perimeter of first rectangle} &= 16 \\\\
2 \times (7 + b)                    &= 16 \\\\
7 + b                               &= \dfrac {16} {2} \\\\ 
7 + b                               &= \dfrac {2 \times 8} {2} \\\\ 
7 + b                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 2 \times 8} {\cancel 2} \\\\
7 + b                               &= 8 \\\\
b                                   &= 8 - 7 \\\\
b                                   &= 1 \ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Perimeter of second rectangle} &=  2 \times (7 + (7-1)) \\\\
                                     &=  2 \times (7 + 6) \\\\
                                     &=  2 \times 13 \\\\
                                     &=  26 \ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='option'>
<p>A</p>
</div>
<div class='answer'>

$26 \ \text{cm}$

</div>
</div>

- $9.5 \ \text{cm}$

- $7.5 \ \text{cm}$
- $7.25 \ \text{cm}$
- $9.25 \ \text{cm}$

$
\begin{aligned}

b                             &= \dfrac {4 \times 9.5} {4} \\\\
b                             &= \dfrac {\cancel {4} \times 9.5} {\cancel {4}} \\\\
b                             &= 9.5 \ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$

Length of the base of the parallelogram $= 9.5 \ \text{cm}$.
