<div class='question subquestion'>

Isaac wants to make $36$ cupcakes. How much flour should he use?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the amount of flour used $= a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
12 \ \text{cupcakes} : 180 \ \text{grams}       &= 36 \ \text{cupcakes} : a \ \text{grams} \\\\
\dfrac{12} {180}                                &= \dfrac {36} {a} \\\\
12 \times a                                     &= 36 \times 180 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {36 \times 180} {12} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {3 \times 12 \times 180} {12} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {3 \times \cancel {12} \times 180} {\cancel {12}} \\\\
a                                               &= 3 \times 180 \\\\
a                                               &= 540 \ \text{g}
\end{aligned}
$

Flour used $= 540 \ \text{g}$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using ratios, 

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{cupcakes}                 &:&     \text{flour} \\\\
= & 12                              &:&     180 \ \text{g} \\\\
= & 12  \times 3                    &:&     180 \times 3 \\\\
= & 36                              &:&     540 \ \text{g}                   
\end{array}
$

Flour used $= 540 \ \text{g}$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$540 \ \text{g}$

Shortcrust pastry is made using flour and fat in the ratio $2 : 1$.

How many grams of flour are needed to make $900 \ \text{grams}$ of shortcrust pastry?  

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Flour needed}              &= \dfrac {2} {3} \times 900 \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {2 \times 900} {3} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {2 \times 300 \times 3} {3} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {2 \times 300 \times \cancel{3}} {\cancel{3}} \\\\
                                 &= 2 \times 300  \\\\
                                 &= 600 \ \text{g}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Let the quantity of flour needed $= a$

Quantity of fat needed $= 900 - a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
1 \ \text{grams} : 2 \ \text{grams}                     &= (900 - a) \ \text{grams} : a \ \text{grams} \\\\
\dfrac{1} {2}                                   &= \dfrac {900 - a} {a} \\\\
a                                               &= (900 - a) \times 2 \\\\
a                                               &= 1800 - 2a \\\\
a + 2a                                          &= 1800 \\\\
3a                                              &= 1800 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {1800} {3} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {3 \times 600} {3} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 3 \times 600} {\cancel 3} \\\\
a                                               &= 600 \ \text{g}
\end{aligned}
$

Flour $= 600 \ \text{g}$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using whole to part ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{pastry}                       &:& \text{flour} \\\\
= & 2 + 1                               &:& 2 \\\\
= & 3                                   &:& 2 \\\\
= & 3 \times 300                        &:& 2 \times 300 \\\\
= & 900 \ \text{g}                      &:& 600 \ \text{g}
\end{array}
$

Flour $= 600 \ \text{g}$

<div class='question subquestion'>

What mass of plain flour does Helen need to use in order to make $18$ waffles?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the mass of flour needed $= a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
12 \ \text{waffles} : 250 \ \text{grams}            &= 18 \ \text{waffles} : a \ \text{grams} \\\\
\dfrac{12} {250}                                &= \dfrac {18} {a} \\\\
12 \times a                                     &= 18 \times 250 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {18 \times 250} {12} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {3 \times 6 \times 2 \times 125} {2 \times 6} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {3 \times \cancel 6 \times \cancel 2 \times 125} {\cancel 2 \times \cancel 6} \\\\
a                                               &= 3 \times 125 \\\\
a                                               &= 375 \ \text{g}
\end{aligned}
$

Flour needed $= 375 \ \text{g}$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{waffles}                          &:& \text{plain flour} \\\\
= & 12                                      &:& 250 \ \text{g} \\\\
= & 1                                       &:& \dfrac {250} {12} \\\\
= & 1 \times 18                             &:& \dfrac {250 \times 18} {12} \\\\
= & 18                                      &:& \dfrac {2 \times 125 \times 6 \times 3} {6 \times 2} \\\\
= & 18                                      &:& \dfrac {\cancel 2 \times 125 \times \cancel 6 \times 3} {\cancel 6 \times \cancel 2} \\\\
= & 18                                      &:& 125 \times 3 \\\\
= & 18                                      &:& 375 \ \text{g}
\end{array}
$

Flour needed $= 375 \ \text{g}$
