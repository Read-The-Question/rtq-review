Jamie got seven questions wrong in a test out of $25$ questions. What percentage of the questions did he get wrong?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Percentage of wrong questions} &= \dfrac {7} {25} \times 100 \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {7 \times 100} {25} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {7 \times 5 \times 5 \times 4} {25} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {7 \times \cancel {5}\times \cancel {5} \times 4} {\cancel {5} \times \cancel{5}} \\\\
                                      &= 7 \times 4 \\\\
                                      &= 28 \%
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$28 \%$

</div>
</div>

A pizza is divided in $12$ equal slices. Matthew eats $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of 
the pizza and Katie eats another $\dfrac{1}{6}$.

How many slices are left?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of slices left}       &= 12 - \dfrac {1} {4} \ \text{of} \ 12 - \dfrac {1} {6} \ \text{of} \ 12 \\\\
                                   &= 12 - \dfrac {1} {4} \times 12 - \dfrac {1} {6} \times 12 \\\\
                                   &= 12 - \dfrac {12} {4} - \dfrac {12} {6} \\\\
                                   &= 12 - \dfrac {4 \times 3} {4} - \dfrac {6 \times 2} {6} \\\\
                                   &= 12 - \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times 3} {\cancel 4} - \dfrac {\cancel 6 \times 2} {\cancel 6} \\\\
                                   &= 12 - 3 - 2 \\\\
                                   &= 12 - 5 \\\\
                                   &= 7
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$7$

</div>

Some fairy cakes are delivered for Janey's party.

Janey can use big plates or small plates to put the cakes on.

If she uses big plates she can put seven cakes on each plate with five left over.

If she uses small plates she can put five cakes on each plate with three left over.

![the-london-independent-girls-schools-consortium--11-plus--maths--2011--group-2/section-1-question-32-00.png](/assets/the-london-independent-girls-schools-consortium--11-plus--maths--2011--group-2/section-1-question-32-00.png "the-london-independent-girls-schools-consortium--11-plus--maths--2011--group-2/section-1-question-32-00.png")

What is the smallest number of cakes that were delivered for Janey's party?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number of big plates $= a$

Let the number of small plates $= b$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
5b + 3         &= 7a + 5 \\\\
5b             &= 7a + 5 - 3 \\\\
5b             &= 7a + 2 \\\\
5b - 7a        &= 2
\end{aligned}
$

We can easily infer the value using trial and error. Lets find out the value of a and b that satisfies the above equation.

| $a$  | $b$ |                   $5b - 7a$                  |
|:----:|:---:|:--------------------------------------------:|
| $0$  | $1$ |    $5 \times 1 - 7 \times 0 = 5 - 0 = 5$     |
| $1$  | $2$ |    $5 \times 2 - 7 \times 1 = 10 - 7 = 3$    |
| $2$  | $3$ |    $5 \times 3 - 7 \times 2 = 15 - 14 = 1$   |
| $2$  | $4$ |    $5 \times 4 - 7 \times 2 = 20 - 14 = 6$   |
| $3$  | $5$ |    $5 \times 5 - 7 \times 3 = 25 - 21 = 4$   |
| $4$  | $6$ |    $5 \times 6 - 7 \times 4 = 30 - 28 = 2$   |

A quick check shows that $a = 4$ and $b = 6$ meets all the criteria above.

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Smallest number of cakes delivered}         &= 7 \times a + 5 \\\\
                                                  &= 7 \times 4 + 5 \\\\
                                                  &= 28 + 5 \\\\
                                                  &= 33
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$33$
