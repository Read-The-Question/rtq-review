<div class='question question'>

Josie and Holly share $\pounds 42$ in between them. Josie 
has three times as much as Holly. How much has Holly?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the share of Holly $= a$

Share of Josie $= 3a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
a + 3a &= 42 \\\\
4a     &= 42 \\\\
a      &= \dfrac{42}{4} \\\\
a      &= \dfrac{2 \times 21}{2 \times 2} \\\\
a      &= \dfrac{\cancel 2 \times 21}{\cancel 2 \times 2} \\\\
a      &= \dfrac{21}{2} \\\\
a      &= 10.5
\end{aligned}
$

Holly has $=10.5$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$10.5$

</div>

<div class='question question'>

I bought a packet of fruit gums and ate $2$ of them, secretly, in my Music lesson.

At break, I shared one third of the remaining fruit gums with 
my friends. I then finished the fruit gums, eating an equal amount in each of 
the next four lessons.

What is the smallest number of fruit gums there could have been in the packet?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number of gums $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
&= ((a - 2) \times (1 - \dfrac{1}{3})) \div 4 \\\\
&= ((a - 2) \times (\dfrac{1 \times 3}{1\times 3} - \dfrac{1}{3}))  \div 4 \\\\
&= ((a - 2) \times (\dfrac{3 - 1}{3})) \div 4 \\\\
&= \dfrac{2(a - 2)}{3} \times \dfrac{1}{4} \\\\
&= \dfrac{2(a - 2)}{3 \times 4} \\\\
&= \dfrac{2(a - 2)}{3 \times 2 \times 2} \\\\
&= \dfrac{\cancel 2 \times (a - 2)}{3 \times \cancel 2 \times 2} \\\\
&= \dfrac{a-2}{6}
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
(a - 2) \times \dfrac{1}{6}   &= 1 \\\\
(a - 2)                       &= 1 \times 6 \\\\
a                             &= 6 + 2 \\\\
a                             &= 8
\end{aligned}
