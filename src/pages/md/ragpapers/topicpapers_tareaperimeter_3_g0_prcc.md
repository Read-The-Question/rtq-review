<div class='question question'>

A piece of wire $30 \ \text{cm}$ long is bent into the shape of a rectangle. 
If the rectangle is twice as long as it is wide, what is its area?  

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Perimeter of rectangle} = 2 \times (\text{length} + \text{width})$


ABBR: $\text{Area of rectangle} = \text{length} \times \text{width}$


Let the width of the rectangle $= a$

Length of the rectangle $= 2a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
2 \times (2a + a)                         &= 30 \\\\
2 \times 3a                               &= 30 \\\\
6a                                        &= 30 \\\\
a                                         &= \dfrac {30} {6} \\\\
a                                         &= \dfrac {5 \times 6} {6} \\\\
a                                         &= \dfrac {5 \times \cancel {6}} {\cancel {6}} \\\\
a                                         &= 5 \ \text {cm}
\end{aligned}
$

Width of the rectangle $= 5 \ \text {cm}$

Length of the rectangle $= 10 \ \text {cm}$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Area of the rectangle}        &= 5 \times 10 \\\\
                                    &= 50 \ \text {cm} ^2
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$50 \ \text{cm} ^2$

</div>
</div>

<div class='question question'>

A room measures $8 \ \text{m}$ by $20 \ \text{m}$.
Isabelle wants to tile the floor with square tiles.

What is the length of the largest tile she can use so that all the floor will be covered?

-  $2 \ \text{m}$
-  $3 \ \text{m}$
-  $4 \ \text{m}$
-  $5 \ \text{m}$

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

As per the question, 

Length $= 8 \ \text{m}$

Width  $= 20 \ \text{m}$


To use complete tiles, without cutting, the size of the square tile must divide both the length and width of the room. 

Let's find the highest common factor of $8$ and $20$.

Factors of $8 = 1, 2, \bold 4, 8$

Factors of $20 = 1, 2, \bold 4, 5, 20$

The highest common factor of $8$ and $20=4$.

So, the length of the largest square Isabelle can use so that all the floor will be covered $= 4 \ \text{m}$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Alternative solution

As per the question,

LCM of 8 and 20 $= 40$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Area of room}                                   &= \text{Length} \times \text{Width} \\\\
                                                      &= 8 \ \text{m} \times 20 \ \text{m}\\\\
                                                      &= 160 \ \text{m}^2 \\\\
\text{Largest square tile that can be used}           &= \dfrac {160} {40} \\\\
                                                      &= 4 \ \text{m}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='option'>
<p>C</p>
</div>
<div class='answer'>

$4 \ \text{m}$

</div>

What has the perimeter increased by?

Let the original width $= w$.

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Perimeter of original rectangle}              &= 2 \times (l + w)
\end{aligned}

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Perimeter of increased rectangle}    &= 2 \times (l + 10 + w + 10) \\\\
                                            &= 2 \times (l + w + 20) \\\\
                                            &= 2 \times (l + w) + 40 \\\\
                                            &= \text {Perimeter of original rectangle} + 40
\end{aligned}

<div class='question subquestion'>

Find the length of the side of the square.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Perimeter of square} = 4 \times \text{side}$


Let the side of the square $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
4 \times a          &= 20 \\\\
4a                  &= 20 \\\\
a                   &= \dfrac {20} {4} \\\\
a                   &= \dfrac {5 \times \cancel {4}} {\cancel {4}} \\\\
a                   &= 5 \ \text {cm}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$5 \ \text {cm}$
