The arrow is spun and it has an equal chance of stopping at any of the numbers. Is it

- More likely to land on a prime number than not a prime number?

- Just as likely to land on a prime number as not a prime number?

- More likely to land on a number that is not a prime number rather than a prime number?

![the-queens-school--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-1/section-1-question-23-00.png](/assets/the-queens-school--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-1/section-1-question-23-00.png "the-queens-school--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-1/section-1-question-23-00.png")

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Probability}  = \dfrac { \text{ Favourable outcomes} } { \text{Total outcomes} }$


$
\begin{aligned}
\text{prime numbers}         &= 2, 7, 11, 13 \\\\
\text{P(prime number)}       &= \dfrac {4} {8} \\\\  
                             &= \dfrac {4} {2 \times 4} \\\\
                             &= \dfrac {\cancel {4}} {2 \times \cancel {4}} \\\\
                             &= \dfrac {1} {2} \\\\
\text{not a prime number}    &= 6, 9, 12, 4 \\\\                             
\text{P(not a prime number)} &= \dfrac {4} {8} \\\\ 
                             &= \dfrac {4} {2 \times 4} \\\\
                             &= \dfrac {\cancel {4}} {2 \times \cancel {4}} \\\\
                             &= \dfrac {1} {2} \\\\                   
\end{aligned}
$

Just as likely to land on a prime number as not a prime number.

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='option'>
<p>B</p>
</div>
<div class='answer'>

Just as likely to land on a prime number as not a prime number.

</div>
