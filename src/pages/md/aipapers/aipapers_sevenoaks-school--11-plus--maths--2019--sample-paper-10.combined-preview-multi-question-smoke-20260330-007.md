Dini has $72$ small wooden cubes, each measuring $1 \ \text{cm} \times 1 \ \text{cm} \times 1 \ \text{cm}$. She arranges them all so that they form a cuboid. Given that the perimeter of the base of the cuboid is $16 \ \text{cm}$, what is its height?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

The cuboid is made from $1 \ \text{cm}$ cubes, so its length, width and height must all be whole numbers.

For the base,

$
\begin{aligned}
2 \times (l + w) &= 16 \\
l + w &= 8
\end{aligned}
$

So the possible whole-number side lengths of the base are:

$1$ and $7$, $2$ and $6$, $3$ and $5$, $4$ and $4$

Now check how many cubes would be in one layer.

$1 \times 7 = 7$, but $72 \div 7$ is not a whole number.

$2 \times 6 = 12$, and

$
\begin{aligned}
72 \div 12 &= 6
\end{aligned}
$

So a base of $2 \ \text{cm}$ by $6 \ \text{cm}$ gives a height of $6 \ \text{cm}$.

$3 \times 5 = 15$, but $72 \div 15$ is not a whole number.

$4 \times 4 = 16$, but $72 \div 16$ is not a whole number.

So the only possible height is $6 \ \text{cm}$.

m - 5 &= 15 \\\\
m &= 15 + 5 \\\\
m &= 20 \ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}$

Snowfall on Monday $= 20 \ \text{cm}$
