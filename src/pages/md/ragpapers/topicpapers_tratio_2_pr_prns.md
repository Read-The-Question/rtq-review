<div class='question question'>

A bottle of blackcurrant cordial makes enough drink to fill $60$ glasses when it is diluted in the ratio $1 \ \text{part}$ cordial to $4 \ \text{parts}$ water. 
How many glasses of drink would a bottle of cordial make if it is diluted in the ratio $1 \ \text{part}$ cordial to $5 \ \text{parts}$ water?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number of glasses of cordial used $= a$

Number of glasses of water used $= 60 - a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
1 \ \text{cordial} : 4 \ \text{water}           &= a \ \text{cordial} : (60 - a)) \ \text{water} \\\\
\dfrac {1} {4}                                  &= \dfrac {a} {60 - a} \\\\
60 - a                                          &= 4a \\\\
60                                              &= 4a + a \\\\
4a + a                                          &= 60 \\\\
5a                                              &= 60 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {60} {5} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {5 \times 12} {5} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 5 \times 12} {\cancel 5} \\\\
a                                               &= 12
\end{aligned}
$

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{cordial}                          &:& \text{water} \\\\
= & 1                                       &:& 5 \\\\
= & 12                                      &:& 5 \times 12 \\\\
= & 12                                      &:& 60
\end{array}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of glasses of drink}       &= 12 + 60 \\\\
                                        &= 72
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$72$

</div>
</div>

<div class='question question'>

Pairs of avocados are sold in a shop. $16$ pairs of avocados are sold at a total cost of $\pounds 24$. The shopkeeper now decides to separate the pairs and sell the avocados individually, but without any change to the total price. What is the cost of an individual avocado? Give your answer in pence.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cost of 1 pair of avacoda}      &= \pounds \dfrac {24} {16} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {24 \times 100} {16} \ \text{pence} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {2400} {16} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {16 \times 150} {16} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {\cancel {16} \times 150} {\cancel {16}} \\\\
                                      &= 150 \ \text{pence}
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cost of 1 avacoda}      &= \dfrac {150} {2} \\\\
                              &= \dfrac {2 \times 75} {2} \\\\
                              &= \dfrac {\cancel 2 \times 75} {\cancel 2} \\\\
                              &= 75 \ \text{pence}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>

Jason and Patrick win a prize of $\pounds 75$ for their entry into an inventing competition. For every $2$ hours that Patrick worked on the project Jason worked $3$, so it is decided that for every $\pounds 2$ that Patrick receives Jason should receive $\pounds 3$. How much will Jason and Patrick receive? Write your 
answers in the boxes below.

<div class='working'>

Let the money received by Jason $= a$

Money received by Patrick $= 75 - a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
2 \ \text{Patrick} : 3 \ \text{Jason}           &= (75 - a) \ \text{Patrick} : a \ \text{Jason} \\\\
\dfrac {2} {3}                                  &= \dfrac {75 - a} {a} \\\\
2a                                              &= 3 \times (75 - a) \\\\
2a                                              &= 3 \times 75 - 3a \\\\
2a                                              &= 225 - 3a \\\\
2a + 3a                                         &= 225 \\\\
5a                                              &= 225 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {225} {5} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {5 \times 45} {5} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 5 \times 45} {\cancel 5} \\\\
a                                               &= \pounds 45
\end{aligned}

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Money received by Jason}        &= \pounds 45 \\\\

\end{aligned}

<div class='question subquestion'>

Convert $472 \ \text{feet}$ to chains, yards and feet using as many chains as possible, then yards and then feet.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of chains}     &= \dfrac {472} {22 \times 3} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {472} {66} \\\\
                            &= 7 \dfrac {10} {66} \\\\
                            &= 7 \\\\
\text{Remaining feet}       &= 472 - 7 \times 22 \times 3 \\\\
                            &= 472 - 462 \\\\
                            &= 10 \\\\
\text{Number of yards}      &= \dfrac {10} {3} \\\\
                            &= 3 \dfrac {1} {3} \\\\
                            &= 3 \\\\
\text{Remaining feet}       &= 10 - 3 \times 3 \\\\
                            &= 10 - 9 \\\\
                            &= 1
\end{aligned}
$
