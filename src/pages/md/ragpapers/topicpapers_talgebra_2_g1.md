<div class='question question'>

$40 \%$ of the children on a school trip are boys and there are $72$ girls. How many children are on the trip?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the total number of children $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
(100 - 40) \% \ \text{of} \ a         &= 72 \\\\
\dfrac {60} {100} \times a          &= 72 \\\\
a                                   &= 72 \times \dfrac {100} {60} \\\\
a                                   &= \dfrac {72 \times 100} {60} \\\\
a                                   &= \dfrac {12 \times 6 \times 100} {6 \times 10} \\\\
a                                   &= \dfrac {12 \times \cancel 6 \times 10 \cancel 0} {\cancel 6 \times 1 \cancel 0} \\\\
a                                   &= 12 \times 10 \\\\
a                                   &= 120
\end{aligned}
$

The total number of children $= 120$.

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$120$

</div>

What is the largest of the three numbers?

a                                   &= \dfrac {60} {3} \\\\
a                                   &= \dfrac {20 \times 3} {3} \\\\

a                                   &= 20
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}

                                    &= 21
\end{aligned}

Let the missing denominator $= d$

a              &= \dfrac {2 \times 3} {6} \\\\

                                    &= \dfrac {1} {2} \times \dfrac {2} {3} \times \dfrac {2} {3} \times 9000000 \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {1 \times 2 \times 2 \times 9000000} {2 \times 3 \times 3} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 1000000} {2 \times 3 \times 3} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {2 \times \cancel 2 \times \cancel 3 \times \cancel 3 \times 1000000} {\cancel 2 \times \cancel 3 \times \cancel 3} \\\\
                                    &= 2 \times 1000000 \\\\
                                    &= \pounds 2000000
\end{aligned}
$

Share of Archie in Mr Rich wealth $= \pounds 2 \ \text{million}$.

<div class='question question'>

Half of the pupils in a school are boys;
one third of the boys play soccer;
one quarter of the boy soccer players get soccer colours;
one fifth of those who win soccer colours are boys in form 8CC.
There are $10$ boy with soccer in 8CC.

How many pupils are there in the school?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number of pupils in the school $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac {1} {2} \times \left( \dfrac {1} {3} \times \left( \dfrac {1} {4} \times \left( \dfrac {1} {5} \times a \right) \right) \right)  &= 10 \\\\
\dfrac {1} {2} \times \dfrac {1} {3} \times \dfrac {1} {4} \times \dfrac {1} {5} \times a                                               &= 10 \\\\
\dfrac {a} {2 \times 3 \times 4 \times 5}                                                                                               &= 10 \\\\
a                                                                                                                                       &= 10 \times 2 \times 3 \times 4 \times 5 \\\\
a                                                                                                                                       &= 1200
\end{aligned}
$

Total number of pupils in the school $= 1200$.

</div>
</div>

$1200$
