The angles in a triangle always add up to $180 \degree$ . In the triangle shown above angle $a \degree$ is three times as big as angle $b \degree$ and angle $b \degree$ is twice as large as angle $c \degree$. Find the angle $d \degree$ shown in the diagram.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Sum of angles in a triangle} = 180 \degree$


ABBR: $\text{Angles on a straight line add up to} = 180 \degree$


As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
b                                             &= 2c \\\\
a                                             &= 3b \\\\
a                                             &= 3 \times 2c \\\\
a + b + c                                     &= 180 \\\\
3 \times 2c+ 2c + c                           &= 180 \\\\
6c+ 2c + c                                    &= 180 \\\\
9c                                            &= 180 \\\\
c                                             &= \dfrac {180} {9} \\\\
c                                             &= 20 \degree \\\\
b                                             &= 2c \\\\
b                                             &= 2 \times 20 \\\\
b                                             &= 40 \degree
\end{aligned}
$

Let's use the value of $b$ to find $d$.

$
\begin{aligned}
b + d                                         &= 180 \\\\
40 + d                                        &= 180 \\\\
d                                             &= 180 - 40 \\\\
d                                             &= 140 \degree
\end{aligned}
$

</div>

<div class='answer'>

$140 \degree$

For every $2 \ \text{hours}$ that Patrick worked on the project Jason worked $3$, so it is decided that for every $\pounds 2$ that Patrick receives Jason should receive $\pounds 3$. Money received by Patrick $= 75 - a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}

\dfrac {2} {3}                 &= \dfrac {75 - a} {a} \\\\
