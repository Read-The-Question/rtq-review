James is driving  on the M25. He is annoyed because a $35  \ \text{mile}$ stretch of road works has a speed restriction of $50 \ \text{mph}$ rather being able to travel at $70 \ \text{mph}$.

How much longer is his journey as a result of the roadworks?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text {Time} = \dfrac {\text{Distance}} {\text{Speed}}$


Let the time taken (without restriction) $= t$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
t                   &= \dfrac{35}{70} \\\\
t                   &= \dfrac{35}{35 \times 2} \\\\
t                   &= \dfrac{\cancel{35}}{\cancel{35} \times 2} \\\\
t                   &= \dfrac{1}{2} \\\\
t                   &= 0.5 \text{ hour} \\\\
\text{Time}         &= \dfrac{35}{50} \\\\
                    &= \dfrac{\cancel{5} \times 7}{\cancel{5} \times 10} \\\\
                    &= \dfrac{7}{10} \\\\
                    &= 0.7 \text{ hour} \\\\
\text{Extra time}   &= 0.7 - t \\\\
                    &= 0.7 - 0.5 \\\\
                    &= 0.2 \text{ hour}
\end{aligned}
$

<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

Milly has some marbles at the start of the week.

One day at school she does well in her marble games with her friends and increase her total by a tenth.

Has she ended up with more, less or the same as she had at the start of the week? Explain your answer carefully.

</div>

<div class='working'>

Let the number of marbles Milly had at the start of the week $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Total marbles at the end of first day}     &= a + 10 \% \ \text{of} \ a \\\\
                                                 &= a + \dfrac {10} {100} \times a \\\\
                                                 &= a + \dfrac {10a} {100} \\\\
                                                 &= \dfrac {a \times 100} {1 \times 100} + \dfrac {10a} {100} \\\\
                                                 &= \dfrac {100a} {100} + \dfrac {10a} {100} \\\\
                                                 &= \dfrac {100a + 10a} {100} \\\\
                                                 &= \dfrac {110a} {100} \\\\
                                                 &= \dfrac {11 \cancel 0a} {10 \cancel 0} \\\\
                                                 &= \dfrac {11a} {10} \\\\
\text{Total marbles at the end of second day}    &= \dfrac {11a} {10} - 10 \% \ \text{of} \ \dfrac {11a} {10} \\\\
                                                 &= \dfrac {11a} {10} - \dfrac {10} {100} \times \dfrac {11a} {10} \\\\
                                                 &= \dfrac {11a} {10} - \dfrac {10 \times 11a} {100 \times 10} \\\\
                                                 &= \dfrac {11a} {10} - \dfrac {\cancel {10} \times 11a} {100 \times \cancel {10}} \\\\
                                                 &= \dfrac {11a} {10} - \dfrac {11a} {100} \\\\
                                                 &= \dfrac {11a \times 10} {10 \times 10} - \dfrac {11a} {100} \\\\
                                                 &= \dfrac {110a} {100} - \dfrac {11a} {100} \\\\
                                                 &= \dfrac {110a - 11a} {100} \\\\
                                                 &= \dfrac {99a} {100} \\\\
                                                 &= 0.99a
\end{aligned}
