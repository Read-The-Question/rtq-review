Below are diagrams of a small cube and larger cuboid.
How many of the small cubes can be fully fitted into the larger cuboid?
All measurements are in centimetres.

![chigwell-school--11-plus--maths--2020--sample-paper-1/section-1-question-27-00.png](/assets/chigwell-school--11-plus--maths--2020--sample-paper-1/section-1-question-27-00.png "chigwell-school--11-plus--maths--2020--sample-paper-1/section-1-question-27-00.png")

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Volume of a cuboid} = \text{length} \times \text{width} \times \text{height}$


ABBR: $\text{Volume of a cube} = (\text{Side}) ^ { 3 }$


$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of small cubes} &= \dfrac {\text{Volume of large cuboid}} {\text{Volume of small cube}} \\\\
                       &= \dfrac {18 \times 9 \times 9} {3 \times 3 \times 3} \\\\
                       &= \dfrac {6 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 9} {3 \times 3 \times 3} \\\\
                       &= \dfrac {6 \times \cancel{3} \times \cancel{3} \times \cancel{3} \times 9} { \cancel{3} \times \cancel{3} \times \cancel{3}} \\\\
                       &= 6 \times 9 \\\\
                       &= 54 
\end{aligned}
$


</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$54$

</div>
</div>

What is the largest number of $15\ \text{cm}$ by $30\ \text{cm}$ by $20\ \text{cm}$ shoe boxes that can fit in a $1\ \text{m} \times 3\ \text{m} \times 2\ \text{m}$ crate?

Be careful - you can’t chop up the shoe boxes!

![city-of-london-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-1/section-2-question-5-00.png](/assets/city-of-london-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-1/section-2-question-5-00.png "city-of-london-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-1/section-2-question-5-00.png")       ![city-of-london-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-1/section-2-question-5-01.png](/assets/city-of-london-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-1/section-2-question-5-01.png "city-of-london-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-1/section-2-question-5-01.png")

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Volume of a cuboid} = \text{length} \times \text{width} \times \text{height}$


$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of shoe boxes} &= \dfrac {\text{Volume of the crate}} {\text{Volume of the shoe boxes}} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {0.9 \ \text{m} \times 3 \ \text{m} \times 2 \ \text{m}}{15 \ \text{cm} \times 30 \ \text{cm} \times 20 \ \text{cm}} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {90 \ \text{cm} \times 300 \ \text{cm} \times 200 \ \text{cm}}{15 \ \text{cm} \times 30 \ \text{cm} \times 20 \ \text{cm}} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {3 \times 3 \times 10 \times 30 \times 10 \times 20 \times 10}{3 \times 5 \times 30 \times 20} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {\cancel{3} \times 3 \times 2 \times \cancel{5} \times \cancel{30} \times 10 \times \cancel{20} \times 10} {\cancel{3} \times \cancel{5} \times \cancel{30} \times \cancel{20}} \\\\
                            &= 3 \times 2 \times 10 \times 10 \\\\
                            &= 600
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$600$

A second cube has a volume of $27 \ \text{cm}^3$. 

What is the total surface area of the second cube?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Surface area of cube} = 6 \times \text{edge} \times \text{edge}$


Let the edge of cube $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned} 
a^3                             &= 27 \\\\
a                               &= \sqrt[3]{27} \\\\
                                &= \sqrt[3]{3 \times 3 \times 3} \\\\
                                &= 3 \ \text{cm} \\\\
\text{Surface area}             &= 6a^2 \\\\ 
                                &= 6 \times 3^2 \\\\
                                &= 6 \times 3^2 \\\\
                                &= 6 \times 9 \\\\ 
                                &= 54 \ \text{cm}^2
\end {aligned}
$

Surface area $= 54 \ \text {cm} ^ 3$
