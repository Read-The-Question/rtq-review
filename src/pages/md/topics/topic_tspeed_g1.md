**Question**

The same coach takes $3 \ \text{h}$ to get to Bristol, a journey of $120 \ \text{miles}$. What is its average speed in $\text{miles per hour}$?

- ABBR: $\text {Speed} = \dfrac {\text{Distance}} {\text{Time}}$
</div>

**Working**

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Speed}    &= \dfrac {120 \ \text {miles}} {3 \ \text {hour}} \\\\
                 &= \dfrac {3 \times 40 } {3} \\\\
                 &= \dfrac { \cancel 3 \times 40} {\cancel 3} \\\\
                 &= 40 \ \text {miles/hour}
\end{aligned}
$

**Question**

When the speed is $40 \ \text{m/s}$, how far has the ball fallen?

</div>
<div class="questioncodeblock" style="margin: 1rem 0; padding: 0.85rem 1rem; border: 1px solid #9ea7b3; border-radius: 6px;">
<div class="questioncodeblock_title">

<div class="tips placeholder" style="margin: 1rem 0; padding: 0.85rem 1rem; border: 1px solid #9ea7b3; border-radius: 6px;">

<div class="tip">

<div class="formulas placeholder" style="margin: 1rem 0; padding: 0.85rem 1rem; border: 1px solid #9ea7b3; border-radius: 6px;">

<div class="formula">
%empty%
</div>

<div class="working" style="margin: 1rem 0; padding: 0.85rem 1rem; border: 1px solid #9ea7b3; border-radius: 6px;">

**Working**

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
S^2          &= 20 \times d \\\\
40^2         &= 20 \times d \\\\
20 \times d  &= 40 \times 40 \\\\
d            &= \dfrac {40 \times 40}{20} \\\\
d            &= \dfrac {40 \times 20 \times 2}{20} \\\\
d            &= \dfrac {40 \times \cancel {20} \times 2}{\cancel {20}} \\\\
