This sequence of numbers goes up by $30$ each time.

$30,\:\:\:\: 60,\:\:\:\: 90,\:\:\:\: 120,\:\:\:\: 150,\:\:\:\: …….$

The sequence continues.

Will the number $1330$ be in the sequence? 

Explain how you know:

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR:  Let's find the relation between the consecutive terms of the sequence.


$
\begin{matrix}
&   \footnotesize {\maroonC {(+30)} }
&&  \footnotesize {\maroonC {(+30)} }
&&  \footnotesize {\maroonC {(+30)} }
&&  \footnotesize {\maroonC {(+30)} }
&&  \footnotesize {\maroonC {(+30)} }  \\
30,  &&    60,  &&  90,  &&   120,  &&  150,  && \ldots
\end{matrix}
$

The next term is calculated by adding $30$ to the previous term.

This is an arithmetic sequence too and the difference between the consecutive patterns of the sequence is constant.

The next term is calculated by adding multiple of $30$ to the first term and the number of times it is added is based on the position in the sequence.


Let the number of term $= n$

$
\begin{aligned}
30 + 30 \times (n - 1)  &= 1330 \\\\
30 + 30n - 30           &= 1330 \\\\
30n                     &= 1330 \\\\
n                       &= \dfrac {1330} {30} \\\\
n                       &= \dfrac {133 \cancel {0}} {3 \cancel {0}} \\\\
n                       &= \dfrac {133} {3} \\\\
n                       &= 44 \dfrac {1} {3}
\end{aligned}
$

$44 \dfrac {1} {3}$ is not a whole number.

Hence, the number $1330$ will not be in the sequence.

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

No

</div>

Which is bigger: $20\%$ of $25$ or $25\%$ of $20$?

(Marks will only be given for answers accompanied by appropriate working)

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{First}    &= 20\% \ \text{of} \ 25 \\\\
                &= \dfrac{20}{100} \times 25 \\\\
                &= \dfrac{20 \times 25}{100}  \\\\
                &= \dfrac{5 \times 4 \times 25}{4 \times 25}  \\\\
                &= \dfrac{5 \times \cancel {4} \times \cancel {25}}{\cancel {4} \times \cancel {25}}  \\\\
                &= 5 \\\\
\text{Second}   &= 25\% \ \text{of} \ 20 \\\\
                &= \dfrac{25}{100} \times 20 \\\\
                &= \dfrac{25 \times 20}{100}  \\\\
                &= \dfrac{25 \times 4 \times 5}{25 \times 4}  \\\\
                &= \dfrac{\cancel {25} \times \cancel {4} \times 5}{\cancel {25} \times \cancel{4}}  \\\\
                &= 5
\end{aligned}
$

Both are equal.

</div>

Both are equal.

Write the same missing number in each box.

$\Box\:\:\:\:\times\:\:\:\:\Box\:\:\:\:\times\:\:\:\:\Box= \:\:\:512$

a^3                 &= 8 \times 64 \\\\
a^3                 &= 8 \times 8 \times 8 \\\\
a^3                 &= 8^3 \\\\
a                   &= 8
\end{aligned}
$

The number in the missing box $= 8$
