<div class='question subquestion'>

The number of centimetres in $4 \ \text{inches}$.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number of centimetres $= a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
12 \ \text{inches} : 30 \ \text{cm}             &= 4 \ \text{inches} : a \ \text{cm} \\\\
\dfrac {12} {30}                                &= \dfrac {4} {a} \\\\
12a                                             &= 4 \times 30 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {4 \times 30} {12} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {4 \times 3 \times 10} {4 \times 3} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times \cancel 3 \times 10} {\cancel 4 \times \cancel 3} \\\\
a                                               &= 10 \ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$

Number of centimetres in $4 \ \text{inches} = 10 \ \text{cm}$ 

</div>

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{inches}                       &:& \text{centimeters} \\\\
= & 12                                  &:& 30 \\\\

= & 1 \times 4                          &:& \dfrac {30 \times 4} {12} \\\\
= & 4                                   &:& \dfrac {3 \times 10 \times 4} {3 \times 4} \\\\
= & 4                                   &:& \dfrac {\cancel 3 \times 10 \times \cancel 4} {\cancel 3 \times \cancel 4} \\\\
= & 4 \ \text{inches}                   &:& 10 \ \text{cm}
\end{array}

<div class='question question'>

If a dozen eggs cost $\pounds 2.80$, how much do $18$ eggs cost?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cost of 1 egg}      &= \pounds \dfrac {2.8} {12} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {280} {12} \text{p} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {4 \times 70} {4 \times 3} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times 70} {\cancel 4 \times 3} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {70} {3} \text{p} \\\\
\text{Cost of 18 egg}     &= 18 \times \dfrac {70} {3} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {18 \times 70} {3} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {6 \times 3 \times 70} {3} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {6 \times \cancel 3 \times 70} {\cancel 3} \\\\
                          &= 6 \times 70 \\\\
                          &= 420 \text{p} \\\\
                          &= \pounds 4.2
\end{aligned}
$

12a                                             &= 18 \times 2.8 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {18 \times 2.8} {12} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {18 \times 28} {12 \times 10} \\\\

a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 6 \times 3 \times \cancel 2 \times 14} {\cancel 6 \times \cancel 2 \times 10} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {3 \times 14} {10} \\\\
