Look at these three numbers.

![leicester-high-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-4/section-1-question-13-subquestion-1-00.png](/assets/leicester-high-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-4/section-1-question-13-subquestion-1-00.png "leicester-high-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-4/section-1-question-13-subquestion-1-00.png")   ![leicester-high-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-4/section-1-question-13-subquestion-1-01.png](/assets/leicester-high-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-4/section-1-question-13-subquestion-1-01.png "leicester-high-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-4/section-1-question-13-subquestion-1-01.png")   ![leicester-high-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-4/section-1-question-13-subquestion-1-02.png](/assets/leicester-high-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-4/section-1-question-13-subquestion-1-02.png "leicester-high-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-4/section-1-question-13-subquestion-1-02.png")

Show that the **mean** of the three numbers is $\bold{\text{10}}$.

Explain why the **median** of the three numbers is $\bold{\text{10}}$.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Mean} = \dfrac {\text{Total of items}} {\text{Number of items}}$


As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Mean}    &= \dfrac {9 + 11 + 10} {3} \\\\
                &= \dfrac {30} {3} \\\\
                &= \dfrac {3 \times 10} {3} \\\\
                &= \dfrac {\cancel {3} \times 10} {\cancel {3}} \\\\
                &= 10
\end {aligned}
$

ABBR: If there is an odd number of numbers, the median is the middle number. If there is an even count of numbers, the median is the average of the middle two numbers.


$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Numbers}  &= 9, 11, 10 \\\\
\text {Ordered Numbers}  &= 9, \maroonC{10}, 11 \\\\
\end {aligned}
$

Median $= 10$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$10$

</div>

Give an example.

ABBR: $\text{Mean} = \dfrac {\text{Total of items}} {\text{Number of items}}$

                &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times 10} {\cancel 4} \\\\
                &= \green{10} \\\\

The mean age of the five members of the `Macaroon $5$' boy band is $18$. 

A new member joins the band. His age is $24$. 
What is the new mean age of the group?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Mean} = \dfrac {\text{Total of items}} {\text{Number of items}}$


$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Total previous ages}      &= 18 \times 5 \\\\
                                &= 90 \\\\
\text{New mean}                 &= \dfrac {90 + 24} {6} \\\\
                                &= \dfrac {114} {6} \\\\
                                &= \dfrac {6 \times 19} {6} \\\\
                                &= \dfrac {\cancel 6 \times 19} {\cancel 6} \\\\
                                &= 19
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$19$

<div class='question subquestion'>

Find $4$ numbers which have a mean of $7$, a median of $8$ and a mode of $9$.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: If there is an odd number of numbers, the median is the middle number. If there is an even count of numbers, the median is the average of the middle two numbers.

ABBR: $\text{Mean} = \dfrac {\text{Total of items}} {\text{Number of items}}$


If mode is $9$, then at least $2$ numbers must be $9$.

There are even count of numbers, the median will be the mean of the middle two values. As the median is $8$, $9$ are not the middle values.

Let the four numbers in ascending order $= a, b, 9, 9$

As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
\dfrac {a + b + 9 + 9} {4}              &= 7 \\\\
a + b + 9 + 9                           &= 7 \times 4 \\\\
a + b + 18                              &= 28 \\\\
a + b                                   &= 28 - 18 \\\\
a + b                                   &= 10   & (1)
\end {aligned}
$

Lets arrange the numbers in ascending order.

$
\begin {aligned}
\text{Ordered numbers}          &= a, \quad \maroonC{b}, \quad \maroonC{9}, \quad 9 \\\\
\dfrac {b + 9} {2}              &= 8 \\\\
b + 9                           &= 8 \times 2 \\\\
b + 9                           &= 16 \\\\
b                               &= 16 - 9 \\\\
b                               &= 7
\end {aligned}
$

Lets use value of $b$ in equation $(1)$ to get the value of $a$.

$
\begin {aligned}
a + b                   &= 10 \\\\
a                       &= 10 - b \\\\
a                       &= 10 - 7 \\\\
a                       &= 3
\end {aligned}
$

Required four numbers $= 3, 7, 9, 9$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$3, 7, 9, 9$

</div>

<div class='question subquestion'>

The mean weight of $5$ children is $45 \ \text{kg}$.

The mean weight of the lightest three children is $42 \ \text{kg}$ and the mean weight of the heaviest three children is $49 \ \text{kg}$. 

What is the median weight of the children in kg?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: If there is an odd number of numbers, the median is the middle number. If there is an even count of numbers, the median is the average of the middle two numbers.

ABBR: $\text{Mean} = \dfrac {\text{Total of items}} {\text{Number of items}}$


Let the weights of $5$ children $= a, b, c, d, e$

As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
\dfrac {a + b + c + d + e} {5}        &= 45 \\\\
a + b + c + d + e                     &= 45 \times 5 \\\\
a + b + c + d + e                     &= 225   & (1) \\\\
\dfrac {a + b + c} {3}                &= 42 \\\\
a + b + c                             &= 42 \times 3 \\\\
a + b + c                             &= 126   & (2) \\\\
\dfrac {b + c + d} {3}                &= 49 \\\\
c + d + e                             &= 49 \times 3 \\\\
c + d + e                             &= 147   & (3)
\end {aligned}
$

Let's substract equation $(1)$ from the sum of equation $(2)$ and equation $(3)$.

$
\begin{aligned}
a + b + c + c + d + e - (a + b + c + d + e)             &= 126 + 147 - 225 \\\\
a + b + 2c + d + e - a - b - c - d - e                  &= 273 - 225 \\\\
a - a + b - b + 2c - c + d - d + e - e                  &= 48 \\\\
c                                                       &= 48 \ \text{kg}
\end{aligned}
$

Median weight of the children $= 48 \ \text{kg}$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$48 \ \text{kg}$

</div>

<div class='question question'>

A set of five different positive integers has a mean (average) of $20$ and a median of $18$.

What is the greatest possible integer in the set? 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Mean} = \dfrac {\text{Total of items}} {\text{Number of items}}$


ABBR: If there is an odd number of numbers, the median is the middle number. If there is an even count of numbers, the median is the average of the middle two numbers.


Let the five different positive numbers $= a1, a2, a3, a4, a5$

Let's arrange the numbers in ascending order.

$
\begin {aligned}
\text{Ordered numbers}   &= a1, \quad a2, \quad \maroonC{a3}, \quad a4, \quad a5 \\\\
\text{Median}            &= a3 \\\\
\end {aligned}
$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
a3                                              &= 18 \\\\
\dfrac {a1 + a2 + a3 + a4 + a5} {5}             &= 20 \\\\
a1 + a2 + a3 + a4 + a5                          &= 20 \times 5 \\\\
a1 + a2 + 18 + a4 + a5                          &= 100 \\\\
a1 + a2 + a4 + a5                               &= 100 - 18 \\\\
a1 + a2 + a4 + a5                               &= 82
\end{aligned}
$

Minimum positive value of $a1 = 1$

Minimum positive value of $a2 = 2$

Minimum positive value of $a4 = 19$

Using minimum possible values of $a1, a2$ and $a4$ to find the maximum possible value of $a5$.

$
\begin{aligned}
a1 + a2 + a4 + a5               &= 82 \\\\
1 + 2 + 19 + a5                 &= 82 \\\\
22 + a5                         &= 82 \\\\
a5                              &= 82 - 22 \\\\
a5                              &= 60
\end{aligned}
$

Greatest possible value in the set $= 60$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$60$

</div>
