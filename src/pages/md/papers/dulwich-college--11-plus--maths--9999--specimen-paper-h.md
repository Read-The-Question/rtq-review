Given that Tom eats $\dfrac{3}{5}$ of a cake and his friend, Jerry, eats $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the cake, what fraction of the cake is left? 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Cake left}   &= 1 - \left( \dfrac {3} {5} + \dfrac {1} {3} \right) \\\\
                    &= 1 - \left( \dfrac {3 \times 3} {5 \times 3} + \dfrac {1 \times 5} {3 \times 5} \right) \\\\
                    &= 1 - \left( \dfrac {9} {15} + \dfrac {5} {15} \right) \\\\
                    &= 1 - \left( \dfrac {9 + 5} {15} \right) \\\\
                    &= 1 - \dfrac {14} {15} \\\\
                    &= \dfrac {1 \times 15} {1 \times 15} - \dfrac {14} {15} \\\\
                    &= \dfrac {15} {15} - \dfrac {14} {15} \\\\
                    &= \dfrac {15 - 14} {15} \\\\
                    &= \dfrac {1} {15}
\end{aligned} 
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$\dfrac {1} {15}$

<div class='question question'>

Shortcrust pastry is made using flour and fat in the ratio $2 : 1$.

How many grams of flour are needed to make $900 \ \text{grams}$ of shortcrust pastry?  

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Flour needed}              &= \dfrac {2} {3} \times 900 \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {2 \times 900} {3} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {2 \times 300 \times 3} {3} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {2 \times 300 \times \cancel{3}} {\cancel{3}} \\\\
                                 &= 2 \times 300  \\\\
                                 &= 600 \ \text{g}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Let the quantity of flour needed $= a$

Quantity of fat needed $= 900 - a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
1 \ \text{grams} : 2 \ \text{grams}                     &= (900 - a) \ \text{grams} : a \ \text{grams} \\\\
\dfrac{1} {2}                                   &= \dfrac {900 - a} {a} \\\\
a                                               &= (900 - a) \times 2 \\\\
a                                               &= 1800 - 2a \\\\
a + 2a                                          &= 1800 \\\\
3a                                              &= 1800 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {1800} {3} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {3 \times 600} {3} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 3 \times 600} {\cancel 3} \\\\
a                                               &= 600 \ \text{g}
\end{aligned}
$

Flour $= 600 \ \text{g}$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using whole to part ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{pastry}                       &:& \text{flour} \\\\
= & 2 + 1                               &:& 2 \\\\
= & 3                                   &:& 2 \\\\
= & 3 \times 300                        &:& 2 \times 300 \\\\
= & 900 \ \text{g}                      &:& 600 \ \text{g}
\end{array}
$

Flour $= 600 \ \text{g}$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$600 \ \text{g}$

</div>
