Calculate how many seconds there are in $15 \dfrac{1}{2}$ hours.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin {aligned}
&= 15 \dfrac {1} {2} \ \text {hours} \\\\
&= \dfrac {31} {2} \ \text {hours} \\\\
&= \dfrac {31} {2} \times 60 \times 60 \ \text {seconds} \\\\
&= \dfrac {31 \times 60 \times 60} {2} \\\\
&= \dfrac {31 \times 60 \times 30 \times 2} {2} \\\\
&= \dfrac {31 \times 60 \times 30 \times \cancel{2}} {\cancel{2}} \\\\
&= 31 \times 60 \times 30 \\\\
&= 55800 \ \text {seconds}
\end {aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$55800 \ \text {seconds}$

Find the mean average of $8, 11,  15, 26$.

<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Mean} = \dfrac {\text{Total of items}} {\text{Number of items}}$


$
\begin {aligned}
\text{Mean}     &= \dfrac {8 + 11 + 15 + 26} {4} \\\\
                &= \dfrac {60} {4} \\\\
                &= \dfrac {4 \times 15} {4} \\\\
                &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times 15} {\cancel 4} \\\\
                &= 15
\end {aligned}

<div class='question question'>

Find the greatest number of $28 \text{p}$ stamps which can be bought for $\pounds 5$.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: It is really important to use the right units in calculations and convert where required
.

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Number of stamps}    &= \dfrac {\pounds 5} {28 \text {p}} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {500 \text {p}} {28 \text {p}} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {4 \times 125} {4 \times 7} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {\cancel {4} \times 125} {\cancel {4} \times 7} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {125} {7} \\\\
                            &= 17 \ \text {remainder} \ 6 \\\\
                            &\approx 17
\end {aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$17$

</div>
