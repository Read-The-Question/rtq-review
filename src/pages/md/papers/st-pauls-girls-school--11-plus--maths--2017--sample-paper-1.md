I think of a number $n$. 

$5n$ is more than $60$, but $n + 5$ is less than $20$. What are the possible values of $n$?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
5n                                              &> 60 \\\\
\dfrac  {5n} {5}                                &> \dfrac {60} {5} \\\\
\dfrac {\cancel {5} \times n} {\cancel {5}}     &> \dfrac {5 \times 12} {5} \\\\
n                                               &> \dfrac {\cancel {5} \times 12} {\cancel {5}} \\\\
n                                               &> 12
\end{aligned}
$

Also,

$
\begin{aligned}
n + 5       &< 20 \\\\
n + 5 - 5   &< 20 - 5 \\\\
n           &< 15
\end{aligned}
$

As per the above calcuations,

$12 < n < 15$

Hence the possible values of $n$ $= 13, 14$

A shop sells bananas and pears. 

Max buys $1$ banana and $2$ pears. He paid $94 \text{p}$. 

Emily buys $1$ banana and $1$ pear. She paid $62 \text{p}$. 

How much does $1$ banana cost?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the cost of a banana (in $\text {p}$) $= a$

Let the cost of a pear (in $\text {p}$) $= b$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
a + b   &= 62 \text {p} \\\\
a + 2b  &= 94 \text {p}
\end{aligned}
$

We have got $2$ variables and we have $2$ equations to solve them. Lets eliminate to reduce it to just one variable.

Subtract the first equation from the second to reduce it to one variable.

$
\begin {aligned}
a + 2b - (a + b)        &= 94 - 62 \\\\
a - a + 2b - b          &= 32 \\\\
b                       &= 32\text{p}
\end {aligned}
$

Let's substitute the value of $b$ in one of the equations.

$
\begin {aligned}
a + b                   &= 62 \\\\
a + 32                  &= 62 \\\\
a                       &= 62 - 32 \\\\
a                       &= 30\text {p}
\end {aligned}
$

Cost of a banana $= 30\text {p}$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$30 \text{p}$

</div>

\dfrac {4c - 3c} {12}                                                   &= 2 \\\\
\dfrac {c} {12}                                                         &= 2 \\\\
c                                                                       &= 2 \times 12 \\\\
c                                                                       &= 24
\end{aligned}
$

Number of coins on the table $= 24$
