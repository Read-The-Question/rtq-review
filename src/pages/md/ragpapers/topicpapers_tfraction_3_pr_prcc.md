<div class='question question'>

The contents of part of a wardrobe are shown in the pie chart below:

![missing image](/papers/missing_image.svg)


What fraction, in its simplest form, of **Footwear** are boots? 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Fraction of Boots}       &= \dfrac {\text {Boots}} {\text {Total Footwear}} \\\\
                                &= \dfrac {9} {63+9}    \\\\
                                &= \dfrac {9} {72}      \\\\
                                &= \dfrac {\cancel{9}} {8 \times \cancel{9}}    \\\\
                                &= \dfrac {1} {8}   
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$\dfrac {1} {8}$

<div class='question question'>


Which of these Is the smallest fraction? Circle your answer.
 
$\bold{\dfrac{3}{4}\:\:\dfrac{2}{3}\:\:\dfrac{5}{12}\:\:\dfrac{9}{4}}$

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let's make the denominator same for all fractions

LCM for $4, \quad 3 \ \text{and} \ 12 = 12$

$
\begin{aligned}
&= \dfrac{3}{4}, \quad \dfrac{2}{3}, \quad \dfrac{5}{12}, \quad \dfrac{9}{4} \\\\
&= \dfrac {3 \times 3} {4 \times 3}, \quad \dfrac {2 \times 4} {3 \times 4}, \quad \dfrac {5} {12}, \quad \dfrac {9 \times 3} {4 \times 3} \\\\
&= \dfrac {9} {12}, \quad \dfrac {8} {12}, \quad \dfrac {5} {12}, \quad \dfrac {27} {12}
\end{aligned}
$

The smallest fraction is $\dfrac {5} {12}$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$\dfrac {5} {12}$

</div>
</div>
