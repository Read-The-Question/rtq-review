Fawzan went shopping with $\pounds 100$ in his wallet.

He spent $10 \%$ of his money on a book and $20 \%$ of the rest on a DVD. How much money did he then have left?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Money spent on book}              &= 10 \% \ \text{of} \ 100 \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {10} {100} \times 100 \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {10 \times 100} {100} \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {10 \times \cancel {100}} {\cancel {100}} \\\\
                                        &= \pounds 10
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Money left after buying books}            &= 100 - 10 \\\\
                                                &= \pounds 90
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Money spent on DVD}               &= 20 \% \ \text{of} \ 90 \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {20} {100} \times 90 \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {20 \times 90} {100} \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {2 \cancel 0 \times 9 \cancel 0} {1 \cancel 0 \cancel 0} \\\\
                                        &= 2 \times 9 \\\\
                                        &= \pounds 18
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Money left with Fawzan after shooping}            &= 90 - 18 \\\\
                                                        &= \pounds 72
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Alternate solution.

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Money left with Fawzan after shopping}        &= 80 \% \ \text{of} \ \left( 90 \% \ \text{of} \ \pounds 100 \right) \\\\
                                                    &= \dfrac {80} {100} \times \dfrac {90} {100} \times 100 \\\\
                                                    &= \dfrac {80 \times 90 \times 100} {100 \times 100} \\\\
                                                    &= \dfrac {8 \cancel 0 \times 9 \cancel 0 \times \cancel {100}} {1 \cancel 0 \cancel 0 \times \cancel {100}} \\\\
                                                    &= 8 \times 9 \\\\
                                                    &= \pounds 72
\end{aligned}

My flask holds $480 \ \text{ml}$ when it is one quarter empty. 

How much does it hold when it is one quarter full?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let quantity of water it hold at one quarter full $= a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
\left( 1 - \dfrac {1} {4} \right) \ \text{fraction} : 480 \ \text{volume}  &= \dfrac {1} {4} \ \text{fraction} : a \ \text{volume} \\\\
\dfrac{\left( 1 - \dfrac {1} {4} \right)} {480}                            &= \dfrac {\dfrac {1} {4}} {a} \\\\
\dfrac{\dfrac {3} {4}} {480}                                               &= \dfrac {\dfrac {1} {4}} {a} \\\\
\dfrac {3} {4 \times 480}                                                  &= \dfrac {1} {4 \times a} \\\\
3 \times 4 \times a                                                        &= 4 \times 480 \\\\
a                                                                          &= \dfrac {4 \times 480} {3 \times 4} \\\\
a                                                                          &= \dfrac {4 \times 3 \times 160} {3 \times 4} \\\\
a                                                                          &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times \cancel 3 \times 160} {\cancel 3 \times \cancel 4} \\\\
a                                                                          &= 160 \ \text{ml}
\end{aligned}
$

Quantity of water it hold at one quarter full $= 160 \ \text{ml}$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{fraction}                                   &:& \text{volume} \\\\
= & \left( 1 - \dfrac {1} {4} \right)                 &:& 480 \ \text{ml} \\\\
= & \left( \dfrac {4} {4} - \dfrac {1} {4} \right)    &:& 480 \\\\
= & \dfrac {4 - 1} {4}                                &:& 480 \\\\
= & \dfrac {3} {4}                                    &:& 480 \\\\
= & \dfrac {1} {4}                                    &:& \dfrac {480} {3} \\\\
= & \dfrac {1} {4}                                    &:& \dfrac {3 \times 160} {3} \\\\
= & \dfrac {1} {4}                                    &:& \dfrac {\cancel 3 \times 160} {\cancel 3} \\\\
= & \dfrac {1} {4}                                    &:& 160 \ \text{ml}
\end{array}
$

Quantity of water it hold at one quarter full $= 160 \ \text{ml}$
