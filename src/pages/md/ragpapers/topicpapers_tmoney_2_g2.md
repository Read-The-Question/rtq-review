<div class='question question'>

A multipack of $12$ cans of Ned's cola cost $\pounds 6.95$. 

A single can of Ned's cola costs $65 \ \text{pence}$ if bought individually. 

Calculate how much cheaper it is to buy a multipack of $12$ cans than to buy $12$ cans individually, giving your answer in pence.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: It is really important to use the right units in calculations and convert where required
.

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Cost of 12 cans}                 &= 12 \times 65 \\\\
                                        &= 780 \text {p} \\\\
\text {Cheaper by}                      &= 780 \text {p} - \pounds 6.95 \\\\
                                        &= 780 \text {p} - 695 \text {p} \\\\
                                        &= 85 \text {p}
\end {aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$85 \text {p}$

</div>
</div>

A packet of crisps costs $35 \ \text{pence}$ if they are brought individually.

                            &= 525 \text {p} - 390 \text {p} \\\\
                            &= 135 \text {p}
\end {aligned}
$

</div>

$135 \ \text {pence}$

Work out how much money Clare had in total, giving your answer in pounds and pence.

Work out the price of one packet of jelly gum.

                                &= \dfrac {135} {3} \\\\
                                &= \dfrac {45 \times 3} {3} \\\\
                                &= \dfrac {45 \times \cancel{3}} {\cancel{3}} \\\\
                                &= 45 \text {p}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>

<div class='question question'>

Jon has $30\%$ more money than Miguel and together they have $\pounds 46$. Calculate how much money Jon has. 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let Miguel has (in $\pounds$) $= a$

Jon has (in $\pounds$) $= a + 0.3 \times a = 1.3a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
a + 1.3a                        &= 46 \\\\
2.3a                            &= 46 \\\\
a                               &= \dfrac {46} {2.3} \\\\
a                               &= \dfrac {46 \times 10} {23} \\\\
a                               &= \dfrac {23 \times 2 \times 10} {23} \\\\
a                               &= \dfrac {\cancel{23} \times 2 \times 10} {\cancel{23}} \\\\
a                               &= 2 \times 10 \\\\
a                               &= \pounds 20 \\\\
\text {Jon has}                 &= 1.3 \times a \\\\
                                &= 1.3 \times 20 \\\\
                                &= \pounds 26
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$\pounds 26$

</div>
</div>
