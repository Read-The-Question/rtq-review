<div class='question subquestion'>

If he uses $6 \ \text{litres}$ of red paint, how much blue paint should he use? 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the blue paint needed $= a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
2 \ \text{litres} : 3 \ \text{litres}           &= 6 \ \text{litres} : a \ \text{litres} \\\\
\dfrac {2} {3}                                  &= \dfrac {6} {a} \\\\
2 \times a                                      &= 6 \times 3 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {6 \times 3} {2} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {2 \times 3 \times 3} {2} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 2 \times 3 \times 3} {\cancel 2} \\\\
a                                               &= 3 \times 3 \\\\
a                                               &= 9 \ \text{litres}
\end{aligned}
$

Blue paint needed $= 9 \ \text{litres}$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{red paint}                        &:& \text{blue paint} \\\\
= & 2 \ \text{litres}                       &:& 3 \ \text{litres} \\\\
= & 2 \times 3                              &:& 3 \times 3 \\\\
= & 6 \ \text{litres}                       &:& 9 \ \text{litres}
\end{array}
$

Blue paint needed $= 9 \ \text{litres}$

</div>

<div class='question subquestion'>

How much blue paint is needed to make $35 \ \text{litres}$ of purple paint?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let amount of blue paint used $= a$

Amount of red paint used $= 35 - a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
2 \ \text{litres} : 3 \ \text{litres}           &= (35 - a) \ \text{litres} : a \ \text{litres} \\\\
\dfrac{2} {3}                                   &= \dfrac {35 - a} {a} \\\\
2 \times a                                      &= (35 - a) \times 3 \\\\
2a                                              &= 35 \times 3 - 3a \\\\
2a                                              &= 105 - 3a \\\\
2a + 3a                                         &= 105 \\\\
5a                                              &= 105 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {105} {5} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {5 \times 21} {5} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 5 \times 21} {\cancel 5} \\\\
a                                               &= 21 \ \text{litres}
\end{aligned}
$

Blue paint needed $= 21 \ \text{litres}$

= & 5                                       &:& 3 \\\\
= & 5 \times 7                              &:& 3 \times 7 \\\\

Annie and Bradley each think of a number. 

The difference between their numbers is $6$.

The sum of their numbers is $20$.

What are the two numbers?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number Annie thinks $= a$

Let the number Bradley thinks $= b$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned} 
a + b      &= 20 \\\\
a - b      &= 6         
\end{aligned}
$

There are two variables, $a$ and $b$. Let's eliminate to reduce it to just one variable.

Subtract the second equation from the first to reduce it to one variable.

$
\begin{aligned}
a + b - (a - b)     &= 20 - 6 \\\\
a + b -a + b        &= 14 \\\\
2b                  &= 14 \\\\
b                   &= \dfrac {14} {2} \\\\
b                   &= \dfrac {7 \times 2} {2} \\\\
b                   &= \dfrac {7 \times \cancel 2} {\cancel 2} \\\\
b                   &= 7
\end{aligned}
$

Let's substitute the value of $b$ in one of the initial equations.

$
\begin{aligned} 
a + b                          &= 20 \\\\
a + 7                          &= 20 \\\\
a                              &= 20 - 7 \\\\
a                              &= 13
\end{aligned}
$

Annie's number $= 13$

Bradley's number $= 7$

</div>
