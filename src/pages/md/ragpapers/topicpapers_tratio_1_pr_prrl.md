<div class='question subquestion'>

My flask holds $480 \ \text{ml}$ when it is one quarter empty. 

How much does it hold when it is one quarter full?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let quantity of water it hold at one quarter full $= a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
\left( 1 - \dfrac {1} {4} \right) \ \text{fraction} : 480 \ \text{volume}  &= \dfrac {1} {4} \ \text{fraction} : a \ \text{volume} \\\\
\dfrac{\left( 1 - \dfrac {1} {4} \right)} {480}                            &= \dfrac {\dfrac {1} {4}} {a} \\\\
\dfrac{\dfrac {3} {4}} {480}                                               &= \dfrac {\dfrac {1} {4}} {a} \\\\
\dfrac {3} {4 \times 480}                                                  &= \dfrac {1} {4 \times a} \\\\
3 \times 4 \times a                                                        &= 4 \times 480 \\\\
a                                                                          &= \dfrac {4 \times 480} {3 \times 4} \\\\
a                                                                          &= \dfrac {4 \times 3 \times 160} {3 \times 4} \\\\
a                                                                          &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times \cancel 3 \times 160} {\cancel 3 \times \cancel 4} \\\\
a                                                                          &= 160 \ \text{ml}
\end{aligned}
$

Quantity of water it hold at one quarter full $= 160 \ \text{ml}$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{fraction}                                   &:& \text{volume} \\\\
= & \left( 1 - \dfrac {1} {4} \right)                 &:& 480 \ \text{ml} \\\\
= & \left( \dfrac {4} {4} - \dfrac {1} {4} \right)    &:& 480 \\\\
= & \dfrac {4 - 1} {4}                                &:& 480 \\\\
= & \dfrac {3} {4}                                    &:& 480 \\\\
= & \dfrac {1} {4}                                    &:& \dfrac {480} {3} \\\\
= & \dfrac {1} {4}                                    &:& \dfrac {3 \times 160} {3} \\\\
= & \dfrac {1} {4}                                    &:& \dfrac {\cancel 3 \times 160} {\cancel 3} \\\\
= & \dfrac {1} {4}                                    &:& 160 \ \text{ml}
\end{array}
$

Quantity of water it hold at one quarter full $= 160 \ \text{ml}$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$160 \ \text{ml}$

$4$ oranges and $7$ apples weigh $51 \ \text{ounces}$.
$7$ oranges and $4$ apples weigh $48 \ \text{ounces}$.

What do $1$ apple and $1$ orange weigh?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the weight of $1$ apple $= a \ \text{ounces}$

Let the weight of $1$ orange $= b \ \text{ounces}$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
4b + 7a           &= 51 \\\\
7b + 4a           &= 48
\end{aligned}
$

Let's add both the equations.

$
\begin{aligned}
4b + 7a + 7b + 4a           &= 51 + 48 \\\\
7a + 4a + 4b + 7b           &= 99 \\\\
11a + 11b                   &= 99 \\\\
11 \times ( a + b )         &= 99 \\\\
a + b                       &= \dfrac {99} {11} \\\\
a + b                       &= \dfrac {11 \times 9} {11} \\\\
a + b                       &= \dfrac {\cancel {11} \times 9} {\cancel {11}} \\\\
a + b                       &= 9
\end{aligned}
$

Weight of $1$ apple and $1$ orange $= 9 \ \text{ounces}$.

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$9 \ \text{ounces}$

Janice is running a bath. She  mixes hot water with cold water to make sure the temperature is just right.

She uses $5$ times more hot water than cold water. Janice's bath holds $90 \ \text{litres}$ of water in total. 
How much hot water does she use?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the volume of hot water $= a \ \text{litres}$

Volume of cold water $= (90 - a) \ \text{litres}$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
5 \ \text{litres} : 1 \ \text{litres}           &= a \ \text{litres} : (90 - a) \ \text{litres} \\\\
\dfrac {5} {1}                                  &= \dfrac {a} {90 - a} \\\\
5 \times (90 - a)                               &= 1 \times a \\\\
450 - 5a                                        &= a \\\\
450                                             &= a + 5a \\\\
a + 5a                                          &= 450 \\\\
6a                                              &= 450 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {450} {6} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {6 \times 75} {6} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 6 \times 75} {\cancel 6} \\\\
a                                               &= 75 \ \text{litres}
\end{aligned}
$

Hot water used $= 75 \ \text{litres}$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using whole to part ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{total water}                        &:& \text{hot water} \\\\
= & 5 + 1                                     &:& 5  \\\\
= & 6 \ \text{litres}                         &:& 5 \ \text{litres} \\\\
= & 6 \times 15                               &:& 5 \times 15 \\\\
= & 90 \ \text{litres}                        &:& 75 \ \text{litres}
\end{array}
$

Hot water used $= 75 \ \text{litres}$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$75 \ \text{litres}$

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the amount of kroner I get $= a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
100 \ \text{pence} : 18 \ \text{kroner}         &= 90 \ \text{pence} : a \ \text{kroner} \\\\
\dfrac{100} {18}                                &= \dfrac {90} {a} \\\\
100a                                            &= 90 \times 18 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {90 \times 18} {100} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {1620} {100} \\\\
a                                               &= 16.2 \ \text{kroner}
\end{aligned}
$

Answer $= 16.2 \ \text{kroner}$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{pence}                   &:& \text{kroner} \\\\
= & 100                            &:& 18 \\\\
= & 1                              &:& \dfrac {18} {100} \\\\
= & 1 \times 90                    &:& \dfrac {18 \times 90} {100} \\\\
= & 90                             &:& \dfrac {1620} {100} \\\\
= & 90 \text{pence}                &:& 16.2 \ \text{kroner}
\end{array}
$

Answer $= 16.2 \ \text{kroner}$

Jo buys some sweets and a magazine that cost $\pounds 5.10$. 
How much is this in kroner?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the value in kroner $= a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
\pounds 1 : 18 \ \text{kroner}                  &= \pounds 5.1 : a \ \text{kroner} \\\\
\dfrac{1} {18}                                  &= \dfrac {5.1} {a} \\\\
a                                               &= 5.1 \times 18 \\\\
a                                               &= 91.8 \ \text{kroner}
\end{aligned}
$

Answer $= 91.8 \ \text{kroner}$

</div>
<div class='working'>

= & \pounds 5.10                   &:& 91.8 \ \text{kroner}
\end{array}
$

Answer $= 91.8 \ \text{kroner}$
