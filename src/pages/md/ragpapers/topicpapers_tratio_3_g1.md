Eleven pencils cost $\pounds 4.95$. How much would seven pencils cost?

- $\pounds 3.50$
- $\pounds 3.20$
- $\pounds 4.00$
- $\pounds 3.15$
- $\pounds 4.15$ 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cost of 1 pencil}                       &= \dfrac {4.95} {11} \\\\
\text{Cost of 7 pencils}                      &= 7 \times \dfrac {4.95} {11} \\\\
                                              &= \dfrac {7 \times 4.95} {11} \\\\
                                              &= \dfrac {7 \times 495} {11 \times 100} \\\\
                                              &= \dfrac {7 \times 11 \times 45} {11 \times 100} \\\\
                                              &= \dfrac {7 \times \cancel {11} \times 45} {\cancel {11} \times 100} \\\\
                                              &= \dfrac {315} {100} \\\\
                                              &= \pounds 3.15
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Let the cost of seven pencils $= a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
11 \ \text{pencils} : \pounds 4.95              &= 7 \ \text{pencils} : \pounds a  \\\\
\dfrac {11} {4.95}                              &= \dfrac {7} {a} \\\\
11 \times a                                     &= 7 \times 4.95 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {7 \times 4.95} {11} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {7 \times 495} {11 \times 100} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {7 \times 11 \times 45} {11 \times 100} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {7 \times \cancel {11} \times 45} {\cancel {11} \times 100} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {315} {100} \\\\
a                                               &= \pounds 3.15
\end{aligned}
$

Cost of $7$ pencils $= \pounds 3.15$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{pencils}                &:& \text{cost} \\\\
= & 11                            &:& \pounds 4.95 \\\\
= & 1                             &:& \dfrac{4.95} {11} \\\\
= & 1 \times 7                    &:& \dfrac{4.95 \times 7} {11} \\\\
= & 7                             &:& \dfrac{495 \times 7} {11 \times 100} \\\\
= & 7                             &:& \dfrac{11 \times 45 \times 7} {11 \times 100} \\\\
= & 7                             &:& \dfrac{\cancel {11} \times 45 \times 7} {\cancel {11} \times 100} \\\\
= & 7                             &:& \dfrac{315} {100} \\\\
= & 7                             &:& \pounds 3.15
\end{array}
$

Cost of $7$ pencils $= \pounds 3.15$

</div>

<div class='question question'>

If $750 \ \text{grams}$ of pasta cost $90 \text{p}$ find the cost of $1 \ \text{kilogram}$.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: It is really important to use the right units in calculations and convert where required.


$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Cost of 1 gram pasta}         &= \dfrac {90} {750} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {9 \cancel 0} {75 \cancel 0} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {9} {75} \text{p} \\\\
\text{Cost of 1000 gram pasta}      &= \dfrac {9} {75} \times 1000 \\\\
\text{Cost of 1 kilogram pasta}     &= \dfrac {9 \times 1000} {75} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {3 \times 3 \times 25 \times 40} {25 \times 3} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {3 \times \cancel 3 \times \cancel {25} \times 40} {\cancel {25} \times \cancel 3} \\\\
                                    &= 3 \times 40 \\\\
                                    &= 120 \text{p} \\\\
                                    &= \pounds 1.20
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Let price of pasta $= a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
750 \ \text{grams} : 90 \ \text{pence}          &= 1000 \ \text{grams} : a \ \text{pence} \\\\
\dfrac{750} {90}                                &= \dfrac {1000} {a} \\\\
750 \times a                                    &= 90 \times 1000 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {90 \times 1000} {750} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {3 \times 30 \times 250 \times 4} {3 \times 250} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 3 \times 30 \times \cancel {250} \times 4} {\cancel 3 \times \cancel {250}} \\\\
a                                               &= 30 \times 4 \\\\
a                                               &= 120 \ \text{pence} \\\\
a                                               &= \pounds 1.20
\end{aligned}
$

Price of $1 \ \text{kilogram}$ of pasta $= 120 \text{p}$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{pasta}                                  &:& \text{cost} \\\\
= & 750 \ \text{g}                                &:& 90 \text{p} \\\\
= & 1                                             &:& \dfrac {90} {750} \\\\
= & 1 \times 1000 \ \text{g}                      &:& \dfrac {90 \times 1000} {750} \\\\
= & 1 \ \text{kg}                                 &:& \dfrac {3 \times 30 \times 250 \times 4} {3 \times 250} \\\\
= & 1                                             &:& \dfrac {\cancel 3 \times 30 \times \cancel {250} \times 4} {\cancel 3 \times \cancel {250}} \\\\
= & 1                                             &:& 30 \times 4 \\\\
= & 1                                             &:& 120 \text{p} \\\\
= & 1 \ \text{kg}                                 &:& \pounds 1.20
\end{array}
$

Price of $1 \ \text{kilogram}$ of pasta $= \pounds 1.20$

</div>

$
\begin{aligned}

                          &= \dfrac {18 \times 2.8} {12} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {18 \times 28} {12 \times 10} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {6 \times 3 \times 7 \times 4} {3 \times 4 \times 10} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {6 \times \cancel 3 \times 7 \times \cancel 4} {\cancel 3 \times \cancel 4 \times 10} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {6 \times 7} {10} \\\\
                          &= \dfrac {42} {10} \\\\
                          &= \pounds 4.2
\end{aligned}
$

</div>

$
\begin{aligned}
12 \ \text{eggs} : \pounds 2.8                  &= 18 \ \text{eggs} : \pounds a \\\\
\dfrac {12} {2.8}                                &= \dfrac {18} {a} \\\\
12a                                             &= 18 \times 2.8 \\\\

a                                               &= \dfrac {3 \times 14} {10} \\\\

Five glass marbles cost a total of $\pounds 7.50$. How many marbles could I buy with $\pounds 24$?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of marbles in 1 pound}   &= \dfrac {5} {7.5} \\\\
\text{Number of marbles in 24 pounds} &= 24 \times \dfrac {5} {7.5} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {24 \times 5} {7.5} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {24 \times 5 \times 10} {75} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {3 \times 8 \times 5 \times 5 \times 2} {5 \times 5 \times 3} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {\cancel 3 \times 8 \times \cancel 5 \times \cancel 5 \times 2} {\cancel 5 \times \cancel 5 \times \cancel 3} \\\\
                                      &= 8 \times 2 \\\\
                                      &= 16
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Let the number of marbles $= a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
\pounds 7.5 : 5 \ \text{marbles}                &= \pounds 24 : a \ \text{marbles} \\\\
\dfrac {7.5} {5}                                &= \dfrac {24} {a} \\\\
7.5 \times a                                    &= 24 \times 5 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {24 \times 5} {7.5} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {24 \times 5 \times 10} {75} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {120 \times 50 \times 2} {50} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {3 \times 8 \times 5 \times 5 \times 2} {5 \times 5 \times 3} \\\\
a                                               &= 8 \times 2 \\\\
a                                               &= 16
\end{aligned}
$

Number of marbles $= 16$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{cost}                             &:& \text{marbles} \\\\
= & \pounds 7.5                             &:& 5 \\\\
= & 1                                       &:& \dfrac {5} {7.5} \\\\
= & 1 \times 24                             &:& \dfrac {24 \times 5} {7.5} \\\\
= & 24                                      &:& \dfrac {24 \times 5 \times 10} {75} \\\\
= & 24                                      &:& \dfrac {8 \times 3 \times 5 \times 5 \times 2} {3 \times 5 \times 5} \\\\
= & 24                                      &:& \dfrac {8 \times \cancel 3 \times \cancel 5 \times \cancel 5 \times 2} {\cancel 3 \times \cancel 5 \times \cancel 5} \\\\
= & 24                                      &:& 8 \times 2 \\\\
= & \pounds 24                              &:& 16
\end{array}
$

Number of marbles $= 16$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$16$

</div>

Disc $B$ turns twice when $A$ turns $5$ times. If disc $A$ turns $45$ times, how many times does disc $B$ turn?

![the-london-independent-girls-schools-consortium--11-plus--maths--2009--group-2/section-1-question-14-00.png](/assets/the-london-independent-girls-schools-consortium--11-plus--maths--2009--group-2/section-1-question-14-00.png "the-london-independent-girls-schools-consortium--11-plus--maths--2009--group-2/section-1-question-14-00.png")

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number of times disc B turned $= a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
5 \ \text{disc A} : 2 \ \text{disc B}           &= 45 \ \text{disc A} : a \ \text{disc B} \\\\
\dfrac{5} {2}                                   &= \dfrac {45} {a} \\\\
5a                                              &= 45 \times 2 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {45 \times 2} {5} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {9 \times 5 \times 2} {5} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {9 \times \cancel 5 \times 2} {\cancel 5} \\\\
a                                               &= 9 \times 2 \\\\
a                                               &= 18
\end{aligned}
$

Number of times disc B turned $= 18$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{disc A}                         &:& \text{disc B} \\\\
= & 5                                     &:& 2 \\\\
= & 1                                     &:& \dfrac{2} {5} \\\\
= & 1 \times 45                           &:& \dfrac{2 \times 45} {5} \\\\
= & 45                                    &:& \dfrac{2 \times 5 \times 9} {5} \\\\
= & 45                                    &:& \dfrac{2 \times \cancel 5 \times 9} {\cancel 5} \\\\
= & 45                                    &:& 2 \times 9 \\\\
= & 45                                    &:& 18
\end{array}
$

Number of times disc B turned $= 18$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$18$

Mr Cooper’s car uses $3 \ \text{litres}$ of petrol to travel $50 \ \text{kilometres}$.

Petrol costs $\pounds 1.45$ per litre.

How much will Mr Cooper spend on petrol to drive $600 \ \text{kilometres}$ from Oxford to Glasgow?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Petrol needed for 1 km}      &= \dfrac {3} {50} \\\\
\text {Petrol needed for 600 km}    &= \dfrac {3} {50} \times 600 \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {3 \times 600} {50} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {3 \times 50 \times 12} {50} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {3 \times \cancel {50} \times 12} {\cancel {50}} \\\\
                                    &= 3 \times 12 \\\\
                                    &= 36 \ \text{litres} \\\\
\text {Cost of the petrol}          &= 1.45 \times 36 \\\\
                                    &= \pounds 52.2
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{km}                                     &:& \text{litres} \\\\
= & 50                                            &:& 3 \\\\
= & 1                                             &:& \dfrac {3} {50} \\\\
= & 1 \times 600                                  &:& \dfrac {3 \times 600} {50} \\\\
= & 600                                           &:& \dfrac {3 \times 50 \times 12} {50} \\\\
= & 600                                           &:& \dfrac {3 \times \cancel {50} \times 12} {\cancel {50}} \\\\
= & 600                                           &:& 3 \times 12 \\\\
= & 600 \ \text{km}                               &:& 36 \ \text{litres}
\end{array}
$

<br>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Petrol needed}       &= 36 \ \text{litres} \\\\
\text {Cost of the petrol}  &= 1.45 \times 36 \\\\
                            &= \pounds 52.2
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$\pounds 52.2$

                                  &= \dfrac {250} {2 + 0.5} \\\\
                                  &= \dfrac {250} {2.5} \\\\
                                  &= \dfrac {250 \times 10} {25} \\\\
                                  &= \dfrac {25 \times 10 \times 10} {25} \\\\
                                  &= \dfrac {\cancel {25} \times 10 \times 10} {\cancel {25}} \\\\
                                  &= 100
\end{aligned}
$

</div>

<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$100 \ \text {bags}$
