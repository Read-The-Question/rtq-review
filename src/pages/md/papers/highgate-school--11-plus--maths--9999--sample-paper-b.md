                                                &= \dfrac {36} {4} \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {4 \times 9} {4} \\\\

A rectangle is split into two smaller rectangles, labelled $A$ and $B$. Rectangle $A$ is shaded. One seventh of the original rectangle

![highgate-school--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-b/section-1-question-2-subquestion-4-00.png](/assets/highgate-school--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-b/section-1-question-2-subquestion-4-00.png "highgate-school--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-b/section-1-question-2-subquestion-4-00.png")

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let area of rectangle A $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}

                               &= \dfrac {2 \times 7a} {3} \\\\
                               &= \dfrac {14a} {3}
\end {aligned}
$

$
\begin {aligned}
\text{Fraction of the remaining shaded shape} &= \dfrac {a} {a + \dfrac {14a} {3}} \\\\
                                              &= \dfrac {a} {\dfrac {a \times 3} {3} + \dfrac {14a} {3}} \\\\
                                              &= \dfrac {a} {\dfrac {3a} {3} + \dfrac {14a} {3}} \\\\
                                              &= \dfrac {a} { \dfrac {3a + 14a} {3}} \\\\
                                              &= \dfrac {a} {\dfrac {17a} {3}} \\\\
                                              &= \dfrac {a \times 3} {17a} \\\\
                                              &= \dfrac {\cancel a \times 3} {17 \times \cancel a} \\\\
                                              &= \dfrac {3} {17}                    
\end {aligned}
$

</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$\dfrac {3} {17} $

a &= 180
\end {aligned}

\begin {aligned}
\text{Number of green sweets} &= 600 - a - a - 8 \\\\
                              &= 592 - 2a \\\\
                              &= 592 - 2 \times 180 \\\\
                              &= 592 - 360 \\\\
                              &=232
\end {aligned}

Jack and Jill are buying chocolates in a Chocolate Shop.

Jack bought $6$ chocolate frogs and $5$ truffles for $\pounds 9.55$.

Jill bought $3$ chocolate frogs and $3$ truffles for $\pounds 5.25$.

How much does a chocolate frog cost, and how much does a truffle cost?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let a chocolate frog cost $= a$

Let a truffle cost $= b$

As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
6a + 5b &= 9.55 \\\\
3a + 3b &= 5.25 
\end {aligned}
$

We have got 2 variables and we have 2 equations to solve them .Let's use elimination to reduce it to just one variable. 

Multiply second equation by 2.

$
\begin{aligned}
3a \times 2 + 3b \times 2   &= 5.25 \times 2 \\\\
6a + 6b                     &= 10.50
\end{aligned}
$

Subtract first equation from the above equations.

$
\begin{aligned} 
6a + 6b - 6a -5b         &= 10.50 - 9.55 \\\\
b                        &= 0.95 
\end{aligned}
$

Substituting the value of $b$ in second equation.

$
\begin{aligned}
3a + 3 \times 0.95 &= 5.25 \\\\
3a                 &= 5.25 - 2.85 \\\\
3a                 &= 2.4 \\\\
a                  &= \dfrac{2.4}{3} \\\\
a                  &= \dfrac{0.8 \times 3}{3} \\\\
a                  &= \dfrac{0.8 \times \cancel 3}{\cancel 3} \\\\
a                  &= \pounds 0.8
\end{aligned}
$

Cost of a chocolate frog $= \pounds 0.8$

Cost of a truffle $= \pounds 0.95$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

Chocolate frog $=$ $ \pounds 0.8$

An artist mixes red, yellow and white paint to create a new shade of orange.

Two fifths of the mixture is yellow paint.

The artist uses three times as much red paint as white paint.

In total, the artist mixes $180 \ \text{ml}$ orange paint.

How much red paint does the artist need?

</div>

<div class='working'>

Let white paint used $= a$

Red paint used $= 3a $

As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
180 - 3a - a &= \dfrac{2} {5} \ \text{of} \ 180 \\\\
180 - 4a     &= \dfrac{2} {5} \times 180 \\\\
180 - 4a     &= \dfrac{2  \times 180 } {5} \\\\
180 - 4a     &= \dfrac{2  \times 36 \times 5} {5} \\\\
180 - 4a     &= \dfrac{2  \times 36 \times \cancel 5} {\cancel 5} \\\\
180 - 4a     &= 2  \times 36  \\\\
180 - 4a     &= 72  \\\\
4a           &= 180 - 72  \\\\
4a           &= 108  \\\\
a            &= \dfrac {108} {4}  \\\\
a            &= \dfrac {4 \times 27} {4}  \\\\
a            &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times 27} {\cancel 4}  \\\\
a            &= 27 \ \text{ml}
\end {aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Red paint used} &= 3a \\\\
                      &= 3 \times 27 \\\\
                      &= 81 \ \text{ml}
\end{aligned}
$



</div>
</div>

<div class='answer'>
