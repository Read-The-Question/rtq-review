                    &= \dfrac {5} {10} - \dfrac {10} {5} \\\\

                    &= \dfrac {5 - 20} {10} \\\\
                    &= - \dfrac {15} {10} \\\\
                    &= - \dfrac {5 \times 3} {5 \times 2} \\\\
                    &= - \dfrac {\cancel{5} \times 3} {\cancel{5} \times 2} \\\\
                    &= - \dfrac {3} {2}
\end {aligned}

Let first label $= + a$

\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
8b + ab &= 10 \\\\
8b + (4 - 5b) &= 10 \\\\

3b &= 6 \\\\

\end{aligned}

The rule $= - 3 \ \text{then}\ \times 2$

Three hedgehogs, Rana, Sarah and Tina, are collecting leaves.

Rana collects twice as many as Sarah.

Sarah collects one and a half times as many as Tina.

Between them they collect $198$ leaves.


How many leaves did each hedgehog collect?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the leaves collected by Sarah $= a$

Let the leaves collected by Rana $= 2a$

Let the leaves collected by Tina $= \dfrac {2a}{3} $

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
a + 2a + \dfrac {2a} {3}                                             &= 198 \\\\
3a + \dfrac {2a} {3}                                                 &= 198 \\\\
\dfrac {3a \times 3}{1 \times 3} + \dfrac {2a} {3}                   &= 198 \\\\
\dfrac {9a + 2a} {3}                                                 &= 198 \\\\
\dfrac {11a} {3}                                                     &= 198 \\\\
a                                                                    &= \dfrac {198 \times 3} {11} \\\\\
a                                                                    &= \dfrac {18 \times 11 \times 3} {11} \\\\
a                                                                    &= \dfrac {18 \times \cancel {11} \times 3} {\cancel {11}} \\\\
a                                                                    &= 18 \times 3 \\\\
a                                                                    &= 54
\end{aligned}
$

\begin{aligned}
\text {Leaves collected by Sarah}     &= 54 \\\\

                                      &= \dfrac {2 \times 18 \times \cancel 3} {\cancel 3} \\\\
                                      &= 18 \times 2 \\\\
                                      &= 36
\end{aligned}

In the same time that Tom can run $100 \ \text{metres}$, Jerry can run only $60 \ \text{metres}$.

How many metres will Tom run before he catches Jerry?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the distance covered by Tom $= a$

Distance covered by Jerry $= a - 100$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
100 \ \text{m} : 60 \ \text{m}                  &= a \ \text{m} : (a - 100) \ \text{m} \\\\
\dfrac{100} {60}                                &= \dfrac {a} {a - 100} \\\\
100 \times (a - 100)                            &= a \times 60 \\\\
100a - 10000                                    &= 60a \\\\
100a - 60a                                      &= 10000 \\\\
40a                                             &= 10000 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {10000} {40} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {40 \times 250} {40} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel {40} \times 250} {\cancel {40}} \\\\
a                                               &= 250 \ \text{m}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>

<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$250 \ \text{m}$
