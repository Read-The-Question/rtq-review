<div class='question question'>

A box of biscuits contains $36$ biscuits. $20$ biscuits have foil wrappers. $15$ are chocolate biscuits with foil wrappers. 
If $9$ are not chocolate and do not have a foil wrapper, then how many chocolate biscuits are there?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Number of chocolate biscuits without foil wrappers}  &= 36 - 20 - 9 \\\\
                                                            &= 16 - 9 \\\\
                                                            &= 7
\end {aligned}
$

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Total chocolate biscuits}    &= 15 + 7 \\\\
                                    &= 22
\end {aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>

<div class='question question'>

$75$ students need $3$ pens each. Pens are sold in packs of $6$. How many whole packs need to be bought?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Total packs needed}       &= \dfrac{\text{Total pens required}}{\text{Pens per pack}} \\\\
                                &= \dfrac{75 \times 3}{6} \\\\
                                &= \dfrac{75 \times 3}{2 \times 3} \\\\
                                &= \dfrac{75 \times \cancel{3}}{2 \times \cancel{3}} \\\\
                                &= \dfrac{75}{2} \\\\
                                &= 37 \ \text {remainder} \ 1 \\\\
                                &\approx 38
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$38$

</div>

- $38$

- $51$
- $45$
- $49$

$6$ matches increase $1$ on next one

\begin{aligned}

&= 1 + 6 \times 2 \\\\
&= 1 + 12 \\\\

&= \dfrac{98}{2} \\\\
&= \dfrac{49 \times 2}{2} \\\\

\end{aligned}

<div class='question question'>

If $161$ sweets are shared equally between $7$ people, how many sweets does each person receive?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Each person get}      & = \dfrac {161} {7} \\\\
                                        & = \dfrac {7 \times 23}{7} \\\\
                                        & = \dfrac {\cancel 7 \times 23} {\cancel 7} \\\\
                                        & = 23
\end{aligned}
$

The same thing happens if this rope is cut into $6$ pieces. What is the shortest length that this rope could be?

Multiple of $6 = 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, \ldots$

The LCM of $8$ and $6$ is the smallest number that is a multiple of both numbers $=24$.

The shortest length that this rope could be $= 24 \ \text{cm}$

</div>
</div>
