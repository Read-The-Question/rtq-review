Marc's mother is $\bold{168\ \text{cm}}$ tall. His father is $\bold{194\ \text{cm}}$ tall.

What is the **greatest** height Marc is likely to be when he grows up? 

Show your working.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Max height of Marc}           &= \dfrac {1} {2} \times (168 + 194) + 7 + 10 \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {168 + 194} {2} + 17  \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {362} {2} + 17 \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {2 \times 181} {2} + 17 \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {\cancel 2 \times 181} {\cancel 2} + 17 \\\\
                                    &= 181 + 17 \\\\
                                    &= 198 \ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>

<div class='answer'>

$191 \ \text{cm}$

Andrew went on a cycling holiday.

The table shows how far he cycled each day. 

|       Monday      |      Tuesday      |     Wednesday     |      Thursday     |
|:-----------------:|:-----------------:|:-----------------:|:-----------------:|
| $32.3\ \text{km}$ | $38.7\ \text{km}$ | $43.5\ \text{km}$ | $45.1\ \text{km}$ |

He claimed; 'On average, I cycled over $\bold{40 \ \text{km}}$ a day'. 

Show that Andrew is wrong.

</div>

<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Mean} = \dfrac {\text{Total of items}} {\text{Number of items}}$


$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Average distance}         &= \dfrac {32.3 + 38.7 + 43.5 + 45.1} {4} \\\\
                                &= \dfrac {159.6} {4} \\\\
                                &= \red{39.9 \ \text{km}} \\\\
                                &< 40 \ \text{km}
\end{aligned}
$

Average distance covered is less than $40 \ \text{km}$.

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$39.9 \ \text{km}$

Which two numbers have a **mean** of $\bold{10}$ and a **range** of $\bold{8}$?

ABBR: $\text{Range} = \text{Highest number} - \text{Lowest number}$


Let the smaller number $= a$

Second number $= a + 8$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}

2a                      &= 12 \\\\
a                       &= \dfrac {12} {2} \\\\
a                       &= \dfrac {2 \times 6} {2} \\\\
a                       &= \dfrac {\cancel 2 \times 6} {\cancel 2} \\\\
a                       &= 6 \\\\
\text{Second number}    &= a + 8 \\\\
                        &= 6 + 8 \\\\
                        &= 14
\end{aligned}
$

Numbers $= 6, 14$

\text {Average speed}   &= \dfrac {2} {\dfrac {1} {60} + \dfrac {1} {30}} \\\\
                        &= \dfrac {2} {\dfrac {1} {60} + \dfrac {2} {30 \times 2}} \\\\
                        &= \dfrac {2} {\dfrac {1} {60} + \dfrac {2} {60}} \\\\

                        &= \dfrac {2 \times 60} {3} \\\\
                        &= \dfrac {2 \times 20 \times 3} {3} \\\\
                        &= \dfrac {2 \times 20 \times \cancel{3}} {\cancel{3}} \\\\
                        &= 40 \ \text {m.p.h}
\end{aligned}
$

$ \approx$ means approximately equal to. Given that $1\ \text{kilogram (kg)}$  $ \approx$ 2·2\ \text{pounds (lb)}$, convert $154\ \text{lb}$ to $\text{kg}$.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
&= 154 \ \text{lb} \\\\
&= 154 \times \dfrac{1}{2.2} \ \text{kg} \\\\
&= \dfrac{154}{2.2} \\\\
&= \dfrac{154 \times 10}{2.2 \times 10} \\\\
&= \dfrac{154 \times 10}{22} \\\\
&= \dfrac{7 \times 22 \times 10}{22} \\\\
&= \dfrac{7 \times \cancel{22} \times 10}{\cancel{22}} \\\\
&= 7 \times 10  \\\\
&= 70 \ \text{kg}
\end{aligned} 
$

</div>

$70 \ \text{kg}$
