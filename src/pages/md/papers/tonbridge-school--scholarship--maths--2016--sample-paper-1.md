<div class='question question'>

I am thinking of two numbers. 

If I double the first and add the second, my answer is $35$. 

If I double the second and subtract the first, my answer is $15$.

By forming and solving two simultaneous equations, find the two numbers.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the first number $= a$

Let the second number $= b$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
2a + b &= 35 \\\\
2b - a &= 15
\end{aligned}
$

First, let's find a

$
\begin{aligned}
2b - a          &= 15 \\\\
2(35 - 2a) - a  &= 15 \\\\
70 - 4a - a    &= 15 \\\\
70 - 5a        &= 15 \\\\
70 -15          &= 5a \\\\
5a             &= 55 \\\\
a               &= \dfrac {55} {5} \\\\
a               &= 11
\end{aligned}
$

Now, let's find b

$
\begin{aligned}
2a + b &= 35 \\\\
2 \times 11 + b &= 35 \\\\
22 + b &= 35 \\\\
b &= 35 - 22 \\\\
b &= 13
\end{aligned}
$

<div class='question question'>

For a certain journey, I always start at the same time in the morning and arrive later in the day.

If I travel at $10\, km$ per hour, I arrive at 1.00 p.m.

If I travel at $15\, km$ per hour, I arrive at 11.00 a.m. 

</div>




<ul class='subquestion lower-alpha'>

<div class='question_envelope rag_not_found rag_not_found subquestion'>
<div class='question subquestion'>

How far is the journey?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text {Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time}$


Let the time taken in first case (in hours)  $= t$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
15 \times (t - 2)   &=  10 \times t \\\\
15t - 30            &=  10t \\\\
15t - 10t           &=  30 \\\\
5t                  &=  30 \\\\
t                   &=  \dfrac{30}{5} \\\\
t                   &=  \dfrac{6 \times 5}{5} \\\\
t                   &=  \dfrac{6 \times \cancel{5}}{\cancel{5}} \\\\
t                   &=  6 \text{ hours} \\\\
\text{Distance}     &= 10 \times t \\\\
                    &= 10 \times 6 \\\\
                    &= 60 \text{ km}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$60 \text{ km}$

</div>

<div class='question subquestion'>

Find the area of the region between the circles (labelled R above).

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Area of square} = \text{side} \times \text{side}$


ABBR: $\text{Area of circle} = \text{3.14} \times \text{radius} \times \text{radius}$


$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Side of square} &= 2 \times 6 \\\\
                      &= 12 \ \text{cm} \\\\ 
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Area}  &= \text{Area of square} - \text{Area of 4 quarter circle} \\\\
             &= 12 \times 12 - 4 \times \dfrac{1} {4}\times 6 \times 6 \times 3.14 \\\\
             &= 144 - \cancel 4 \times \dfrac{1} {\cancel 4}\times 6 \times 6 \times 3.14 \\\\
             &= 144 - 36 \times 3.14 \\\\
             &= 30.9 \ \text{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>

$30.9 \ \text{cm}^2$

Find the radius of the largest circle that will fit in the region labelled R.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Diagnol distance of the formed square} &= \sqrt{2} \times \text{side} \\\\
                                             &= \sqrt{2} \times 12 \ \text{cm} \\\\
\text{Distance of R from center of circle}   &= \dfrac {12 \sqrt{2}} {2} \\\\
                                             &= \dfrac {6 \times 2 \times \sqrt{2}} {2} \\\\
                                             &= \dfrac {6 \times \cancel 2 \times \sqrt{2}} {\cancel 2} \\\\
                                             &= 6 \sqrt{2} \ \text{cm}                                          
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Largest radius that could fit}  &= \text{Distance of R from center of circle} - \text{Radius of the circle} \\\\
                                      &= 6 \sqrt{2} - 6  \\\\
                                      &= 6 (\sqrt{2} - 1) \\\\
                                      &= 2.48 \ \text{cm}                                         
\end{aligned}
$
