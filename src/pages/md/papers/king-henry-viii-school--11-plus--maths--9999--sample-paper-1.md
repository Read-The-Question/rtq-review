\pounds 300 &= \dfrac {6 \times 10 \times 4 \times 75} {6 \times 4} \\\\
\pounds 300 &= \dfrac {\cancel {6} \times 10 \times \cancel {4} \times 75} {\cancel {6} \times \cancel {4}} \\\\
\pounds 300 &= 10 \times 75 \\\\
\pounds 300 &= 750 \ \text {dinars}
\end {aligned}

Bill has a bag containing $60$ sweets. He eats a half of the sweets himself and then gives away a third of the sweets that remain. How many sweets does Bill have left?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Sweets left}  &= \text {Total sweets} - (\text {Sweets eaten} + \text {Sweets given away}) \\\\
                     &= 60 - \bigg(\dfrac {1} {2} \ \text {of} \ 60 + \dfrac {1} {3} \ \text {of} \ \bigg(\dfrac {1} {2} \ \text {of} \ 60 \bigg) \bigg) \\\\
                     &= 60 - \bigg(\dfrac {1} {2} \times 60 +\dfrac {1} {3} \times \bigg(\dfrac {1} {2} \times 60 \bigg) \bigg) \\\\
                     &= 60 - \bigg(\dfrac {60} {2} + \dfrac {1} {3} \times \dfrac {60} {2} \bigg) \\\\
                     &= 60 - \bigg(\dfrac {30 \times 2} {2} + \dfrac {10 \times 3 \times 2} {3 \times 2} \bigg) \\\\
                     &= 60 - \bigg(\dfrac {30 \times \cancel 2} {\cancel 2} + \dfrac {10 \times \cancel 3 \times \cancel 2} {\cancel 3 \times \cancel 2} \bigg) \\\\
                     &= 60 - (30 + 10) \\\\
                     &= 60 - 40 \\\\
                     &= 20
\end {aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$20$

Bill is twice as old as his son, who is $50$ years younger than Bill’s father. If the total of their ages is $142$, how old is Bill?

<div class='working'>

Let Bill’s father age $= a$

Bill’s son age $= a - 50$

Bill’s age $= 2 \times (a - 50)$

As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
a + (a - 50) + 2 \times (a - 50)                                         &= 142 \\\\
a + a - 50 + 2a - 100                                                    &= 142 \\\\
4a - 150                                                                 &= 142 \\\\
4a                                                                       &= 142 + 150 \\\\
4a                                                                       &= 292 \\\\
a                                                                        &= \dfrac {292} {4} \\\\
a                                                                        &= \dfrac {73 \times 4} {4} \\\\
a                                                                        &= \dfrac {73 \times \cancel {4}} {\cancel {4}} \\\\
a                                                                        &= 73 \\\\
\text {Bill’s age}                                                       &= 2 \times (a - 50) \\\\
                                                                         &= 2 \times (73 - 50) \\\\
                                                                         &= 2 \times 23 \\\\
                                                                         &= 46
\end {aligned}
$

<div class='answer'>
