Two icebergs, $A$ and $B$, are floating in the ocean.

On $1$ January, iceberg $A$ weighs $4 \ \text{tonnes}$, but loses $25 \ \text{kg}$ every day.

$1000 \ \text{kg} = 1 \ \text{tonne}$

</div>


<ul class='subquestion lower-alpha'>
<li>
<div class='question_envelope rag_not_found rag_not_found subquestion'>
<div class='question subquestion'>

After how many days will iceberg $A$ weigh $3850 \ \text{kg}$?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Lost weight}  &= 4000 - 3850 \\\\
                    &= 150 \text{ kg}
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of days}  &= \dfrac{150}{25} \\\\
                       &= \dfrac{25 \times 6}{25} \\\\
                       &= \dfrac{\cancel{25} \times 6}{\cancel{25}} \\\\
                       &= 6
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$6$

</div>
</div>

</div>

On $1$ January, iceberg $B$ weighs $4500 \ \text{kg}$ and loses $50 \ \text{kg}$ every day.

After how many days will the two icebergs have the same mass?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number of days $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
4000 - 25 \times a  &=  4500 - 50 \times a  \\\\
4000 - 25a          &=  4500 - 50a  \\\\
50a - 25a           &=  4500 - 4000  \\\\
25a                 &=  500  \\\\
a                   &=  \dfrac{500}{25}  \\\\
a                   &=  \dfrac{20 \times \cancel{25}}{\cancel{25}}  \\\\
a                   &=  20
\end{aligned}
$

After $20$ days, the two icebergs will have the same mass.

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$20$

</div>
</div>

The stopping distance for a car is made up of two parts. 

The first is the distance travelled by the car while the driver is reacting to something that makes them want to brake. 
This is called the $\textbf{Thinking Distance}$. 

The second is the distance travelled by the car while the brakes are applied. This is called the $\textbf{Braking Distance}$. 

The $\textbf{Stopping Distance}$ is given by adding these two distances together.

So $\textbf{Stopping Distance} = \textbf{Thinking Distance} + \textbf{Braking Distance}$

The table below shows the $\textbf{Thinking Distance}$, in metres, for various speeds in km per hour.

-->

$\text{Braking Distance} = \dfrac{1}{60} \times \text{speed} \times \text{speed}$ or $= \dfrac{1}{60} \times (\text{speed})^2$

A truck travels $50 \ \text{metres}$ while accelerating at a rate of $4$. How long does this take?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the time (in seconds) $= t$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac {4 \times t^2}{2}                                     &= 50 \\\\
\dfrac {2 \times 2 \times t^2}{2}                            &= 50 \\\\
\dfrac {2 \times \cancel{2} \times t^2}{\cancel{2}}          &= 50 \\\\
2t^2                                                         &= 50 \\\\
t^2                                                          &= \dfrac{50}{2} \\\\
t^2                                                          &= \dfrac{25 \times 2}{2} \\\\
t^2                                                          &= \dfrac{25 \times \cancel{2}}{\cancel{2}} \\\\
t^2                                                          &= 25 \\\\
t                                                            &= \sqrt{25} \\\\
t                                                            &= 5 \text{ seconds}
\end{aligned}
$

Time taken $ = 5$ seconds.

</div>

<div class='answer'>

$5 \ \text {seconds}$
