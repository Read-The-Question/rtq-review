\end{aligned}

<div class='question question'>

Jeff has $8$ pizzas to share between himself and his friends.

If each person is to get $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of a pizza how many friends can he feed?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of persons}    &= \dfrac {\text{Total pizzas}} {\text{Pizza per person}} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {8} { \dfrac {2} {3}} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {8 \times 3} { 2} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {4 \times 2 \times 3} {2} \\\\
                            &= \dfrac {4 \times \cancel{2} \times 3} {\cancel{2}} \\\\
                            &= 4 \times 3 \\\\
                            &= 12 \\\\
\text{Number of friends}    &= 12 - 1 \\\\
                            &= 11
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Let the number of persons $= a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{2}{3} \text{ pizzas} : 1 \text{ people}  &= 8 \text{ pizzas} : a \text{ persons} \\\\
\dfrac{2}{3 \times 1}                           &= \dfrac {8} {a} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {8 \times 3} { 2} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {4 \times 2 \times 3} {2} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {4 \times \cancel{2} \times 3} {\cancel{2}} \\\\
a                                               &= 4 \times 3 \\\\
a                                               &= 12
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of persons}    &= 12 \\\\
\text{Number of friends}    &= 12 - 1 \\\\
                            &= 11
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{pizzas}                                                           &:& \text{persons} \\\\
= & \dfrac{2}{3}                                                            &:& 1 \\\\
= & \dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{3}{2}                                        &:& 1 \times \dfrac{3}{2} \\\\
= & 1                                                                       &:& \dfrac{3}{2} \\\\
= & 1 \times 8                                                              &:& \dfrac{3 \times 8}{2} \\\\
= & 8                                                                       &:& \dfrac{3 \times 4 \times 2}{2} \\\\
= & 8                                                                       &:& \dfrac{3 \times 4 \times \cancel{2}}{\cancel{2}} \\\\
= & 8                                                                       &:& 12
\end{array}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of persons}    &= 12 \\\\
\text{Number of friends}    &= 12 - 1 \\\\
                            &= 11
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>

<div class='question question'>

One quarter of the pupils in a class are girls. The rest are boys. What is the ratio of girls to boys?

-  $1:1$
-  $1:2$
-  $1:3$ 
-  $1:4$ 
-  $1:5$

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

If $\dfrac {1} {4}$ of the pupils are girls. Then $1 - \dfrac {1} {4} = \dfrac {3} {4}$ will be boys. Using that, we get

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{girls}                                &:& \text{boys} \\\\
= & \dfrac{1} {4}                               &:& \dfrac {3} {4} \\\\
= & 1                                           &:& \dfrac {3 \times 4} {4} \\\\
= & 1                                           &:& \dfrac {3 \times \cancel 4} {\cancel 4} \\\\
= & 1                                           &:& 3
\end{array}
$

<div class='question question'>

$5$ toffees weigh the same as $6$ maltesers and $3$ toffees.

How many maltesers would weigh the same as $9$ toffees?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let number of maltesers $= a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
(5 - 3) \ \text{toffees} : 6 \ \text{maltesers} &= 9 \ \text{toffees} : a \ \text{maltesers} \\\\
\dfrac{2} {6}                                   &= \dfrac {9} {a} \\\\
2a                                              &= 9 \times 6 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {9 \times 6} {2} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {9 \times 2 \times 3} {2} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {9 \times \cancel 2 \times 3} {\cancel 2} \\\\
a                                               &= 9 \times 3 \\\\
a                                               &= 27
\end{aligned}
$

Number of maltesers $= 27$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{toffees}                    &:& \text{maltesers} \\\\
= & (5 - 3)                           &:& 6 \\\\
= & 2                                 &:& 6 \\\\
= & 1                                 &:& \dfrac {6} {2} \\\\
= & 1 \times 9                        &:& \dfrac {6 \times 9} {2} \\\\
= & 9                                 &:& \dfrac {2 \times 3 \times 9} {2} \\\\
= & 9                                 &:& \dfrac {\cancel 2 \times 3 \times 9} {\cancel 2} \\\\
= & 9                                 &:& 3 \times 9 \\\\
= & 9                                 &:& 27
\end{array}
$

Number of maltesers $= 27$

</div>
</div>
