Here are three squares that overlap.

![missing image](/papers/missing_image.svg)


The non-overlapping parts of the squares have a total area of $90 \ \text{cm}^{2}$.

The areas of overlapping parts are $2 \ \text{cm}^{2}$, 
$3 \ \text{cm}^{2}$ and $4 \ \text{cm}^{2}$.

Work out the area of one of the squares. 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

area of non-overlapping squares $=90 cm^2$

area of non-overlapping of one squares $=90 cm^2 \div 3$

$ 90 \div{3} = 30cm^2$

area of overlapping parts $=2cm^2,3cm^2,4cm^2$

area of one square $= area of non-overlapping of one square + area of 2 overlapping parts$

$
\begin{aligned}
&= 30cm^2 + 2cm^2 +3cm^2 \\\\
&=   35 cm^2
\end{aligned}
$

Four strips of paper are stuck on a table as shown. Each one is a rectangle that is $12 \ \text{cm}$ long and $2 \ \text{cm}$ wide.

![missing image](/papers/missing_image.svg)


What area of the table is covered? 

</div>

$
\begin{aligned}

                                  &= (12 \times 2 \times 4) - (2 \times 2 \times 4) \\\\

<div class='question question'>

A cuboid has faces with areas of $24 \ \text{cm}^{2}, 32 \ \text{cm}^{2}$ and $48 \ \text{cm}^{2}$.

What are the lengths of its edges? 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the length of first edge of cuboid $= l$

Let the length of second edge of cuboid $= w$

Let the length of third edge cuboid $= h$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
l \times w                                      &= 24 \\\\
w                                               &= \dfrac {24} {l} \\\\
w \times h                                      &= 32 \\\\
\dfrac {24} {l} \times h                        &= 32 \\\\
\dfrac {24h} {l}                                &= 32 \\\\
24h                                             &= 32 \times l \\\\
h                                               &= \dfrac {32 \times l} {24} \\\\
l \times h                                      &= 48 \\\\
l \times \dfrac{32 \times l} {24}               &= 48 \\\\
\dfrac{l \times 32 \times l} {24}               &= 48 \\\\
\dfrac{32 \times l^2} {24}                      &= 48 \\\\
32 \times l^2                                   &= 48 \times 24 \\\\
l^2                                             &= \dfrac {48 \times 24} {32} \\\\
l^2                                             &= \dfrac {16 \times 3 \times 2 \times 12} {16 \times 2} \\\\
l^2                                             &= \dfrac {\cancel {16} \times 3 \times \cancel {2} \times 12} {\cancel {16} \times \cancel {2}} \\\\
l^2                                             &= 3 \times 12 \\\\
l^2                                             &= 36 \\\\
l                                               &= \sqrt {36} \\\\
l                                               &= 6 \ \text {cm} \\\\
w                                               &= \dfrac {24} {6} \\\\
w                                               &= \dfrac {6 \times 4} {6} \\\\
w                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel {6} \times 4} {\cancel {6}} \\\\
w                                               &= 4 \ \text {cm} \\\\
l \times h                                      &= 48 \\\\
6 \times h                                      &= 48 \\\\
h                                               &= \dfrac {48} {6} \\\\
h                                               &= \dfrac {6 \times 8} {6} \\\\
h                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel {6} \times 8} {\cancel {6}} \\\\
h                                               &= 8 \ \text {cm}
\end{aligned}
$

Length of the edges of the cuboid are $6 \ \text {cm}, \ 4 \ \text {cm}, \ 8 \ \text {cm}$.

</div>
</div>

<div class='answer'>

$6 \ \text {cm}, \ 4 \ \text {cm}, \ 8 \ \text {cm}$

</div>

Two identical square holes are cut from a rectangular sheet leaving an 
area of $58$ square centimetres. How long are the sides of the square?

![missing image](/papers/missing_image.svg)

2 \times a ^ 2                  &= 90 - 58 \\\\
2 \times a ^ 2                  &= 32 \\\\
a ^ 2                           &= \dfrac {32} {2} \\\\
a ^ 2                           &= \dfrac {16 \times 2} {2} \\\\
a ^ 2                           &= \dfrac {16 \times \cancel {2}} {\cancel {2}} \\\\
a                               &= \sqrt 16 \\\\
a                               &= 4 \ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$
