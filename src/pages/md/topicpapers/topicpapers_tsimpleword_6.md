A box of biscuits contains $36$ biscuits. $20$ biscuits have foil wrappers. $15$ are chocolate biscuits with foil wrappers. 
If $9$ are not chocolate and do not have a foil wrapper, then how many chocolate biscuits are there?

\text {Number of chocolate biscuits without foil wrappers}  &= 36 - 20 - 9 \\\\
                                                            &= 16 - 9 \\\\
                                                            &= 7 \\\\
\text {Total chocolate biscuits}                            &= 15 + 7 \\\\
                                                            &= 22
\end {aligned}
$

$75$ students need $3$ pens each. Pens are sold in packs of $6$. How many whole packs need to be bought?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

                                &= \dfrac{75 \times 3}{6} \\\\
                                &= \dfrac{75 \times 3}{2 \times 3} \\\\
                                &= \dfrac{75 \times \cancel{3}}{2 \times \cancel{3}} \\\\
                                &= \dfrac{75}{2} \\\\
                                &= 37 \ \text {remainder} \ 1 \\\\
                                &\approx 38
\end{aligned}
$

<div class='question question'>

You are told that $56\%$ of the pupils in a class are girls. 

What is the smallest number of pupils the class could contain?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

The smallest number of pupils the class could contain can be found by writing the fraction in its simplest form.

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Percentage of girls}          &= 56 \ \% \\\\
\text{Percentage of boys}           &= 100 - 56 \\\\
                                    &= 44 \ \% \\\\
\text{Ratio}                        &= \dfrac {44} {56} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {4 \times 11} {4 \times 14} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times 11} {\cancel 4 \times 14} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {11} {14} \\\\
\text{Smallest number of pupils}    &= 11 + 14 \\\\
                                    &= 25
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>

<div class='answer'>

Sam and Alice have baked a cake.

Sam eats $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of the cake.

Alice eats $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of the cake.

What fraction of the cake have they eaten altogether?

                                          &=  \dfrac{5}{20} + \dfrac{8}{20} \\\\
                                          &=  \dfrac{5 + 8} {20} \\\\

\end{aligned}
