Complete the table below to show the percentages of each type of woodpecker.

|    Type A        |    Type B        |    Type C        |
|:------------:    |:------------:    |:------------:    |
| ...........%     | ...........%     | ...........%     |

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of type A woodpecker}      &= 6 \times 10000 \\\\
                                        &= 60000 \\\\
\text{Number of type B woodpecker}      &= 10000 \\\\
\text{Number of type C woodpecker}      &= 3 \times 10000 \\\\
                                        &= 30000 \\\\
\text{Total number of woodpeckers}      &= 60000 + 30000 + 10000 \\\\
                                        &= 100000
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Percentage of type A woodpecker}      &= \dfrac {60000} {100000} \times 100 \\\\
                                            &= \dfrac {60000 \times 100} {100000} \\\\
                                            &= \dfrac {60 \cancel {000} \times 1 \cancel {00}} {1 \cancel {000} \cancel {00}} \\\\
                                            &= 60 \% \\\\
\text{Percentage of type B woodpecker}      &= \dfrac {10000} {100000} \times 100 \\\\
                                            &= \dfrac {10000 \times 100} {100000} \\\\
                                            &= \dfrac {10 \cancel {000} \times 1 \cancel {00}} {1 \cancel {000} \cancel {00}} \\\\
                                            &= 10 \% \\\\
\text{Percentage of type C woodpecker}      &= \dfrac {30000} {100000} \times 100 \\\\
                                            &= \dfrac {30000 \times 100} {100000} \\\\
                                            &= \dfrac {30 \cancel {000} \times 1 \cancel {00}} {1 \cancel {000} \cancel {00}} \\\\
                                            &= 30 \%
\end{aligned}
$

<div class='answer'>

<div class='question question'>

$x$ and $y$ are positive whole numbers. The ratio of $x$ to $y$ is $7:3$. The difference between $x$ and $y$ is $24$.

Find the value of $x$ and the value of $y$.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
x - y                                                       &= 24 \\\\
x                                                           &= 24 + y
\end{aligned}
$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
7 \ \text{x} : 3 \ \text{y}                     &= (24 + y) \ \text{x} : y \ \text{y} \\\\
\dfrac{7}{3}                                    &= \dfrac {24 + y} {y} \\\\
7 \times y                                      &= (24 + y) \times 3 \\\\
7y                                              &= 24 \times 3 + 3y \\\\
7y                                              &= 72+ 3y \\\\
7y - 3y                                         &= 72 \\\\
4y                                              &= 72 \\\\
y                                               &= \dfrac {72} {4} \\\\
y                                               &= \dfrac {4 \times 18} {4} \\\\
y                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times 18} {\cancel 4} \\\\
y                                               &= 18
\end{aligned}
$

Let's use the value of y to find x.

$
\begin{aligned}
x           &= 24 + y \\\\
            &= 24 + 18 \\\\
            &= 42
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$x= 42,y= 18$

</div>

<div class='question question'>

In a group of $48$ children, the ratio of boys to girls is $3:5$.

How many boys must join the group to make the ratio of boys to girls $5 : 3$? 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number of boys before adding new boys $= b$

Number of girls before adding new boys $= 48 - b$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
b \ \text{boys} : (48 - b) \ \text{girls}       &= 3 \ \text{boys} : 5 \ \text{girls} \\\\
\dfrac {b} {48 - b}                             &= \dfrac {3} {5} \\\\
b \times 5                                      &= 3 \times (48 - b) \\\\
5b                                              &= 3 \times 48 - 3b \\\\
5b                                              &= 144 - 3b \\\\
5b + 3b                                         &= 144 \\\\
8b                                              &= 144 \\\\
b                                               &= \dfrac {144} {8} \\\\
b                                               &= \dfrac {8 \times 18} {8} \\\\
b                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 8 \times 18} {\cancel 8} \\\\
b                                               &= 18
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of boys}       &= 18 \\\\
\text{Number of girls}      &= 48 - 18 \\\\
                            &= 30
\end{aligned}
$

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{girls}        &:& \text{boys} \\\\
= & 3                   &:& 5 \\\\
= & 1                   &:& \dfrac{5} {3} \\\\
= & 30                  &:& \dfrac{5 \times 30} {3} \\\\
= & 30                  &:& \dfrac{5 \times 3 \times 10} {3} \\\\
= & 30                  &:& \dfrac{5 \times \cancel 3 \times 10} {\cancel 3} \\\\
= & 30                  &:& 5 \times 10 \\\\
= & 30                  &:& 50
\end{array}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of boys added}       &= 50 - 18 \\\\
                                  &= 32
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$32$

</div>
</div>

Alex has $9$ rangos and $4$ fangos. Barbara has $7$ rangos and $3$ fangos. How many fangos do they both have in total?

<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Total fangos} &= 9 \ \text{rangos} + 4 \ \text{fangos} + 7 \ \text{rangos} + 3 \ \text{fangos} \\\\
                     &= 9 \times 6 \ \text{fangos} + 4 \ \text{fangos} + 7 \times 6 \ \text{fangos} + 3 \ \text{fangos} \\\\
                     &= 9 \times 6 + 4 + 7 \times 6 + 3 \\\\
                     &= 54 + 4 + 42 + 3 \\\\
                     &= 103 \ \text{fangos}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>

<div class='answer'>

$103  \ \text{fangos}$
