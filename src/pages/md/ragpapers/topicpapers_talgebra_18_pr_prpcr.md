$\frac{1}{2}x+\frac{1}{3}y=\frac{1}{4}$

<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{1}{2}x+\dfrac{1}{3}y    &= \dfrac{1}{4} \\\\
2x + 3y                        &= 4
\end{aligned}
$

We have got $2$ variables and we have $2$ equations to solve them. Let's eliminate to reduce it to just one variable. 

Multiply the first equation by $4$.

$
\begin{aligned} 
4 \times \dfrac{1}{2}x + 4 \times \dfrac{1}{3}y                             &= 4 \times \dfrac{1}{4} \\\\
\dfrac{2 \times 2 }{2}x + 4 \times \dfrac{1}{3}y                            &= \cancel 4 \times \dfrac{1}{\cancel 4} \\\\
\dfrac{\cancel 2 \times 2}{\cancel 2}x + 4 \times \dfrac{1}{3}y             &= 1 \\\\
2x + \dfrac{4}{3}y                                                          &= 1 \\\\
\end{aligned}
$

Subtract the above equation from the second equation to reduce it to one variable.

$
\begin{aligned} 
2x + 3y - (2x + \dfrac{4y}{3} )                                  &= 4 - 1 \\\\
2x + 3y - 2x - \dfrac{4y}{3}                                     &= 3 \\\\
3y - \dfrac{4y}{3}                                               &= 3 \\\\ 
\dfrac{3y \times 3}{1 \times 3} - \dfrac{4y}{3}                  &= 3 \\\\
\dfrac{9y - 4y}{3}                                               &= 3 \\\\
\dfrac{5y}{3}                                                     &= 3 \\\\
y                                                                &= \dfrac{3 \times 3}{5} \\\\
y                                                                &= \dfrac{9}{5} \\\\
y                                                                &= 1.8
\end{aligned}
$

Substuting the value of y in second equation

$
\begin{aligned} 
2x + 3y            &= 4 \\\\
2x + 3 \times 1.8  &= 4 \\\\
2x                 &= 4 - 5.4 \\\\
2x                 &= -1.4 \\\\
x                  &= \dfrac{1.4}{2} \\\\  
x                  &= \dfrac{1.4 \times 10 }{2 \times 10} \\\\
x                  &= -\dfrac{14 }{2 \times 10} \\\\  
x                  &= -\dfrac{2 \times 7 }{2 \times 10} \\\\ 
x                  &= -\dfrac{\cancel 2 \times 7 }{\cancel 2 \times 10} \\\\
x                  &= -\dfrac{7}{10} \\\\
x                  &= -0.7                                                   
\end{aligned}
