\begin{aligned}

&= \dfrac {1010 \times \cancel{9}}{\cancel{9}} \\\\

\end{aligned}

<div class='question subquestion'>

The sum of two consecutive whole numbers is $91$. 

What is the larger of these two numbers?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the first number $= a$

Second number $= a + 1$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
a + a + 1               &= 91 \\\\
2 a + 1                 &= 91 \\\\
2 a                     &= 91 - 1 \\\\
2 a                     &= 90 \\\\
a                       &= \dfrac {90} {2} \\\\
a                       &= \dfrac {45 \times 2} {2} \\\\
a                       &= \dfrac {45 \times \cancel {2}} {\cancel {2}} \\\\
a                       &= 45 \\\\
\text {Larger number}   &= a + 1 \\\\
                        &= 45 + 1 \\\\
                        &= 46
\end{aligned}
$ 

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$46$

</div>
</div>

<div class='question subquestion'>

I thought of a number, doubled it and then subtracted $14$. 

I then divided the result by $3$ and got a final answer of $8$. 

What was the original number?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
(2 \times a - 14) \div 3 &= 8 \\\\
2a - 14          &= 8 \times 3 \\\\
2a - 14          &= 24 \\\\
2a               &= 24 + 14 \\\\
2a               &= 38 \\\\
a                &= \dfrac {38} {2} \\\\
a                &= \dfrac {19 \times 2} {2} \\\\
a                &= \dfrac {19 \times \cancel {2}} {\cancel {2}} \\\\
a                &= 19
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$19$
