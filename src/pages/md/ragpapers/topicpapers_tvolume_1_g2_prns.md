<div class='question question'>

A cube measuring $3 \ \text{cm} \times 3 \ \text{cm} \times 3 \ \text{cm}$ is made up of small cubes and has a
$1 \ \text{cm} \times 1 \ \text{cm}$ square hole pushed right through the centre of each face so that you can see straight through the cube from every side.

![alderley-edge-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--2019--sample-paper-1/section-2-question-22-00.png](/assets/alderley-edge-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--2019--sample-paper-1/section-2-question-22-00.png "alderley-edge-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--2019--sample-paper-1/section-2-question-22-00.png")

How many small cubes are used to make this shape?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Volume of a cube} = (\text{Side}) ^ { 3 }$


$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Smaller cubes without removing}   &= \dfrac {\text{Volume of the big cube}} {\text{Volume of 1 small cube}} \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {3 \times 3 \times 3} {1 \times 1 \times 1} \\\\
                                        &= 27 \\\\
\text{Cubes to remove}                  &= \text{Cube on each face} + \text{Cube at the center} \\\\
                                        &= 6 + 1 \\\\
                                        &= 7 \\\\
\text{Cubes needed}                     &= 27 - 7 \\\\
                                        &= 20
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$20$

</div>
</div>

<div class='question question'>

Kaylee has some $1 \ \text{cm}$ cubes.

She makes a solid cube with side length $6 \ \text{cm}$ out of the cubes.

![alderley-edge-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-2/section-3-question-9-00.png](/assets/alderley-edge-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-2/section-3-question-9-00.png "alderley-edge-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-2/section-3-question-9-00.png")

Then she uses all these cubes to make some cubes with side length $2 \ \text{cm}$.

How many of these $2 \ \text{cm}$ cubes can Kaylee make?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Volume of a cube} = (\text{Side}) ^ { 3 }$


$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of } 2 \text{ cm cubes}  &= \dfrac {\text{Volume of the } 6 \text{ cm cube}} {\text{Volume of the } 2 \text{ cm cube}} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {6 \times 6 \times 6} {2 \times 2 \times 2} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {2 \times 3 \times 2 \times 3 \times 2 \times 3} {2 \times 2 \times 2} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {\cancel{2} \times 3 \times \cancel{2} \times 3 \times \cancel{2} \times 3} {\cancel{2} \times \cancel{2} \times \cancel{2}} \\\\
                                      &= 3 \times 3 \times 3 \\\\
                                      &= 27
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$27$

</div>
</div>

measures $2 \ \text{centimetres}$ by $3 \ \text{centimetres}$ by $4 \ \text{centimetres}$. 
What is the greatest number of these bricks which can be packed into a

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of bricks} &= \dfrac { \text{Volume of box} } { \text{Volume of a brick} } \\\\
                        &= \dfrac { 16 \times 15 \times 10  } { 2 \times 3 \times 4 } \\\\
                        &= \dfrac { 4 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 10  } { 2 \times 3 \times 4 } \\\\
                        &= \dfrac { \cancel{4} \times \cancel{2} \times 2 \times \cancel{3} \times 5 \times 10  } { \cancel{2} \times \cancel{3} \times \cancel{4} } \\\\
                        &= 2 \times 5 \times 10   \\\\
                        &= 100
\end{aligned}
$
