What is the side length of the square?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Perimeter of square} = 4 \times \text{side}$


ABBR: $\text{Area of square} = \text{side} \times \text{side}$


Let the side length of the square $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
a \times a                  &= 4 \times a \\\\
a \times a                  &= 4a \\\\
a                           &= \dfrac {4 a} {a} \\\\
a                           &= \dfrac {4 \cancel{a}} {\cancel{a}} \\\\
a                           &= 4
\end{aligned}
$

Side length of the square $= 4$

<div class='question subquestion'>

Now I want to draw a rectangle that is twice as long as it is wide. However, its perimeter is still equal in size to its area. What will the length of the rectangle be?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Area of rectangle} = \text{length} \times \text{width}$


ABBR: $\text{Perimeter of rectangle} = 2 \times (\text{length} + \text{width})$


Let the width of the rectangle $= a$

Length of the rectangle $= 2a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
2a \times a                         &= 2 \times (2a + a) \\\\
2a \times a                         &= 2 \times 3a \\\\
a                                   &= \dfrac{2 \times 3 \times a} {2 \times a} \\\\
a                                   &= \dfrac{\cancel{2} \times 3 \times \cancel{a}} {\cancel{2} \times \cancel{a}} \\\\
a                                   &= 3
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Length of rectangle} &= 2 \times a \\\\
                            &= 2 \times 3 \\\\
                            &= 6 \ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$6 \ \text{cm}$

</div>
</div>

<div class='question question'>

The   length   of a  rectangle   is twice its   width.   Its   area  is **$98 \ \text{cm}^2$** .  Find  its   length   and   width.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Area of rectangle} = \text{length} \times \text{width}$


Let the width of the  rectangle (in cm) $= w$

Length $= 2 w$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}                            
2\times w \times w  &= 98 \\\\\
2 \times w^2        &= 98 \\\\
w^2                 &= \dfrac {98} {2} \\\\
w^2                 &= \dfrac {49 \times 2} {2} \\\\
w^2                 &= \dfrac {49 \times \cancel {2} } {\cancel {2} } \\\\
w^2                 &= 49 \\\\
w                   &= \sqrt {49} \\\\
w                   &= 7
\end{aligned}
$

Width $= 7 \ \text {cm}$

$
\begin{aligned}                            
\text {Length}    &= 2 \times w \\\\
                  &= 2 \times 7 \\\\
                  &= 14 \ \text {cm}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$14 \ \text {cm}, 7 \ \text {cm}$

</div>
