<div class='question subquestion'>

Ann and Ben share $28$ sweets in the ratio 4 : 3

(Ann has $4$ sweets for every $3$ sweets that Ben has). 

How many sweets do they each receive?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Sweets received by Ann}                   &= \dfrac {4} {4 + 3} \times 28 \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {4} {7} \times 28 \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {4 \times 28} {7} \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {4 \times 7 \times 4} {7} \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {4 \times \cancel 7 \times 4} {\cancel 7} \\\\
                                                &= 4 \times 4 \\\\
                                                &= 16 \\\\
\text{Sweets received by Ben}                   &= 28 - 16 \\\\
                                                &= 12
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Let the sweets received by Ann $= a$

Sweets received by Ben $= 28 - a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
4 : 3                                           &= a : (28 - a) \\\\
\dfrac{4} {3}                                   &= \dfrac {a} {28 - a} \\\\
4 \times (28 - a)                               &= a \times 3 \\\\
112 - 4a                                        &= 3a \\\\
112                                             &= 3a + 4a \\\\
3a + 4a                                         &= 112 \\\\
7a                                              &= 112 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {112} {7} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {7 \times 16} {7} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 7 \times 16} {\cancel 7} \\\\
a                                               &= 16 \\\\
\text{Sweets received by Ann}                   &= 16 \\\\
\text{Sweets received by Ben}                   &= 28 - 16 \\\\
                                                &= 12
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Using whole to part ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{total}            &:& \text{Ann}                  &:& \text{Ben} \\\\
= & 4 + 3                   &:& 4                           &:& 3 \\\\
= & 7                       &:& 4                           &:& 3 \\\\
= & 7 \times 4              &:& 4 \times 4                  &:& 3 \times 4 \\\\
= & 28                      &:& 16                          &:& 12
\end{array}
$

Sweets received by Ann $= 16$

Sweets received by Ben $= 12$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$16, 12$

</div>

A necklace is made from black and white beads in a ratio of 3 : 2, in other words for every $3$ black beads there were $2$ white beads.

● ● ● ○ ○ ● ● ● ○ ○

If there are $80$ beads altogether, how many beads are black?

</div>

<div class='working'>

Let number of black beads $= a$

Number of white beads $= 80 - a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
3 \ \text{black} : 2 \ \text{white}   &= a \ \text{black} : (80 - a) \ \text{white} \\\\
\dfrac{3} {2}                         &= \dfrac {a} {80 - a} \\\\
3 \times (80 - a)                     &= a \times 2 \\\\
240 - 3a                              &= 2a \\\\
240                                   &= 2a + 3a \\\\
2a + 3a                               &= 240 \\\\
5a                                    &= 240 \\\\
a                                     &= \dfrac {240} {5} \\\\
a                                     &= \dfrac {5 \times 48} {5} \\\\
a                                     &= \dfrac {\cancel 5 \times 48} {\cancel 5} \\\\
a                                     &= 48
\end{aligned}
$

Number of black beads $= 48$

</div>

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{necklace}                             &:& \text{black beads} \\\\
= & 2 + 3                                       &:& 3 \\\\
= & 5                                           &:& 3 \\\\
= & 5 \times 16                                 &:& 3 \times 16 \\\\
= & 80                                          &:& 48
\end{array}
$

Number of black beads $= 48$

If the mass of the alloy is $72 \ \text{kg}$, how much iron and tungsten does it contain?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the mass of iron $= a$

Mass of tungsten $= 72 - a$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
5 \ \text{kg} : 1 \ \text{kg}                   &= a \ \text{kg} : (72 - a) \ \text{kg} \\\\
\dfrac{5}{1}                                    &= \dfrac {a} {72 - a} \\\\
5 \times (72 - a)                               &= a \\\\
5 \times 72 - 5a                                &= a \\\\
360 - 5a                                        &= a \\\\
360                                             &= 5a + a \\\\
5a + a                                          &= 360 \\\\
6a                                              &= 360 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {360} {6} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {6 \times 60} {6} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 6 \times 60} {\cancel 6} \\\\
a                                               &= 60 \ \text{kg}
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Mass of iron}         &= 60 \ \text{kg} \\\\
\text{Mass of tungsten}     &= 72 - 60 \\\\
                            &= 12 \ \text{kg}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \text{alloy}                  &:& \text{iron}                &:& \text{tungsten} \\\\

= & 6                             &:& 5                          &:& 1 \\\\
= & 6 \times 12                   &:& 5 \times 12                &:& 1 \times 12 \\\\
= & 72 \ \text{kg}                &:& 60 \ \text{kg}             &:& 12 \ \text{kg}

<div class='question question'>

In a class the ratio of boys to girls is 3 : 5.

If there are $6$ less boys than girls, how any pupils are in the class in total? 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number of girls $= g$

Number of boys $= g - 6$

Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
(g - 6) \ \text{boys} : g \ \text{girls}        &= 3 \ \text{boys} : 5 \ \text{girls} \\\\
\dfrac {g - 6} {g}                              &= \dfrac {3} {5} \\\\
(g - 6) \times 5                                &= 3 \times g \\\\
5g - 30                                         &= 3g \\\\
5g - 3g                                         &= 30 \\\\
2g                                              &= 30 \\\\
g                                               &= \dfrac {30} {2} \\\\
g                                               &= \dfrac {2 \times 15} {2} \\\\
g                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel 2 \times 15} {\cancel 2} \\\\
g                                               &= 15
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of girls}      &= 15 \\\\
\text{Number of boys}       &= 15 - 6 \\\\
                            &= 9 \\\\
\text{Total pupils}         &= 15 + 9 \\\\
                            &= 24
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$24$

</div>
