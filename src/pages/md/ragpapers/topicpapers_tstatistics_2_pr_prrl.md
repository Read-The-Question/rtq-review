Show that the **mean** of the three numbers is $\bold{\text{10}}$.

Explain why the **median** of the three numbers is $\bold{\text{10}}$.

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Mean} = \dfrac {\text{Total of items}} {\text{Number of items}}$


As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Mean}    &= \dfrac {9 + 11 + 10} {3} \\\\
                &= \dfrac {30} {3} \\\\
                &= \dfrac {3 \times 10} {3} \\\\
                &= \dfrac {\cancel {3} \times 10} {\cancel {3}} \\\\
                &= 10
\end {aligned}
$

ABBR: If there is an odd number of numbers, the median is the middle number. If there is an even count of numbers, the median is the average of the middle two numbers.


$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Numbers}  &= 9, 11, 10 \\\\
\text {Ordered Numbers}  &= 9, \maroonC
{10}, 11 \\\\
\end {aligned}
$

Median $= 10$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$10$

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

8, 9, 11, 12

Lets verify,

ABBR: $\text{Mean} = \dfrac {\text{Total of items}} {\text{Number of items}}$


$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Mean}     &= \dfrac {8 + 9 + 11 + 12} {4} \\\\
                &= \dfrac {40} {4} \\\\
                &= \dfrac {4 \times 10} {4} \\\\
                &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times 10} {\cancel 4} \\\\
                &= \green{10} \\\\
\end{aligned}
$

ABBR: If there is an odd number of numbers, the median is the middle number. If there is an even count of numbers, the median is the average of the middle two numbers.


$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Numbers}           &= \dfrac {8, 9, 11, 12} {4} \\\\
\text{Ordered Numbers}   &= \dfrac {8, 9, 11, 12} {4} \\\\
\text{Median}            &= \dfrac {9 + 11} {2} \\\\
                         &= \dfrac {20} {2} \\\\
                         &= \dfrac {2 \times 10} {2} \\\\
                         &= \dfrac {\cancel 2 \times 10} {\cancel 2} \\\\
                         &= \green{10} \\\\
\end{aligned}
$

<div class='question question'>

A set of five different positive integers has a mean (average) of $20$ and a median of $18$.

What is the greatest possible integer in the set? 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Mean} = \dfrac {\text{Total of items}} {\text{Number of items}}$


ABBR: If there is an odd number of numbers, the median is the middle number. If there is an even count of numbers, the median is the average of the middle two numbers.


Let the five different positive numbers $= a1, a2, a3, a4, a5$

Let's arrange the numbers in ascending order.

$
\begin {aligned}
\text{Ordered numbers}   &= a1, \quad a2, \quad \maroonC
{a3}, \quad a4, \quad a5 \\\\
\text{Median}            &= a3 \\\\
\end {aligned}
$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
a3                                              &= 18 \\\\
\dfrac {a1 + a2 + a3 + a4 + a5} {5}             &= 20 \\\\
a1 + a2 + a3 + a4 + a5                          &= 20 \times 5 \\\\
a1 + a2 + 18 + a4 + a5                          &= 100 \\\\
a1 + a2 + a4 + a5                               &= 100 - 18 \\\\
a1 + a2 + a4 + a5                               &= 82
\end{aligned}
$

Minimum positive value of $a1 = 1$

Minimum positive value of $a2 = 2$

Minimum positive value of $a4 = 19$

Using minimum possible values of $a1, a2$ and $a4$ to find the maximum possible value of $a5$.

$
\begin{aligned}
a1 + a2 + a4 + a5               &= 82 \\\\
1 + 2 + 19 + a5                 &= 82 \\\\
22 + a5                         &= 82 \\\\
a5                              &= 82 - 22 \\\\
a5                              &= 60
\end{aligned}
$

Greatest possible value in the set $= 60$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$60$

</div>
