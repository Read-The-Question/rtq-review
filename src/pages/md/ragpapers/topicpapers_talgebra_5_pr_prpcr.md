In the town of Mathsville there are $360$ residents.

$45 \%$ of the residents are children, the rest are adults.

How many adults are there in Mathsville?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of adults} &= 55 \% \ \text {of} \ 360 \\\\
                       &= \dfrac {55} {100} \times 360 \\\\
                       &= \dfrac {55 \times 360} {100} \\\\
                       &= \dfrac {11 \times 5 \times 20 \times 18} {5 \times 20} \\\\
                       &= \dfrac {11 \times \cancel {5} \times \cancel {20} \times 18} {\cancel {5} \times \cancel {20}} \\\\
                       &= 11 \times 18 \\\\
                       &= 198
\end{aligned}
$

</div>

The price of a bus ticket from Mathsville to Numbertown has **increased** by $50\%$ in the past year. 
A bus ticket now costs $\pounds 1.80$.

What did it cost a year ago?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the cost of bus ticket a year ago (in pounds) $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
a + \dfrac {50} {100} \times a &= 1.80 \\\\
a + \dfrac {50 \times a} {50 \times 2}  &= 1.80 \\\\
a + \dfrac {\cancel {50} \times a} {\cancel {50} \times 2}  &= 1.80 \\\\
a + \dfrac {a} {2}  &= 1.80 \\\\
\dfrac {a \times 2} {2} + \dfrac {a} {2}  &= 1.80 \\\\
\dfrac {2a + a} {2} &= 1.80 \\\\
\dfrac {3a} {2} &= 1.80 \\\\
3a &= 1.80 \times 2 \\\\
3a &=  3.60 \\\\
a &= \dfrac {3.60} {3} \\\\
a &= \dfrac { 3 \times 1.20} {3} \\\\
a &= \dfrac { \cancel 3 \times 1.20} { \cancel 3} \\\\
a &= \pounds 1.20
\end{aligned}
$

<div class='question subquestion'>

Mathsville Maths Supplies is a shop selling maths equipment.

Tina bought $4$ calculators and $3$ protractors for $\pounds 16.10$.
Ahmed bought $2$ calculators and $1$ protractors for $\pounds 7.50$.

How much does a calculator cost, and how much does a protractor cost?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the cost of a calculator (in pounds) $= a$

Let the cost of a protractor (in pounds) $= b$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
4a + 3b &= 16.10 \\\\
2a + b &= 7.50
\end{aligned}
$

Let's substitute b from the first equation to reduce it to just one variable.

$
\begin{aligned}
4a + 3(7.50 - 2a) &= 16.10 \\\\
4a + 3 \times 7.50 - 3 \times 2a &= 16.10 \\\\
4a + 22.5 - 6a &= 16.10 \\\\
22.5 - 2a &= 16.10 \\\\
2a &= 22.5 -  16.10 \\\\
2a &= 6.40 \\\\
a &= \dfrac {6.40} {2} \\\\
a &= \dfrac {3.20 \times 2} {2} \\\\
a &= \dfrac {3.20 \times \cancel 2} {\cancel 2} \\\\
a &= 3.20 \\\\
b &= 7.50 - 2a \\\\
b &= 7.50 - 2 \times 3.20 \\\\
b &= 7.50 - 6.40 \\\\
b &= 1.10
\end{aligned}
$

The cost of a calculator (in pounds) $= 3.20$

The cost of a protractor (in pounds) $= 1.10$

In a charity fun run, one sixth of the runners were adults, and the rest were children.
There were $120$ adults in the race. 

How many children were running the race?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the total number of runners $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{1} {6} \ \text{of} \ a &= 120 \\\\
\dfrac{a} {6}                 &= 120 \\\\
a                             &= 120 \times 6 \\\\
a                             &= 720
\end{aligned}
$

$
begin{aligned}
\text{Number of children running} &= \dfrac{5} {6} \ \text{of} \ 720 \\\\
                                  &= \dfrac{5 \times 720} {6} \\\\
                                  &= \dfrac{5 \times 6 \times 120} {6} \\\\
                                  &= \dfrac{5 \times \cancel 6 \times 120} {\cancel 6} \\\\
                                  &= 5 \times 120 \\\\
                                  &= 600
end{aligned}
$

</div>

A bag contains $600$ sweets. Sweets are either red, blue or green. There are $8$ more red sweets than blue sweets. 
$30\%$ of the sweets are blue. How many sweets are green?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let number of blue sweets $= a$

Number of red sweets $= a + 8$


As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
a &= 30 \ \% \ \text{of} \ 600 \\\\
a &= \dfrac {30} {100} \times 600 \\\\
a &= \dfrac {30 \times 600} {100}  \\\\
a &= \dfrac {30 \times 6 \times 100} {100}  \\\\
a &= \dfrac {30 \times 6 \times \cancel {100}} {\cancel{100}}  \\\\
a &= 30 \times 6  \\\\
a &= 180
\end {aligned}
$

$
\begin {aligned}
\text{Number of green sweets} &= 600 - a - a - 8 \\\\
                              &= 592 - 2a \\\\
                              &= 592 - 2 \times 180 \\\\
                              &= 592 - 360 \\\\
                              &=232
\end {aligned}
$

Let a truffle cost $= b$

$
\begin {aligned}

$
\begin {aligned}

An artist mixes red, yellow and white paint to create a new shade of orange.

Two fifths of the mixture is yellow paint.

The artist uses three times as much red paint as white paint.

In total, the artist mixes $180 \ \text{ml}$ orange paint.

How much red paint does the artist need?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let white paint used $= a$

Red paint used $= 3a $

As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
180 - 3a - a &= \dfrac{2} {5} \ \text{of} \ 180 \\\\
180 - 4a     &= \dfrac{2} {5} \times 180 \\\\
180 - 4a     &= \dfrac{2  \times 180 } {5} \\\\
180 - 4a     &= \dfrac{2  \times 36 \times 5} {5} \\\\
180 - 4a     &= \dfrac{2  \times 36 \times \cancel 5} {\cancel 5} \\\\
180 - 4a     &= 2  \times 36  \\\\
180 - 4a     &= 72  \\\\
4a           &= 180 - 72  \\\\
4a           &= 108  \\\\
a            &= \dfrac {108} {4}  \\\\
a            &= \dfrac {4 \times 27} {4}  \\\\
a            &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times 27} {\cancel 4}  \\\\
a            &= 27
\end {aligned}
$

Red paint used $= 3a = 3 \times 27 = 81 \ \text{ml} $

Jack likes to eat lots of fruit.

How much would he have to pay if he bought one apple, one orange and one banana?

Let cost of one apple (in pounds) $= a$

Let cost of one orange (in pounds) $= b$

Let cost of one banana (in pounds) $= c$

$
\begin{aligned}
4a + 2b       &= 1.54 \\\\
2b + 4c       &= 1.70
\end{aligned}
$

Let’s add all the equations.

$
\begin{aligned}
4a + 2b + 2b + 4c &= 1.54 + 1.70 \\\\
4a + 4b + 4c &= 3.24 \\\\
4 \times (a + b + c) &= 3.24 \\\\
a + b + c &= \dfrac {3.24} {4} \\\\
a + b + c &= \dfrac {4 \times 0.81} {4} \\\\
a + b + c &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times 0.81} {\cancel 4} \\\\
a + b + c &= 0.81 
\end{aligned}
$

The cost of one apple, one orange and one banana $= \pounds 0.81$

<div class='question question'>

Three different, positive odd numbers add together to make $23$. What is the smallest possible value of the largest of the three numbers? 

A: $7$ 

B: $9$ 

C: $11$ 

D: $13$ 

E: $1$

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the three odd numbers $= n, \ n + 2, \ n + 6$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
n + n + 2 + n + 6 &= 23 \\\\
3 n + 8           &= 23 \\\\
3 n               &= 23 - 8 \\\\
3 n               &= 15 \\\\
n                 &= \dfrac {15} {3} \\\\
n                 &= \dfrac {5 \times 3} {3} \\\\
n                 &= \dfrac {5 \times \cancel 3} {\cancel 3} \\\\
n                 &= 5
\end{aligned}
$

Smallest possible value of the largest of the three numbers $= n + 6 = 5 + 6 = 11$


</div>
</div>

<div class='answer'>

</div>
