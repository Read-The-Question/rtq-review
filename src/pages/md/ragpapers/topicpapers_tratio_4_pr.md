<div class='question question'>

A red light flashes four times a minute and a green light flashes six times a minute. If both lights start flashing at the same instant, how many times do they flash at the same instant in one hour including the first and the last time?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Interval at which red light flashes}         &= \dfrac {60} {4} \\\\
                                                   &= \dfrac {15 \times 4} {4} \\\\
                                                   &= \dfrac {15 \times \cancel 4} {\cancel 4} \\\\
                                                   &= 15 \ \text{s} \\\\
\text{Interval at which blue light flashes}        &= \dfrac {60} {6} \\\\
                                                   &= \dfrac {10 \times 6} {6} \\\\
                                                   &= \dfrac {10 \times \cancel 6} {\cancel 6} \\\\
                                                   &= 10 \ \text{s}
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Interval at which both will flash}        &= \text{LCM of 15 and 10} \\\\
                                                &= 30 \ \text{s}
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Number of times they flash together}     &= \dfrac {60 \times 60} {30} + 1 \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {30 \times 2 \times 60} {30} + 1 \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {\cancel {30} \times 2 \times 60} {\cancel {30}} + 1 \\\\
                                                &= 2 \times 60 + 1 \\\\
                                                &= 120 + 1 \\\\
                                                &= 121
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$121$

</div>
</div>

<div class='question subquestion'>

Oliver brought two fish and firstly swapped them for loaves of bread. Then he decided to change the loaves for apples.

How many apples did he get?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of apples Oliver get}        &= 2 \ \text{fish} \\\\
                                          &= 2 \times 2 \ \text{loaves} \\\\
                                          &= 4 \ \text{loaves} \\\\
                                          &= 4 \times 3 \ \text{apples} \\\\
                                          &= 12 \ \text{apples}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>

<div class='answer'>

<div class='question subquestion'>

How many loaves could Irfan have for three fish and twelve apples?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of loaves Irfan get}         &= 3 \ \text{fish} + 12 \ \text{apples} \\\\
                                          &= 3 \times 2 \ \text{loaves} + \dfrac {12} {3} \ \text{loaves} \\\\
                                          &= 6 + \dfrac {4 \times 3} {3} \\\\
                                          &= 6 + \dfrac {4 \times \cancel 3} {\cancel 3} \\\\
                                          &= 6 + 4 \\\\
                                          &= 10 \ \text{loaves}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>

$10 \ \text{loaves}$

<div class='question subquestion'>

Aidan wanted four fish to go with each loaf, how many loaves and fish could he exchange for $54$ apples?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number of loaves be $= a$

Number of fish $= 4a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
a \times 3 + 4a \times 2 \times 3         &= 54 \\\\
3a + 24a                                  &= 54 \\\\
27a                                       &= 54 \\\\
a                                         &= \dfrac {54} {27} \\\\
a                                         &= \dfrac {27 \times 2} {27} \\\\
a                                         &= \dfrac {\cancel {27} \times 2} {\cancel {27}} \\\\
a                                         &= 2
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of loaves}          &= 2 \\\\
\text{Number of fish}            &= 2 \times 4 \\\\
                                 &= 8
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>

$2 \ \text{loaves}, 8 \ \text{fish}$

<div class='question question'>

Complete this bill for a small shopping trip, filling in the **__five__** missing quantities and amounts in the spaces provided.

<!--

|                                                                | $\pounds \ldotp \text{p}$ |
|----------------------------------------------------------------|:-------------------------:|
| ............ biscuits at $45  \text{p}$ each                   |           $3.60$          |
| ............ eggs which cost $\pounds 1.60$ for twelve         |           $0.80$          |
| ............ grams of butter at $\pounds 2.50 \ \text{per Kg}$ |           $1.50$          |
| ............ litres of milk costing $90 \text{p}$ per litre    |          $\ldots$         |
| **TOTAL**                                                      |       $\pounds 8.15$      |

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Number of biscuits}        &= \dfrac {3.6 \times 100} {45} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {36 \times 10} {45} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {9 \times 4 \times 5 \times 2} {9 \times 5} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {\cancel 9 \times 4 \times \cancel 5 \times 2} {\cancel 9 \times \cancel 5} \\\\
                                 &= 4 \times 2 \\\\
                                 &= 8 \\\\
\text{Price of 1 egg}            &= \dfrac {1.6} {12} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {16} {12 \times 10} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {4 \times 2 \times 2} {4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 5} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times 2 \times \cancel 2} {\cancel 4 \times 3 \times \cancel 2 \times 5} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {2} {3 \times 5} \\\\
                                 &= \pounds \dfrac {2} {15} \\\\
\text{Number of eggs}            &= \dfrac {0.8} {\dfrac {2} {15}} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {0.8 \times 15} {2} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {8 \times 15} {2 \times 10} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 5 \times 3} {2 \times 2 \times 5} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {2 \times 2 \times \cancel 2 \times \cancel 5 \times 3} {\cancel 2 \times \cancel 2 \times \cancel 5} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {2 \times \cancel 2 \times \cancel 2 \times \cancel 5 \times 3} {\cancel 2 \times \cancel 2 \times \cancel 5} \\\\
                                 &= 2 \times 3 \\\\
                                 &= 6 \\\\
\text{Amount of butter}          &= \dfrac {1.5} {2.5} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {15 \times 10} {25 \times 10} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {5 \times 3 \times 5 \times 2} {5 \times 5 \times 10} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {\cancel 5 \times 3 \times \cancel 5 \times 2} {\cancel 5 \times \cancel 5 \times 10} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {3 \times 2} {10} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {6} {10} \\\\
                                 &= 0.6 \ \text{kg} \\\\
\text{Money left}                &= 8.15 - 3.6 - 0.8 - 1.5 \\\\
                                 &= \pounds 2.25 \\\\
\text{Volume of milk}            &= \dfrac {2.25 \times 100} {90} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {225 \times 100} {90 \times 100} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {9 \times 25 \times 100} {9 \times 10 \times 100} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {\cancel 9 \times 25 \times \cancel {100}} {\cancel 9 \times 10 \times \cancel {100}} \\\\
                                 &= \dfrac {25} {10} \\\\
                                 &= 2.5 \ \text{litres}
\end{aligned}
$

<div class='answer'>

$8 \ \text{biscuits}, 6 \ \text{eggs}, 0.6 \ \text{kg butter}, \pounds 2.25, 2.5 \ \text{litres of milk}$
