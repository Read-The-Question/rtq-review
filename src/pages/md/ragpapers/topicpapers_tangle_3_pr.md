<div class='question question'>

Work out the value of the angle labelled $x$ in the diagram below. 

The diagram is not drawn to scale.
![missing image](/papers/missing_image.svg)


</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Sum of angles in a triangle} = 180 \degree$


ABBR: $\text{Vertically opposite angles are equal}$.


Let the third angle of triangle $= a$

As per the diagram,

$
\begin{aligned}
a  + 40 + 60                            &= 180 \\\\
a + 100                                 &= 180 \\\\
a                                       &= 180 - 100 \\\\
a                                       &= 80 \degree
\end{aligned}
$

$x$ is vertically opposite to $a$.

Using this we get,

$
\begin{aligned}
x &= a \\\\
x &= 80 \degree
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$80 \degree$

</div>
</div>

<div class='question question'>

The shape is made from a square and an equilateral triangle.

%image as%

Diagram not drawn to scale.

What is the size of the reflex angle marked $p \degree$?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Each angle of an equilateral triangle} = 60 \degree$


$\text{Each angle of a square} = 90 \degree$

ABBR: $\text{Angles around a point add up to} = 360 \degree$


As per the diagram,

$
\begin{aligned}
p + 60 + 90  &= 360 \\\\
p + 150      &= 360 \\\\
p            &= 360 - 150 \\\\
p            &= 210 \degree
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$210 \degree$

</div>
</div>

ABBR: $\text{Sum of angles in a triangle} = 180 \degree$


ABBR: $\text{Angles on a straight line add up to} = 180 \degree$.


Let the second angle in the triangle $= a$

Let the third angle in the triangle $= b$

As per the diagram,

$
\begin{aligned}
a + 110         &= 180 \\\\
a               &= 180 - 110 \\\\
a               &= 70 \degree
\end{aligned}
$

Let's use the value of $a$ to find $b$.

$
\begin{aligned}
a + b + 60              &= 180 \\\\
70 + b + 60             &= 180 - 130 \\\\
b                       &= 50 \degree
\end{aligned}
$

Let's use the value of $b$ to find $χ$.

$
\begin{aligned}
χ + 50              &= 180 \\\\
χ                   &= 180 - 50 \\\\
χ                   &= 130 \degree
\end{aligned}
