<div class='question question'>

A piece of wire $30 \ \text{cm}$ long is bent into the shape of a rectangle. 
If the rectangle is twice as long as it is wide, what is its area?  

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Perimeter of rectangle} = 2 \times (\text{length} + \text{width})$


ABBR: $\text{Area of rectangle} = \text{length} \times \text{width}$


Let the width of the rectangle $= a$

Length of the rectangle $= 2a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
2 \times (2a + a)                         &= 30 \\\\
2 \times 3a                               &= 30 \\\\
6a                                        &= 30 \\\\
a                                         &= \dfrac {30} {6} \\\\
a                                         &= \dfrac {5 \times 6} {6} \\\\
a                                         &= \dfrac {5 \times \cancel {6}} {\cancel {6}} \\\\
a                                         &= 5 \ \text {cm}
\end{aligned}
$

Width of the rectangle $= 5 \ \text {cm}$

Length of the rectangle $= 10 \ \text {cm}$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Area of the rectangle}        &= 5 \times 10 \\\\
                                    &= 50 \ \text {cm} ^2
\end{aligned}
$

</div>

<div class='question question'>

A room measures $8 \ \text{m}$ by $20 \ \text{m}$.
Isabelle wants to tile the floor with square tiles.

What is the length of the largest tile she can use so that all of the floor will be covered?

-  $2 \ \text{m}$
-  $3 \ \text{m}$
-  $4 \ \text{m}$
-  $5 \ \text{m}$

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

As per the question, 

Length $= 8 \ \text{m}$

Width  $= 20 \ \text{m}$


To use complete tiles, without cutting, the size of the square tile must divide both the length and width of the room. 

Let's find the highest common factor of $8$ and $20$.

Factors of $8 = 1, 2, \bold 4, 8$

Factors of $20 = 1, 2, \bold 4, 5, 20$

The highest common factor of $8$ and $20=4$.

So, the length of the largest square Isabelle can use so that all the floor will be covered $= 4 \ \text{m}$

</div>
<div class='working'>

Alternative solution

As per the question,

LCM of 8 and 20 $= 40$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Area of room}                                   &= \text{Length} \times \text{Width} \\\\
                                                      &= 8 \ \text{m} \times 20 \ \text{m}\\\\
                                                      &= 160 \ \text{m}^2 \\\\
\text{Largest square tile that can be used}           &= \dfrac {160} {40} \\\\
                                                      &= 4 \ \text{m}
\end{aligned}
$

What is the area of new rectangle?

                    &= 4 \times (l \times w) \\\\
                    &= 4 \times 20 \ \text {cm} ^2 \\\\

\end{aligned}

<div class='question question'>

Tanya cuts out a piece of card into a rectangle. The rectangle is twice as long as it is wide. 
The area of the rectangle is $128 \ \text{cm}^2$.

What is the perimeter of rectangle?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Area of rectangle} = \text{length} \times \text{width}$


ABBR: $\text{Perimeter of rectangle} = 2 \times (\text{length} + \text{width})$


Let the width of the rectangle $= a$

Length $= 2a$

As per the question, 

$
\begin {aligned}
2a \times a                     &= 128 \\\\
2a ^ 2                          &= 128 \\\\
a ^ 2                           &= \dfrac {128} {2} \\\\
a ^ 2                           &= \dfrac {64 \times 2} {2} \\\\
a ^ 2                           &= \dfrac {64 \times \cancel {2}} {\cancel {2}} \\\\
a ^ 2                           &= 64 \\\\
a                               &= \sqrt {64} \\\\
a                               &= 8 \ \text{cm}
\end {aligned}
$

Width of the rectangle $= 8 \ \text {cm}$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Length of the rectangle}  &= 2 \times 8 \\\\
                                &= 16 \ \text {cm}
\end{aligned}
$

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Perimeter}               &= 2 \times (16 + 8) \\\\
                                &= 2 \times 24 \\\\
                                &= 48 \ \text {cm}
\end {aligned}

<div class='question question'>

I want to cut out a circle
radius $6 \ \text{cm}$ from a square
piece of card.
What is the area of the
smallest square I can use?

- $144 \ \text{cm}^2$
- $48 \ \text{cm}^2$
- $36 \ \text{cm}^2$
- $9 \ \text{cm}^2$ 
- impossible to say

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

ABBR: $\text{Area of square} = \text{side} \times \text{side}$


As per the question,

As the radius of circle is $6 \ \text {cm}$, the length of a side of the square must be at least $12 \ \text {cm}$.

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Area of a square}  &= 12 \times 12 \\\\
                          &= 144 \ \text {cm} ^ 2
\end {aligned}
$
