<div class='question question'>

Khris Krigle gives each of his helper’s one twelfth of a giant chocolate bar that he had. 
$One third of the bar was left. How many helpers did he have?

- $6$
- $8$
- $12$
- $15$
- $18$ 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number of helpers $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
1 - a \times \dfrac {1} {12}    &= \dfrac {1} {3} \\\\
1 - \dfrac {a} {12}             &= \dfrac {1} {3} \\\\
1 - \dfrac {1} {3}              &= \dfrac {a} {12} \\\\
\dfrac {a} {12}                 &= \dfrac {1 \times 3} {1 \times 3} - \dfrac {1} {3} \\\\
\dfrac {a} {12}                 &= \dfrac {3} {3} - \dfrac {1} {3} \\\\
\dfrac {a} {12}                 &= \dfrac {3 - 1} {3} \\\\
a                               &= \dfrac {2} {3} \\\\
a                               &= \dfrac {2 \times 12} {3} \\\\
a                               &= \dfrac {2 \times 3 \times 4} {3} \\\\
a                               &= \dfrac {2 \times \cancel {3} \times 4} {\cancel {3}} \\\\
a                               &= 2 \times 4 \\\\
a                               &= 8
\end{aligned}
$

Number of helpers $= 8$

</div>
</div>

<p>B</p>
</div>

<div class='question question'>

Two numbers $X$ and $Y$ (where $Y$ is bigger than $X$), have a Highest Common Factor of $X$. 
What is the Lowest Common Multiple of $X$ and $Y$?
- $Y$
- $X2$
- $Y2$
- $2Y$
- $XY$

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>


$
\begin{aligned}
\text{X must be in included LCM} \\\\
\text{LCM}          &= X \times \dfrac{Y}{X} \\\\
                &= \cancel{X} \times \dfrac{Y}{\cancel{X}} \\\\
                &= Y
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>

A person's Body Mass Index $(BMI$) is worked out by dividing their weight (in kilograms) by the square of their height (in metres). A person is then classified as "underweight", "normal", "overweight" or "obese" according to the following table:

$BMI = \dfrac{72}{1.82^2} = 21.7$ which is between $18.6$ and $24.9$ so Jill is "normal".
