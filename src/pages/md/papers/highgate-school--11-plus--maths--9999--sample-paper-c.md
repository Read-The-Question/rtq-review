Potatoes are only sold in sacks of $12$ potatoes. One of these sacks costs $56 \ \text{p}$.
Stanley has $\pounds 3.20$.

How many potatoes can Stanley buy, and how much money does he have left over?

\begin {aligned}
\text {Total sacks}                 &= \dfrac {\pounds 3.20} {56 \ \text{p}} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {320 \ \text{p}} {56 \ \text{p}} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {8 \times 40} {8 \times 7} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {\cancel {8} \times 40} {\cancel {8} \times 7} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {40} {7} \\\\
                                    &= 5 \ \text {remainder} \ 5 \\\\
                                    &\approx 5 \\\\
\text {Total potatoes}              &= 12 \times 5 \\\\
                                    &= 60 \\\\
\text {Money left}                  &= \pounds 3.20 - 5 \times 56 \ \text{p} \\\\
                                    &= 320 \ \text{p} - 280 \ \text{p} \\\\
                                    &= 40 \ \text{p}
\end {aligned}

<div class='question subquestion'>

$5$ giant mushrooms cost the same as $3$ cucumbers.
Marvin buys $12$ cucumbers for $\pounds 3.60$.

What is the cost of $8$ giant mushrooms?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let a cucumber cost (in $\pounds$) $= a$

Giant mushroom cost (in $\pounds$) $= \dfrac {3a} {5}$

As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
12a         &= 3.60 \\\\
a           &= \dfrac {3.60} {12} \\\\
a           &= \dfrac {12 \times 0.03} {12} \\\\
a           &= \dfrac {\cancel {12} \times 0.03} {\cancel {12}} \\\\
a           &= \pounds 0.03
\end {aligned}
$

A cucumber cost $= \pounds 0.03$

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Cost of} \ 8 \ \text {giant mushrooms}   &= 8 \times \dfrac {3a} {5} \\\\
                                                &= 8 \times \dfrac {3 \times 0.03} {5} \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {8 \times 3 \times 0.03} {5} \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {0.72} {5} \\\\
                                                &= \pounds 0.144
\end {aligned}
$

</div>

<div class='question subquestion'>

Asha sells vegetables at the market. On Monday she sold half of her vegetables. On Tuesday she sold a third of the vegetables she had 
left.

What fraction of her original stock does she have left to sell on Wednesday?


</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the original stock has vegetables $= a$
 
As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Vegetables Asha sold on Monday}          &= \dfrac {a} {2} \\\\
\text {Vegetables Asha sold on Tuesday}         &= \dfrac {1} {3} \ \text {of} \ (a - \dfrac {a} {2}) \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {1} {3} \times (\dfrac {a \times 2} {1 \times 2} - \dfrac {a} {2}) \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {1} {3} \times (\dfrac {2a} {2} - \dfrac {a} {2}) \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {1} {3} \times \dfrac {a} {2} \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {a} {3 \times 2} \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {a} {6} \\\\
\text {Vegetables left to sell on Wednesday}    &= a - \dfrac {a} {6} \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {a \times 6} {1 \times 6} - \dfrac {a} {6} \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {6a} {6} - \dfrac {a} {6} \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {6a - a} {6} \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {5a} {6} \\\\
\text {Fraction of vegetables left}             &= \dfrac {\text{Vegetables left}} {\text{Total vegetables}} \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {\dfrac {5a} {6}} {a} \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {5a} {6a} \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {5\cancel{a}} {6\cancel{a}} \\\\
                                                &= \dfrac {5} {6}
\end {aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$\dfrac {5} {6}$

</div>

Tori bought $5$ jelly worms and $7$ sherbet dib-dabs for $\pounds 3.37$.
Meera bought $5$ jelly worms and $2$ sherbet dib-dabs for $\pounds 1.57$.

How much does a jelly worm cost, and how much does a sherbet dib-dab cost?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the cost of jelly worm $= a$

Let the cost of sherbet dib-dab $= b$

As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
5a + 7b &= 3.37 \\\\
5a + 2b &= 1.57
\end {aligned}
$

We have got 2 variables and we have 2 equations to solve them .Let's use elimination to reduce it to just one variable. 

Subtract second equation from the first to reduce it to one variable.

$
\begin {aligned}
5a + 7b - 5a - 2b  &= 3.37 - 1.57 \\\\
5b                 &= 1.8 \\\\
b                  &= \dfrac{1.8}{5} \\\\
b                  &= 0.36
\end {aligned}
$

Let's substitute the value of $b$ in one of the equations.

$
\begin {aligned}
5a + 2b &= 1.57 \\\\
5a + 2 \times 0.36 &= 1.57 \\\\
5a + 0.72          &= 1.57 \\\\
5a                 &= 1.57 - 0.72 \\\\
5a                 &= 0.85 \\\\
a                  &= \dfrac {0.85} {5} \\\\
a                  &= \dfrac {5 \times 0.17} {5} \\\\
a                  &= \dfrac {\cancel 5 \times 0.17} {\cancel 5} \\\\
a                  &= 0.17
\end {aligned}
$

The cost of jelly worm $= \pounds 0.17$

The cost of sherbet dib-dab $= \pounds 0.36$

<div class='question subquestion'>

Mr Rossi is looking at his monthly accounts.

Two fifths of his money was made from selling ice cream.

The rest of his money came from selling sweets and from selling fizzy drinks.

He made twice as much money from selling fizzy drinks than from sweets.

He made $\pounds 330$ from selling fizzy drinks.

How much money does he make in total?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the total money he made $= a$

$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Money was made from selling ice cream}            &= \dfrac {2} {5} a \\\\
\text{ Money made from selling sweets and fizzy drinks} &= a - \dfrac {2} {5} a \\\\
                                                        &= \dfrac {a \times 5} {5}  - \dfrac {2a} {5} \\\\
                                                        &= \dfrac {5a} {5}  - \dfrac {2a} {5} \\\\
                                                        &= \dfrac {5a -2a} {5} \\\\
                                                        &= \dfrac {3a} {5}
\end{aligned}
$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac {2} {3} \times \dfrac {3a} {5} &= 330 \\\\
\dfrac {2 \times 3a} {3 \times 5}     &= 330 \\\\
a                                     &= \dfrac {330 \times 3 \times 5} {3 \times 2} \\\\
a                                     &= \dfrac {165 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5} {3 \times 2} \\\\
a                                     &= \dfrac {165 \times \cancel 2 \times \cancel 3 \times 5} {\cancel 3 \times \cancel 2} \\\\
a                                     &= \dfrac {165 \times \cancel 2 \times \cancel 3 \times 5} {\cancel 3 \times \cancel 2} \\\\
a                                     &= 165 \times 5 \\\\
a                                     &= \pounds 825
\end{aligned}
$

The total money he made $= \pounds 825$

</div>
