<div class='question question'>

What is the missing number in the following?  $36 \div \boxed{?} = 4$

- $32$
- $8$
- $9$
- $12$ 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the missing number $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
36 \div a           &= 4 \\\\
\dfrac {36} {a}     &= 4 \\\\
36                  &= 4 \times a \\\\
4 \times a          &= 36 \\\\
a                   &= \dfrac {36} {4} \\\\
a                   &= \dfrac {4 \times 9} {4} \\\\
a                   &= \dfrac {\cancel 4 \times 9} {\cancel 4} \\\\
a                   &= 9
\end{aligned}
$

The missing number $= 9$


</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

C

</div>
<div class='answer'>

$9$

- $72 \ \text{cm}$

Buses leave Halifax to Elland every $9 \ \text{minutes}$. Buses leave Halifax to Sowerby Bridge every $12 \ \text{minutes}$. 
A bus to Elland and a bus to Sowerby Bridge both leave Halifax at $9 \ \text{am}$. At what time will a bus to Elland and a bus to Sowerby Bridge next leave Halifax at the same time?

- $10.12 \ \text{am}$
- $09.27 \ \text{am}$
- $10.48 \ \text{am}$
- $09.36 \ \text{am}$ 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Time at which both buses leave together again} &= 09.00 \ \text{am} + \text {Lowest common multiple of the time intervals} \\\\
                                                      &= 09.00 \ \text{am} + \text {Lowest common multiple of} \ 9 \ \text{and} \ 12 \\\\
                                                      &= 09.00 \ \text{am} + 36 \ \text{minutes} \\\\
                                                      &= 09.36 \ \text{am}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='option'>
<p>D</p>
</div>

$09.36 \ \text{am}$

<div class='question question'>

I have a jar of sweets; after I have given each of the children in my class one sixteenth of sweets in the jar I have one quarter of the sweets left. How many children do I have in my class?

- $4$
- $9$
- $16$
- $12$

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number of sweets in the jar $= a$

Let the number of children $= b$

As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
b \times (\dfrac {1} {16} \ \text {of} \ a)        &= a - (\dfrac {1} {4} \ \text {of} \ a) \\\\
b \times \dfrac {a} {16}                           &= \dfrac {a \times 4} {1 \times 4} - \dfrac {a} {4} \\\\
b \times \dfrac {a} {16}                           &= \dfrac {4a - a} {4} \\\\
b \times \dfrac {a} {16}                           &= \dfrac {3a} {4} \\\\                                         
b                                                  &= \dfrac {16} {a} \times \dfrac {3a} {4} \\\\
b                                                  &= \dfrac {16 \times 3a} {a \times 4} \\\\
b                                                  &= \dfrac {4 \times 4 \times 3a} {a \times 4} \\\\
b                                                  &= \dfrac {\cancel {4} \times 4 \times 3 \times \cancel {a}} {\cancel {a} \times \cancel {4}} \\\\
b                                                  &= 12
\end {aligned}
$

Number of children $= 12$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='option'>
<p>D</p>
</div>
<div class='answer'>

$12$

</div>

The pie chart shows the proportions of different drinks chosen from a machine in one day.
There were $15$ cartons of pineapple juice taken. How many cartons of orange juice were taken? 

![halifax-grammar-school--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-1/section-1-question-5-00.png](/assets/halifax-grammar-school--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-1/section-1-question-5-00.png "halifax-grammar-school--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-1/section-1-question-5-00.png")

- $33$
- $35$
- $45$
- $120$

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let number of cartons of orange juice $= a$

As per the pie chart, 

$
\begin{aligned}
\% \ \text{of oranges}      &= 100 - 13 - 11 - 28 - 15 \\\\
                            &= 33
\end{aligned}
$


Using equivalent ratio,

$
\begin{aligned}
11 \ \% : 15 \ \text{cartons}                   &= 33 \ \% : a \ \text{cartons} \\\\
\dfrac {11} {15}                                &= \dfrac {33} {a} \\\\
11 \times a                                     &= 33 \times 15 \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {33 \times 15} {11} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {11 \times 3 \times 15} {11} \\\\
a                                               &= \dfrac {\cancel {11} \times 3 \times 15} {\cancel {11}} \\\\
a                                               &= 3 \times 15 \\\\
a                                               &= 45
\end{aligned}
$

Number of cartons of oranges $= 45$

</div>
<div class='working'>

As per the pie chart, 

$
\begin{aligned}
\% \ \text{of oranges}      &= 100 - 13 - 11 - 28 - 15 \\\\
                            &= 33
\end{aligned}
$

Using ratio,

$
\begin{array}{cccccccccccccc}
  & \%                         &:& \text{cartons} \\\\
= & 11                         &:& 15 \\\\
= & 1                          &:& \dfrac {15} {11} \\\\
= & 1 \times 33                &:& \dfrac {15 \times 33} {11} \\\\
= & 33                         &:& \dfrac {15 \times 3 \times 11} {11} \\\\
= & 33                         &:& \dfrac {15 \times 3 \times \cancel {11}} {\cancel {11}} \\\\
= & 33                         &:& 15 \times 3 \\\\
= & 33                         &:& 45
\end{array}
$

Number of cartons of oranges $= 45$
