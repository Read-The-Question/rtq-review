&= \dfrac {9 + 4} {12} \\\\

\end{aligned}

</div>

$
\begin{aligned}

&= \dfrac {4} {9} \times \dfrac {11} {5} \\\\
&= \dfrac {4 \times 11} {9 \times 5} \\\\
&= \dfrac {44} {45}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>

$\dfrac {44} {45}$

<div class='question question'>

Mr Shah had $15\dfrac {1} {2} \ \text{m}$ of wire. He cut off a piece of wire $2\dfrac {3} {4} \ \text{m}$ long. How much wire did he have left?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

As per the question,  

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Left over}                  &= \ \text {Total wire} - \text {piece cut off} \\\\
                                   &= 15 \dfrac {1} {2} \text{m} - 2 \dfrac {3} {4} \text{m} \\\\
                                   &= \dfrac {31} {2} - \dfrac {11} {4} \\\\
                                   &= \dfrac {31 \times 2} {2  \times 2} - \dfrac {11} {4} \\\\
                                   &= \dfrac {62} {4} - \dfrac {11} {4} \\\\
                                   &= \dfrac {62 - 11} {4} \\\\
                                   &= \dfrac {51} {4} \\\\
                                   &= 12 \dfrac {3} {4} \ \text{m}
\end{aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$12 \dfrac {3} {4} \ \text{m}$

</div>
</div>
