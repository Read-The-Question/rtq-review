$2$ packets of crisps and $2$ drinks cost $\pounds 1.50$. $2$ packets of crisps and $1$ drink costs $\pounds 1.10$. How much would $4$ packets of crisps and $3$ drinks cost? 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the price of a packet of crisp $= a$

Let the price of a drink $= b$

As per the question,

$
\begin {aligned}
2a + 2b &= 1.50 \\\\
2a + b  &= 1.10 \\\\
\end {aligned}
$

Adding the first equation with the second one, we get

$
\begin {aligned}
2a + 2b + 2a + b  &= 1.50 + 1.10 \\\\
4a + 3b           &= \pounds 2.60
\end {aligned}
$

Cost of $4$ packets of crisps and $3$ drinks $= \pounds 2.60$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$\pounds 2.60$

</div>

h                                   &= 4 \times 2 \\\\
h                                   &= 8 \ \text {cm}
\end {aligned}
$

Height of the triangle $= 8 \ \text {cm}$

Halid adds the same amount each time. The difference between the consecutive terms of the sequence is always same.

Let take the amount $= a$. Hence, the sequence would be:

$
\begin{matrix}
&   \footnotesize {\maroonC
{(+a)}}
&&  \footnotesize {\maroonC
{(+a)}}
&&  \footnotesize {\maroonC
{(+a)}}
&&  \footnotesize {\maroonC
{(+a)}} \\
2,  &&    \ldots ,  &&  \ldots ,  &&   \ldots ,  &&   18
\end{matrix}
$

$
\begin{aligned}
2 + 4a      &= 18 \\\\

a           &= \dfrac {4 \times 4} {4} \\\\

\end{aligned}

$
\begin{matrix}

{(+4)}}

If $p  \ \Phi \ p = 72$, what number must $p$ be? Show all your working.

p^2                           &= \dfrac{72}{2}  \\\\
p^2                           &= \dfrac{36 \times 2}{2}  \\\\
p^2                           &= \dfrac{36 \times \cancel{2}}{\cancel{2}}  \\\\
p^2                           &= 36 \\\\
p                             &= \sqrt{36} \\\\
p                             &= 6
\end{aligned}
$

<div class='question question'>

Julia thinks of a number. She multiplies it by $5$ and then subtracts $14$. The answer she gets is $46$. What number did she originally think of?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number Julia thought of $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
a \times 5 - 14     &= 46 \\\\
5a                  &= 46 + 14 \\\\
5a                  &= 60 \\\\
a                   &= \dfrac{60}{5} \\\\
a                   &= \dfrac {5 \times 12} {5} \\\\
a                   &= \dfrac {\cancel {5} \times 12} {\cancel {5}} \\\\
a                   &= 12
\end{aligned}
$

The number Julia thought of $= 12$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$12$

</div>

<div class='question question'>

A boy spent $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of his allowance and put $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of the remainder in the bank. He then had $\pounds 15$ left. 

How much money did he have to start with?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let total money (in $\pounds$) $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
a - \dfrac {3} {8} \times a - \dfrac {1} {2} \times (a - \dfrac {3} {8} \times a)                  &= \pounds 15 \\\\
a - \dfrac {3a} {8} - \dfrac {1} {2} \times (\dfrac {a \times 8} {1 \times 8} - \dfrac {3a} {8})   &= 15 \\\\
a - \dfrac {3a} {8} - \dfrac {1} {2} \times (\dfrac {8a} {8} - \dfrac {3a} {8})                    &= 15 \\\\
a - \dfrac {3a} {8} - \dfrac {1} {2} \times \dfrac {8a - 3a} {8}                                   &= 15 \\\\
a - \dfrac {3a} {8} - \dfrac {1} {2} \times \dfrac {5a} {8}                                        &= 15 \\\\
a - \dfrac {3a} {8} - \dfrac {5a} {16}                                                             &= 15 \\\\
\dfrac {a \times 16} {1 \times 16} - \dfrac {3a \times 2} {8 \times 2} - \dfrac {5a} {16}          &= 15 \\\\
\dfrac {16a} {16} - \dfrac {6a} {16} - \dfrac {5a} {16}                                            &= 15 \\\\
\dfrac {16a - 6a - 5a} {16}                                                                        &= 15 \\\\
\dfrac {5a} {16}                                                                                   &= 15 \\\\
5a                                                                                                 &= 15 \times 16 \\\\
a                                                                                                  &= \dfrac {15 \times 16} {5} \\\\
a                                                                                                  &= \dfrac {3 \times 5 \times 16} {5} \\\\
a                                                                                                  &= \dfrac {3 \times \cancel {5} \times 16} {\cancel {5}} \\\\
a                                                                                                  &= 3 \times 16 \\\\
a                                                                                                  &= \pounds 48 
\end{aligned}
$

Total money $= \pounds 48$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$\pounds 48$

</div>
</div>

<div class='question question'>

Vale Youth Club has $126$ members. There are $20$ more boys than 
girls. How many girls are members of the Youth Club? 

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let the number of boys $= a$

Let the number of girls $= b$

As per the question,

$a = b + 20$

Using this we get,

$
\begin{aligned}
a + b       &= 126 \\\\
b + 20 + b  &= 126 \\\\
2b          &= 126 - 20 \\\\
2b          &= 106 \\\\
b           &= \dfrac {106} {2} \\\\
b           &= \dfrac {2 \times 53} {2} \\\\
b           &= \dfrac {\cancel {2} \times 53} {\cancel {2}} \\\\
b           &= 53
\end{aligned} 
$

Number of girls $= 53$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>
<div class='answer'>

$53$

</div>
