**Question**

The number of students absent from Year $7$ was recorded as follows.

| Monday | Tuesday | Wednesday | Thursday | Friday |
|:------:|:-------:|:---------:|:--------:|:------:|
| $4$    | $6$     | $5$       | $3$      | $?$    |

If the **mean number** of students absent that week from Year $7$ was $4$, how many students were absent on Friday?

</div>
<div class="questioncodeblock" style="margin: 1rem 0; padding: 0.85rem 1rem; border: 1px solid #9ea7b3; border-radius: 6px;">
<div class="questioncodeblock_title">

| $4$    | $6$     | $5$       | $3$      | $?$    |
<emptyline>

</code></pre>

<div class="formula">
- ABBR: $\text{Mean} = \dfrac {\text{Total of items}} {\text{Number of items}}$
</div>
</div>
<div class="working" style="margin: 1rem 0; padding: 0.85rem 1rem; border: 1px solid #9ea7b3; border-radius: 6px;">

**Working**

Let the number of students absent on Friday $= a$

As per the question,

$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac {4 + 6 + 5 + 3 + a} {5}          &= 4 \\\\
\dfrac {18 + a} {5}                     &= 4 \\\\
18 + a                                  &= 4 \times 5 \\\\
18 + a                                  &= 20 \\\\
a                                       &= 20 - 18 \\\\
a                                       &= 2
\end{aligned}
$

Number of students absent on Friday $= 2$
</div>

**Question**

Three bicycles are priced at $\pounds 79, \pounds 80$ and $\pounds 84$. If the shopkeeper decided to sell them for the same total amount, but made the three prices equal, what would each cost?

</div>

</code></pre>

<div class="working" style="margin: 1rem 0; padding: 0.85rem 1rem; border: 1px solid #9ea7b3; border-radius: 6px;">

**Working**

$
\begin{aligned}
\text {Cost of each bicycle}          &= \text{Average of three bicycles} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {\text{Total cost}} {\text{Number of bicycles}} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {79 + 80 + 84 } {3} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {243} {3} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {81 \times 3} {3} \\\\
                                      &= \dfrac {81 \times \cancel 3} {\cancel 3} \\\\
                                      &= \pounds 81
\end{aligned}
$
</div>

$\pounds 81$
