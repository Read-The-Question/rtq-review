<div class='question question'>

A bag contains $3$ red and $6$ green marbles. If you pick out a marble at random, what is the chance that it will be red?

-  $\dfrac{1}{3}$
-  $\dfrac{1}{2}$
-  $\dfrac{1}{9}$
-  $\dfrac{2}{3}$

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Probability of red marble}   &= \dfrac {\text {Number of red marbles}} {\text {Total number of marbles}} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {3} {3 + 6} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {3} {9} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {3} {3 \times 3} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {\cancel {3}} {\cancel {3} \times 3} \\\\
                                    &= \dfrac {1} {3}
\end {aligned}
$

</div>
</div>
<div class='answers'>

<p>A</p>
</div>
<div class='answer'>

$\dfrac {1} {3}$

Which bag should Toby choose to have the **greatest** chance
of getting an apple?

</div>
<div class='workings'>
<div class='working'>

Let's calculate the probability for each bag of getting an apple,

For bag A:

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Probability of getting an apple} &= \dfrac {\text {Favourable outcomes}} {\text {Total possible outcomes}} \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {2} {2 + 4 + 3} \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {2} {9}
\end {aligned}
$

For bag B:

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Probability of getting an apple} &= \dfrac {\text {Favourable outcomes}} {\text {Total possible outcomes}} \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {4} {4 + 5 + 3} \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {4} {12} \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {4} {3 \times 4} \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {4} {3 \times \cancel {4}} \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {1} {3}
\end {aligned}
$

For bag C:

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Probability of getting an apple} &= \dfrac {\text {Favourable outcomes}} {\text {Total possible outcomes}} \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {3} {3 + 6 + 4} \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {3} {13}
\end {aligned}
$

For bag D:

$
\begin {aligned}
\text {Probability of getting an apple} &= \dfrac {\text {Favourable outcomes}} {\text {Total possible outcomes}} \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {5} {5 + 4 + 9} \\\\
                                        &= \dfrac {5} {18}
\end {aligned}
$

Now let's arrange the above calcuations in ascending order:

$\text {Bag A}, \quad \text {Bag B}, \quad \text {Bag C}, \quad \text {Bag D}$

$\dfrac {2} {9}, \quad \dfrac {1} {3}, \quad \dfrac {3} {13}, \quad \dfrac {5} {18}$

$\dfrac {2 \times 26} {9 \times 26}, \quad \dfrac {1 \times 78} {3 \times 78}, \quad \dfrac {3 \times 18} {13 \times 18}, \quad \dfrac {5 \times 13} {18 \times 13}$

$\dfrac {52} {234}, \quad \dfrac {78} {234}, \quad \dfrac {54} {234}, \quad \dfrac {65} {234}$

$\dfrac {52} {234}, \quad \dfrac {54} {234}, \quad \dfrac {65} {234}, \quad \dfrac {78} {234}$

Hence, Bag B has the highest probability of getting an apple.

</div>
