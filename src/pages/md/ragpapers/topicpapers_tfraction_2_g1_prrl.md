You must justify your answer clearly.

$
\begin{aligned}
&= \dfrac {2} {3}, \quad \dfrac {3} {4} \\\\
&= \dfrac {2 \times 4} {3 \times 4}, \quad \dfrac {3 \times 3} {4 \times 3} \\\\
&= \dfrac {8} {12}, \quad \dfrac {9} {12} \\\\
&= \dfrac {8} {12}  <  \dfrac {9} {12} \\\\
&= \dfrac {2} {3}  <  \dfrac {3} {4} 
\end{aligned}
$

As per the calculation above, three quarters is greater than two thirds.

In a magazine there are three adverts on the same page.

![leicester-high-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-4/section-1-question-12-00.png](/assets/leicester-high-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-4/section-1-question-12-00.png "leicester-high-school-for-girls--11-plus--maths--9999--sample-paper-4/section-1-question-12-00.png")

**In total**, what **fraction** of the page do three adverts use?

Show your working.

</div>
